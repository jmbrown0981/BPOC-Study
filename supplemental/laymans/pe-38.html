§38.02 FAILURE TO IDENTIFY ▾

§38.02 – FAILURE TO IDENTIFY

Refusing to give your name and basic info to a police officer — or lying about it — when lawfully stopped, detained, or arrested.

Track A – Refusing to identify (after lawful arrest)

  • Class C Misdemeanor — suspect refuses to give name, residence address, or date of birth after being lawfully arrested and asked.
  • Class B Misdemeanor — if the suspect was a fugitive from justice (had an active warrant) at the time of the refusal.

Track B – Giving false information

  • Class B Misdemeanor — suspect gives a false or fictitious name, address, or DOB to an officer who has: (1) lawfully arrested them, (2) lawfully detained them, or (3) has good cause to believe the person is a witness to a criminal offense.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — if the suspect was a fugitive from justice when they gave the false information.

Track C – Driver failing to provide license info

  • Class C Misdemeanor — motor vehicle operator lawfully detained for a traffic violation who refuses to give their name, driver's license number, address, or DOB.
  • Class B Misdemeanor — if the driver gave a false or fictitious name or ID during that stop.

Notable exception: If the conduct also violates Section 106.07, Alcoholic Beverage Code (minor misrepresenting age), the suspect may only be prosecuted under that provision.

§38.03 RESISTING ARREST, SEARCH, OR TRANSPORTATION ▾

§38.03 – RESISTING ARREST, SEARCH, OR TRANSPORTATION

Using physical force to prevent a peace officer from arresting, searching, or transporting you or someone else.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — base offense: the suspect intentionally prevents or obstructs a peace officer (or someone acting at the officer's direction) from effecting an arrest, search, or transportation by using force.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the suspect uses a deadly weapon to resist.

Key rule: It is not a defense that the arrest or search was unlawful. Even if the stop was unconstitutional, the suspect cannot legally use force to resist it.

§38.04 EVADING ARREST OR DETENTION ▾

§38.04 – EVADING ARREST OR DETENTION

Running or fleeing from a peace officer who is lawfully trying to arrest or detain you.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — base offense: intentionally fleeing from an officer who is trying to lawfully arrest or detain the person. Also applies to flight from a federal special investigator.
  • State Jail Felony — if:
    • The suspect has a prior conviction for evading arrest; or
    • The suspect uses a vehicle or watercraft during the flight and has no prior evading conviction.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if:
    • The suspect uses a vehicle or watercraft while fleeing and has a prior evading conviction; or
    • Another person suffers serious bodily injury as a direct result of the officer's attempt to apprehend the fleeing suspect; or
    • The suspect uses a tire deflation device against the pursuing officer.
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if another person suffers death as a direct result of the officer's pursuit, or a person suffers serious bodily injury from the suspect's use of a tire deflation device.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree (mandatory) — notwithstanding the above, if the offense was committed in the course of human smuggling (§20.05(a)(2)), the offense is at minimum a 3rd-degree felony.
§38.05 HINDERING APPREHENSION OR PROSECUTION ▾

§38.05 – HINDERING APPREHENSION OR PROSECUTION

Helping someone avoid arrest or prosecution — hiding them, getting them out, or tipping them off.

The suspect must act with intent to hinder the arrest, prosecution, conviction, or punishment of another person. The conduct includes:

  • Harboring or concealing the person;
  • Providing any means of avoiding arrest or escaping; or
  • Warning the person of impending discovery or apprehension.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — base offense (person being helped is not charged with a felony).
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the person being helped is under arrest for, charged with, or convicted of a felony (including sex offender registration violations), and the suspect knew this.

Defense: A warning is not an offense if it was given as part of an effort to bring the other person into compliance with the law.

§38.06 ESCAPE ▾

§38.06 – ESCAPE

Breaking out of lawful custody — whether from arrest, court order, jail, or juvenile detention.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — base offense: escaping from lawful custody when arrested, detained, charged, or convicted of any offense; or in custody by court order; or in a juvenile facility.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the suspect:
    • Is under arrest for, charged with, or convicted of a felony; or
    • Is confined in a secure correctional or law enforcement facility; or
    • Is lawfully detained in a secure juvenile facility.
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if the suspect causes bodily injury in effecting the escape.
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — if the suspect causes serious bodily injury or uses or threatens to use a deadly weapon to escape.

Note: Unlawfulness of the custody is not a defense (see §38.08).

§38.07 PERMITTING OR FACILITATING ESCAPE ▾

§38.07 – PERMITTING OR FACILITATING ESCAPE

A correctional employee or other person who knowingly allows or helps someone in custody escape.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — base offense: an official or employee of a correctional facility who knowingly permits or facilitates an escape; or anyone who helps a juvenile offender or someone involuntarily committed escape.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the person in custody:
    • Was under arrest for, charged with, or convicted of a felony; or
    • Was confined in a correctional facility after a felony conviction.
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if a deadly weapon was used or threatened during the escape, or the person in custody was in a secure correctional facility after a felony conviction.
§38.09 IMPLEMENTS FOR ESCAPE ▾

§38.09 – IMPLEMENTS FOR ESCAPE

Smuggling tools, weapons, or anything that could help a prisoner escape into a jail or prison.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — introducing into a correctional facility (or providing to an inmate) anything that may be useful for escape, with intent to facilitate escape.
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if the item introduced or provided is a deadly weapon.
§38.10 BAIL JUMPING AND FAILURE TO APPEAR ▾

§38.10 – BAIL JUMPING AND FAILURE TO APPEAR

Skipping court after being released on bail or bond.

  • Class C Misdemeanor — if the underlying charge the person was required to appear for is a fine-only offense.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — base offense for most cases: intentionally or knowingly fails to appear as required by the terms of release.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the charge for which the person was required to appear is a felony.

Defenses: (1) The appearance was related to community supervision, parole, or an intermittent sentence. (2) The suspect had a reasonable excuse for the failure to appear.

§38.11 PROHIBITED SUBSTANCES AND ITEMS IN CORRECTIONAL OR CIVIL COMMITMENT FACILITY ▾

§38.11 – PROHIBITED SUBSTANCES AND ITEMS IN CORRECTIONAL OR CIVIL COMMITMENT FACILITY

Bringing or providing drugs, weapons, phones, money, or contraband to jails, prisons, or civil commitment facilities.

Providing/Possessing with Intent to Provide (Subsection a)

  • Alcohol, controlled substances, or dangerous drugs (without valid prescription)
  • Deadly weapons
  • Cell phones or wireless devices
  • Money
  • Cigarettes/tobacco (in facilities where prohibited)

Taking substances into the facility (Subsections b/c)

  • Taking alcohol, controlled substances, or dangerous drugs into a correctional/civil commitment facility
  • Taking controlled substances or drugs onto facility-controlled property

Possessing while inside (Subsection d)

  • Possessing alcohol, drugs, or dangerous drugs while in a facility or on facility property
  • Possessing a deadly weapon while in a facility

Punishment levels

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — base offense for most violations.
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if a facility employee commits the drug/alcohol offense.
  • Felony of the 1st Degree (life or 15–99 years + up to $250,000 fine) — if a facility employee introduces drugs/alcohol and the ingestion causes the death of an inmate.

Inmate possessing a cell phone: Separate offense under Subsection (j) — also a felony of the 3rd degree.

Operating drones to deliver contraband: Covered under §38.115.

§38.111 IMPROPER CONTACT WITH VICTIM ▾

§38.111 – IMPROPER CONTACT WITH VICTIM

An inmate contacting the victim of their crime (or a victim's family) without prior written consent while locked up.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — base offense: an inmate confined for a sex offense or §42.072 (stalking) offense contacts the victim (or victim's family) by letter, phone, or any means without prior written consent of the victim and notification from the facility director.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the inmate was convicted of a felony at the time of the improper contact.

Defense: Indirect contact through an attorney representing the inmate solely for purposes of a criminal proceeding is allowed.

§38.112 TAMPERING WITH ELECTRONIC MONITORING DEVICE ▾

§38.112 – TAMPERING WITH ELECTRONIC MONITORING DEVICE

Removing or disabling your ankle monitor or GPS tracking device while required to wear one.

  • State Jail Felony — base offense: a person required to wear an electronic monitoring device (as a condition of community supervision, parole, mandatory supervision, bail, or under Article 42.035, CCP) knowingly removes, disables, causes, or cooperates with another to remove or disable it.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the person is in the super-intensive supervision program.

Exception: Removal by a health care provider due to medical necessity is not an offense.

§38.113 UNAUTHORIZED ABSENCE FROM COMMUNITY CORRECTIONS FACILITY, COUNTY CORRECTIONAL CENTER, OR ASSIGNMENT SITE ▾

§38.113 – UNAUTHORIZED ABSENCE FROM COMMUNITY CORRECTIONS FACILITY, COUNTY CORRECTIONAL CENTER, OR ASSIGNMENT SITE

Walking away from a halfway house, community corrections center, or community service site without authorization.

  • State Jail Felony — a person sentenced or required to be in a community corrections facility or county correctional center who fails to report or leaves without approval of the court, supervising officer, or facility director.
§38.114 CONTRABAND IN CORRECTIONAL FACILITY ▾

§38.114 – CONTRABAND IN CORRECTIONAL FACILITY

Bringing in, providing, or possessing any unauthorized item in a jail or prison — a catch-all for items not covered by §38.11.

Contraband here means any item not provided or authorized by the facility, or any authorized item altered for a different use. (Does NOT cover items specifically listed in §38.11, which has its own felony penalties.)

  • Class C Misdemeanor — base offense for any person.
  • Class B Misdemeanor — if committed by an employee or volunteer of the facility.
§38.115 OPERATION OF UNMANNED AIRCRAFT OVER CORRECTIONAL FACILITY OR DETENTION FACILITY ▾

§38.115 – OPERATION OF UNMANNED AIRCRAFT OVER CORRECTIONAL FACILITY OR DETENTION FACILITY

Flying a drone over a jail, prison, or immigration detention facility.

  • Class B Misdemeanor — base offense: intentionally or knowingly operating a drone over a correctional or detention facility within 400 feet AGL, allowing the drone to make contact with the facility or its occupants, or allowing it to come close enough to interfere with operations.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — if the suspect has a prior conviction under this section.
  • State Jail Felony — if the drone is used to deliver contraband to an inmate or to introduce contraband into the facility.

Exceptions: Government agencies, persons acting under government direction, the facility owner/operator's authorized personnel, and law enforcement are exempt.

§38.12 BARRATRY AND SOLICITATION OF PROFESSIONAL EMPLOYMENT ▾

§38.12 – BARRATRY AND SOLICITATION OF PROFESSIONAL EMPLOYMENT

Improperly soliciting legal or professional business — ambulance chasing, paying for clients, or otherwise hustling for cases in illegal ways.

Track A – General barratry (§a/b)

Covers filing unauthorized suits, in-person/phone/electronic solicitation of clients, paying money or things of value to get clients or to hire others to solicit, or financing/investing in the above:

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree

Track B – Written/electronic solicitation by licensed professionals (§d)

An attorney, chiropractor, physician, surgeon, or PI who sends a written communication soliciting someone within 31 days of an accident, or to a represented party, or using coercion, fraud, or false statements:

  • Class A Misdemeanor — first offense.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the suspect has a prior conviction under Track B.
§38.122 FALSELY HOLDING ONESELF OUT AS A LAWYER ▾

§38.122 – FALSELY HOLDING ONESELF OUT AS A LAWYER

Pretending to be a licensed attorney to get paid for legal work when you're not actually licensed.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — a person who, with intent to obtain an economic benefit, holds themselves out as a lawyer without being currently licensed and in good standing.
§38.123 UNAUTHORIZED PRACTICE OF LAW ▾

§38.123 – UNAUTHORIZED PRACTICE OF LAW

Doing lawyer work — like handling personal injury claims on a contingency fee basis — without being a licensed attorney.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — base offense: contracting to represent someone in personal injury or property damage matters, advising on rights or settlement values, taking contingency fee contracts, or granting exclusive rights to select legal counsel — all for economic benefit, without a license.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the suspect has a prior conviction for the same conduct.
§38.13 HINDERING PROCEEDINGS BY DISORDERLY CONDUCT ▾

§38.13 – HINDERING PROCEEDINGS BY DISORDERLY CONDUCT

Disrupting a legal proceeding through loud, violent, or tumultuous behavior.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — intentionally hindering an official proceeding through noise, violent, or tumultuous behavior; OR recklessly doing so and continuing after an explicit official request to stop.
§38.14 TAKING OR ATTEMPTING TO TAKE WEAPON FROM PEACE OFFICER, FEDERAL SPECIAL INVESTIGATOR, EMPLOYEE OR OFFICIAL OF CORRECTIONAL FACILITY, PAROLE OFFICER, CSCD OFFICER, OR COMMISSIONED SECURITY OFFICER ▾

§38.14 – TAKING OR ATTEMPTING TO TAKE WEAPON FROM PEACE OFFICER (OR SIMILAR OFFICIAL)

Grabbing or trying to grab a firearm, nightstick, stun gun, or chemical spray from a peace officer or correctional/security official.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — actually taking a firearm, nightstick, stun gun, or personal protection chemical spray from a peace officer, federal special investigator, corrections employee/official, parole officer, CSCD officer, or commissioned security officer — intentionally or knowingly and with force.
  • State Jail Felony — attempting to take such a weapon from those same officials.

Presumption: The suspect is presumed to have known the person was an officer if the officer was in uniform/badge or identified themselves.

Defense: It is a defense if the officer was using excessive force against the suspect or another person at the time.

§38.15 INTERFERENCE WITH PUBLIC DUTIES ▾

§38.15 – INTERFERENCE WITH PUBLIC DUTIES

With criminal negligence, getting in the way of a peace officer, EMT, firefighter, K-9, or utility worker doing their job.

Covered persons include: peace officers, emergency medical personnel, firefighters, police service animals (and their handlers), citizens band radio emergency communications, animal control officers, public health/safety inspectors, and utility workers.

  • Class B Misdemeanor — base offense for interfering with any of the listed duties with criminal negligence.

Defenses:

  • Warning a motorist about a speed trap is a defense if directed at traffic enforcement only.
  • Speech alone (without physical action) is a defense.

Special note for doxing officers: Intentionally publishing a peace officer's home address, phone, or other protected personal information creates a rebuttable presumption of interference.

§38.151 INTERFERENCE WITH POLICE SERVICE ANIMALS ▾

§38.151 – INTERFERENCE WITH POLICE SERVICE ANIMALS

Taunting, hurting, or killing a police K-9 or horse.

  • Class C Misdemeanor — taunting, tormenting, or striking a police service animal.
  • Class B Misdemeanor — throwing an object or substance at a police service animal.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — interfering with or obstructing the animal/handler (inhibiting or depriving control), releasing the animal from its area, or entering its area without consent.
  • State Jail Felony — injuring a police service animal or engaging in conduct likely to injure it (e.g., setting a poison or trap).
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — killing a police service animal; injuring it in a way that permanently prevents it from working; or engaging in conduct likely to cause such permanent injury or death.
§38.152 INTERFERENCE WITH RADIO FREQUENCY LICENSED TO GOVERNMENT ENTITY ▾

§38.152 – INTERFERENCE WITH RADIO FREQUENCY LICENSED TO GOVERNMENT ENTITY

Jamming or disrupting a police, fire department, or EMS radio frequency.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — base offense: without consent of the law enforcement, fire, or EMS agency, intentionally interrupting, disrupting, jamming, or otherwise interfering with their FCC-licensed radio frequency.
  • State Jail Felony — if the interference was intended to facilitate another crime or to prevent emergency response.
§38.16 PREVENTING EXECUTION OF CIVIL PROCESS ▾

§38.16 – PREVENTING EXECUTION OF CIVIL PROCESS

Using words or actions to block a process server or officer from delivering legal papers in a civil case.

  • Class C Misdemeanor — intentionally or knowingly preventing the execution of any civil court process.

Exception: Simply evading service by avoiding detection is not an offense under this section.

§38.17 FAILURE TO STOP OR REPORT SEXUAL OR ASSAULTIVE OFFENSE AGAINST CHILD ▾

§38.17 – FAILURE TO STOP OR REPORT SEXUAL OR ASSAULTIVE OFFENSE AGAINST CHILD

Witnessing a sexual or violent attack on a child and doing nothing — not helping and not calling police.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — a person who observes what a reasonable person would believe is a sexual or assaultive offense being committed or about to be committed against a child, and fails to either assist the child or immediately report it to law enforcement — so long as doing so would not put the witness in danger of serious bodily injury or death.
§38.171 FAILURE TO REPORT FELONY ▾

§38.171 – FAILURE TO REPORT FELONY

Witnessing what looks like a serious felony (one that may have caused death or serious injury) and not reporting it to police.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — a person who observes the commission of a felony where serious bodily injury or death may have resulted, and fails to immediately report it to a peace officer or law enforcement — when a reasonable person would believe it had not been reported and reporting would not put the witness in danger.
§38.172 FAILURE TO REPORT ASSAULT, NEGLECT, OR OMISSION OF CARE IN CERTAIN GROUP HOMES ▾

§38.172 – FAILURE TO REPORT ASSAULT, NEGLECT, OR OMISSION OF CARE IN CERTAIN GROUP HOMES

Knowing that a group home resident was hurt through assault or neglect and not telling law enforcement or CPS.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — a person with actual knowledge that a resident of a qualifying group home suffered bodily injury from assault, neglect, or omission of care who fails to report it to law enforcement or DFPS.

Exceptions: Does not apply to licensed facility operators, or if the injury occurred in certain facility types (hotels, retirement communities, churches, daycare centers, family violence shelters, college dormitories, etc.).

§38.18 USE OF COLLISION REPORT INFORMATION AND OTHER INFORMATION FOR PECUNIARY GAIN ▾

§38.18 – USE OF COLLISION REPORT INFORMATION AND OTHER INFORMATION FOR PECUNIARY GAIN

Taking crash report data or emergency dispatch info obtained from the government and using it to directly solicit business from accident victims.

  • Class B Misdemeanor — a person who obtains accident/emergency report info from a government entity and that information is then used to directly solicit business or employment for profit (by the person, their agent, or a third party on whose behalf it was requested).
§38.19 FAILURE TO PROVIDE NOTICE AND REPORT OF DEATH OF RESIDENT OF INSTITUTION ▾

§38.19 – FAILURE TO PROVIDE NOTICE AND REPORT OF DEATH OF RESIDENT OF INSTITUTION

A superintendent or manager of an institution (like a state school or mental health facility) who fails to properly report the death of a person in their care.

  • Class B Misdemeanor — failing to give required notice of death, failing to submit a required report, or failing to include known material facts in the report.

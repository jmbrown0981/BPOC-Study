§71.02 ENGAGING IN ORGANIZED CRIMINAL ACTIVITY ▾

§71.02 – ENGAGING IN ORGANIZED CRIMINAL ACTIVITY

Committing (or conspiring to commit) a crime as part of an organized group — a gang, a criminal combination, or a foreign terrorist organization. The charge is automatically one level higher than the underlying crime.

A person commits this offense if, with the intent to establish, maintain, or participate in a combination (3 or more people collaborating in criminal activities), in the profits of a combination, or as a member of a criminal street gang or foreign terrorist organization, the person commits or conspires to commit one or more of the following underlying offenses:

  • Murder, capital murder, arson, aggravated robbery, robbery, burglary, theft, aggravated kidnapping, kidnapping, aggravated assault, aggravated sexual assault, sexual assault, continuous sexual abuse of a child or disabled individual, solicitation of a minor, forgery, deadly conduct, Class A assault, burglary of a motor vehicle, or unauthorized use of a motor vehicle
  • Any Class A misdemeanor gambling offense
  • Promotion of prostitution, aggravated promotion of prostitution, or compelling prostitution
  • Unlawful manufacture, transportation, repair, or sale of firearms or prohibited weapons
  • Unlawful manufacture, delivery, or distribution of a controlled substance or dangerous drug (through fraud or with intent to deliver), or unlawful possession with intent to deliver
  • Wholesale promotion or possession of obscene material or devices for wholesale promotion
  • Certain offenses involving sexual content depicting children (Chapter 43)
  • Any felony under Chapter 32 (Fraud) or Chapter 36 (Bribery/Corrupt Influence)
  • Any offense under Chapters 34, 35, or 35A (Money Laundering, Insurance Fraud)
  • Impersonating a public servant (§37.11(a)), Tampering with a Governmental Record (§37.10), or other obstruction offenses (§38.06, §38.07, §38.09, §38.11)
  • Dog fighting (§42.10), certain criminal mischief, certain theft-related offenses (§31.21, §31.22)
  • Human trafficking and related offenses (Chapter 20A, §20.05, §20.06, §20.07)
  • Unlawful interception of communications (§16.02)
  • Certain obstruction of highway offenses (§42.03(d)/(e)), certain weapons offenses (§46.06(a)(1), §46.14)
  • Any felony under the Tax Code
  • Street racing (§545.420, Transportation Code)

Punishment — Automatic One-Level Elevation

  • The offense is one category higher than the most serious underlying offense committed
  • If the most serious underlying offense is a Class A Misdemeanor, the organized crime charge becomes a State Jail Felony
  • The offense is a Felony of the 1st Degree (15–99 years or life) if the most serious underlying offense is already punishable as a 1st degree felony
  • The offense is a Felony of the 1st Degree punishable by 30–99 years or life if the most serious offense is the aggravated trafficking offense under §20.06(g)
  • The offense is a Felony of the 1st Degree punishable by Life Without Parole if the most serious offense is aggravated sexual assault and the defendant is 18 or older and the victim was under 6, under 14 with penetration, or under 17 and suffered serious bodily injury

Deadly Weapon Enhancement

  • If the suspect used or exhibited a deadly weapon during one or more of the underlying offenses, the punishment is one additional level higher than calculated above

Renunciation Defense

At the punishment stage only, a suspect may raise the issue of whether they voluntarily and completely renounced the criminal objective, withdrew from the combination before any listed offense was committed, and made a substantial effort to prevent it. If proven by a preponderance of the evidence, the punishment drops back down to the same level as the most serious underlying offense (not one level higher).

Note: A conspiracy to commit this offense is punished the same as the completed offense itself — there is no reduction for mere conspiracy.

§71.021 VIOLATION OF COURT ORDER ENJOINING ORGANIZED CRIMINAL ACTIVITY ▾

§71.021 – VIOLATION OF COURT ORDER ENJOINING ORGANIZED CRIMINAL ACTIVITY

Violating a court injunction specifically issued to stop organized criminal activity.

A person commits this offense if they knowingly violate a temporary or permanent court order issued under §125.065(a) or (b) of the Civil Practice and Remedies Code — orders specifically issued by courts to enjoin (prohibit) organized criminal activity.

Grade: Class A Misdemeanor

Note: If the same conduct violates another Penal Code section, the suspect can be prosecuted under either or both sections.

§71.022 COERCING, INDUCING, OR SOLICITING MEMBERSHIP IN A CRIMINAL STREET GANG OR FOREIGN TERRORIST ORGANIZATION ▾

§71.022 – COERCING, INDUCING, OR SOLICITING MEMBERSHIP IN A CRIMINAL STREET GANG OR FOREIGN TERRORIST ORGANIZATION

Recruiting someone into a gang or terrorist organization — especially when that recruitment involves threats or violence, or targets a child.

Track A – General Recruitment

A person commits this offense if they knowingly cause, enable, encourage, recruit, or solicit another person to become a member of a criminal street gang or foreign terrorist organization that requires, as a condition of membership or initiation, the commission of any act punishable as a Class A Misdemeanor or a Felony.

Track B – Coercing a Child Through Threats or Violence

A person also commits this offense if, with intent to coerce, induce, or solicit a child to actively participate in a criminal street gang or foreign terrorist organization, the person:

  • Threatens the child or a family member with imminent bodily injury, OR
  • Causes bodily injury to the child or a family member

"Child" means anyone younger than 17 years of age.

Enhancement Ladder

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree (base)
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — for a second or subsequent offense under this section
§71.023 DIRECTING ACTIVITIES OF CRIMINAL STREET GANGS OR FOREIGN TERRORIST ORGANIZATIONS ▾

§71.023 – DIRECTING ACTIVITIES OF CRIMINAL STREET GANGS OR FOREIGN TERRORIST ORGANIZATIONS

Being a gang or terrorist organization leader who finances, directs, or supervises members committing serious felonies.

A person commits this offense if, as part of the identifiable leadership of a criminal street gang or foreign terrorist organization, they knowingly finance, direct, or supervise the commission of — or a conspiracy to commit — one or more of the following offenses by gang or organization members:

  • A felony listed in Article 42A.054(a) of the Code of Criminal Procedure (serious offenses for which deferred adjudication is barred — including murder, sexual assault, aggravated assault, robbery, kidnapping, and others)
  • Any felony where a deadly weapon was used or exhibited during the offense or immediate flight from it
  • Certain high-level drug trafficking felonies under the Health and Safety Code (large-quantity delivery of controlled substances in Penalty Groups 1, 1-B, or 2)

Grade: Felony of the 1st Degree — punishable by imprisonment for 25 years to 99 years or life in the Texas Department of Criminal Justice

Note: This is one of the most serious offenses in the Penal Code. The mandatory minimum of 25 years reflects the legislature's intent to target gang leadership specifically.

§71.028 GANG-FREE ZONES ▾

§71.028 – GANG-FREE ZONES

A location-based enhancement that raises the punishment one level higher when certain gang-related offenses are committed near schools, youth centers, playgrounds, shopping areas, or other protected locations.

This enhancement applies to the following offenses from §71.02(a) when committed in a gang-free zone — but not to burglary, theft, burglary of a motor vehicle, or unauthorized use of a motor vehicle:

  • Offenses listed in §71.02(a)(1) (violent crimes such as murder, assault, robbery, kidnapping, sexual assault, forgery, etc.)
  • Offenses listed in §71.02(a)(4) (unlawful manufacture/sale of firearms or prohibited weapons)
  • Offenses listed in §71.02(a)(7) (child pornography — Chapter 43)

Triggering Conditions

Punishment is raised to the next highest category if the suspect is 17 years of age or older and committed the offense at a location that was:

  • Within 1,000 feet of:
    • Real property owned, rented, or leased by a school or school board
    • Premises of an institution of higher education
    • A public or private youth center
    • A playground
    • A general residential operation functioning as a residential treatment center
  • Within 300 feet of:
    • A shopping mall
    • A movie theater
    • A public swimming pool
    • A video arcade facility
  • On a school bus

Limitation

Punishment cannot be enhanced under this section if the offense is already punishable as a Felony of the 1st Degree under §71.02 — it is already at the maximum level.

Note: Maps produced or approved by a municipality or county to show gang-free zone locations and boundaries are admissible as evidence of those boundaries (§71.029).

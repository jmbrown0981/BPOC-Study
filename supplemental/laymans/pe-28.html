§28.02 ARSON ▾

§28.02 – ARSON

Intentionally starting a fire or causing an explosion to destroy property, damage property belonging to others, or endanger life.

There are three tracks of arson liability:

Track A — Intentional Arson (§28.02(a))

Starting a fire or explosion with intent to destroy or damage:

  • Vegetation, fences, or structures on open land, OR
  • Any building, habitation, or vehicle — knowing it is inside city limits, insured, mortgaged, belongs to another person, contains another's property, or when the suspect is reckless about endangering life or property.
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — base offense.
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — if someone suffered bodily injury or death, OR if the targeted property was a habitation or a place of assembly or worship.

Track B — Reckless Arson During Drug Manufacturing (§28.02(a-1))

Recklessly starting a fire while manufacturing or attempting to manufacture a controlled substance, and that fire damages a building, habitation, or vehicle.

  • State Jail Felony — base offense.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if someone suffered bodily injury or death.

Track C — Reckless Property Damage or Injury While Setting Fire Intentionally (§28.02(a-2))

Intentionally starting a fire or explosion that recklessly damages another's building, or recklessly causes bodily injury or death to another person.

  • State Jail Felony

Defense

Controlled burning of open-space land (with landowner's consent and in a controlled manner) is not an offense under Track A for open land. A permit from the city also provides a defense for Track A fires in an incorporated area.

§28.03 CRIMINAL MISCHIEF ▾

§28.03 – CRIMINAL MISCHIEF

Intentionally or knowingly damaging, destroying, or tampering with someone else's property without their consent.

A person commits criminal mischief without the owner's effective consent by:

  • Intentionally or knowingly damaging or destroying tangible property, OR
  • Intentionally or knowingly tampering with property causing financial loss or substantial inconvenience, OR
  • Intentionally or knowingly making markings (inscriptions, drawings, etc.) on property.

Standard Punishment Scale (by pecuniary loss)

  • Class C Misdemeanor — loss under $100, or causes substantial inconvenience (no dollar value)
  • Class B Misdemeanor — $100 to under $750
  • Class A Misdemeanor — $750 to under $2,500; OR impairs/diverts a public water supply (any amount)
  • State Jail Felony — $2,500 to under $30,000; OR damage to a habitation by firearm/explosive (any amount under $2,500); OR damage to livestock fence (any amount under $2,500); OR damage to public utilities/communications/gas (any amount under $30,000); OR catalytic converter damage during theft attempt
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — $30,000 to under $150,000; OR killing cattle, bison, or horses by firearm; OR impairs ATM access; OR impairs public power supply
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — $150,000 to under $300,000; OR $1,000–$300,000 damage to a habitation during criminal trespass (§30.05)
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — $300,000 or more

Special Rules

  • State Jail Felony (regardless of dollar amount) — damage to a place of worship, burial site, public monument, or community center with damage of $750–$30,000 (§28.03(f)).
  • State Jail Felony — damage of $750–$30,000 to a school or institution of higher education (§28.03(h)).
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — any amount of damage to livestock by introducing mad cow disease or listed agricultural diseases.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — any damage to transportation communications equipment (traffic signals, railroad signs) under $150,000.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — damaging copper/brass components of a critical infrastructure facility causing its impairment.
  • Multiple acts under one scheme may be aggregated for grading purposes.
  • Note: §28.10 raises any misdemeanor or state jail felony under Chapter 28 to a Felony of the 3rd Degree if committed during human smuggling (§20.05(a)(2)).
§28.04 RECKLESS DAMAGE OR DESTRUCTION ▾

§28.04 – RECKLESS DAMAGE OR DESTRUCTION

Carelessly (not intentionally) damaging or destroying someone else's property.

A person commits this offense if, without the owner's effective consent, they recklessly damage or destroy property. Unlike criminal mischief (§28.03), this requires only recklessness — not intent.

Punishment

  • Class C Misdemeanor
§28.07 INTERFERENCE WITH RAILROAD PROPERTY ▾

§28.07 – INTERFERENCE WITH RAILROAD PROPERTY

Throwing things at trains, trespassing on railroad property, tampering with tracks or signals, or derailing trains.

There are two main tracks:

Track 1 — Throwing Objects / Shooting at Trains

Throwing an object or discharging a firearm or weapon at a train or rail-mounted work equipment.

  • Class B Misdemeanor — base offense.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if someone is bodily injured.

Track 2 — Trespassing / Tampering / Obstructing / Derailing

Without the owner's consent:

  • Entering or remaining on railroad property — Class C Misdemeanor
  • Tampering with railroad property, placing obstructions on tracks, or causing a derailment — Class C Misdemeanor base, then elevated by pecuniary loss:
  • Class B Misdemeanor — $100 to under $750 loss
  • Class A Misdemeanor — $750 to under $2,500
  • State Jail Felony — $2,500 to under $30,000
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — $30,000 to under $150,000
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — $150,000 to under $300,000
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — $300,000 or more

Exception

Railroad employees or union representatives exercising rights under federal labor law (Railway Labor Act) are exempt from the trespassing (entering/remaining on property) provision.

§28.08 GRAFFITI ▾

§28.08 – GRAFFITI

Making unauthorized markings on someone's property using paint, indelible markers, or etching/engraving devices.

A person commits graffiti if, without the owner's consent, they intentionally or knowingly make markings (inscriptions, slogans, drawings, paintings) on another's property using:

  • Paint (including aerosol/spray paint)
  • An indelible marker (ink specifically designed to resist removal)
  • An etching or engraving device

Punishment Scale (by pecuniary loss)

  • Class C Misdemeanor — under $100
  • Class B Misdemeanor — $100 to under $750
  • Class A Misdemeanor — $750 to under $2,500
  • State Jail Felony — $2,500 to under $30,000; OR $750–$30,000 damage to a school, institution of higher education, place of worship/burial, public monument, or community center
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — $30,000 to under $150,000
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — $150,000 to under $300,000
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — $300,000 or more

Multiple graffiti acts under one scheme may be aggregated for grading purposes.

§28.09 DAMAGING CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE FACILITY ▾

§28.09 – DAMAGING CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE FACILITY

Attacking an electrical power facility in a way that causes a significant power outage.

A person commits this offense if, without the owner or operator's consent, they intentionally or knowingly damage, destroy, vandalize, or impair a critical infrastructure facility (electrical power generating facility, substation, switching station, control center, or transmission/distribution facility) and that act causes an extended power outage (lasting 2+ hours OR affecting 1,000+ meters).

Punishment

  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — base offense.
  • Felony of the 1st Degree if:
    • The pecuniary damage to the facility is $100,000 or more, OR
    • The suspect used a firearm, drone, cyber attack, or explosive weapon.
§28.10 ENHANCED PENALTY FOR CERTAIN MISDEMEANORS OR STATE JAIL FELONIES ▾

§28.10 – ENHANCED PENALTY FOR CERTAIN MISDEMEANORS OR STATE JAIL FELONIES

Any Chapter 28 offense that is normally a misdemeanor or state jail felony becomes a felony of the 3rd degree if committed during human smuggling.

This is a penalty enhancement provision, not a standalone offense. If a person commits any Chapter 28 offense (arson, criminal mischief, graffiti, etc.) that would normally be a misdemeanor or state jail felony, and they committed it while also committing smuggling of persons under §20.05(a)(2), the punishment is automatically elevated to a Felony of the 3rd Degree.

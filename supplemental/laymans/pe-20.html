§20.02 UNLAWFUL RESTRAINT ▾

§20.02 – UNLAWFUL RESTRAINT

Intentionally or knowingly restricting another person's freedom of movement without their consent.

Elements

The suspect intentionally or knowingly restrains another person — meaning restricts their movement without consent, substantially interfering with their liberty, by moving them somewhere or confining them. Restraint is "without consent" if done by force, intimidation, or deception, or if the victim is a child or incompetent person whose parent/guardian did not agree.

Punishment

  • Base offense: Class A Misdemeanor
  • Enhancement — State Jail Felony: The victim was a child younger than 17.
  • Enhancement — Felony of the 3rd Degree: (a) The suspect recklessly exposed the victim to a substantial risk of serious bodily injury; OR (b) the victim was a public servant the suspect knew was lawfully discharging official duties or being retaliated against; OR (c) the suspect was in custody or committed to a civil commitment facility and restrained any other person.
  • Enhancement — Felony of the 2nd Degree: The victim was a peace officer or judge the suspect knew was lawfully discharging official duties or being retaliated against. (This overrides the 3rd degree public-servant enhancement.)

Defenses

  • Affirmative defense (young child / relative): The restrained person was under 14, the suspect was a relative, and the suspect's only intent was to assume lawful control of the child.
  • Not an offense: Detaining or moving someone for a lawful arrest or detention.
  • Affirmative defense (teenager close in age): The restrained child was 14–16, the suspect used no force/intimidation/deception, and the suspect was not more than 3 years older.
§20.03 KIDNAPPING ▾

§20.03 – KIDNAPPING

Intentionally or knowingly abducting another person — going beyond simple restraint by hiding them or using deadly force.

Elements

The suspect intentionally or knowingly abducts another person. "Abduct" means restraining someone with the intent to prevent their liberation by either: (a) hiding them where they are not likely to be found, OR (b) using or threatening deadly force.

Punishment

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree

Affirmative Defense

  • The abduction was not coupled with intent to use or threaten deadly force; AND the suspect was a relative of the abducted person; AND the suspect's sole intent was to assume lawful control of the victim.
§20.04 AGGRAVATED KIDNAPPING ▾

§20.04 – AGGRAVATED KIDNAPPING

Kidnapping with a specific harmful purpose — or using a deadly weapon during the kidnapping.

Track 1 — Purpose-Based Aggravated Kidnapping

The suspect intentionally or knowingly abducts another person with the intent to:

  • Hold them for ransom or reward
  • Use them as a shield or hostage
  • Facilitate a felony or escape after a felony attempt/commission
  • Inflict bodily injury or sexually abuse them
  • Terrorize the victim or a third person
  • Interfere with any governmental or political function

Track 2 — Weapon-Based Aggravated Kidnapping

The suspect intentionally or knowingly abducts another person AND uses or exhibits a deadly weapon during the offense.

Punishment

  • Base offense: Felony of the 1st Degree
  • Reduction — Felony of the 2nd Degree: At punishment, if the defendant proves by a preponderance of the evidence that they voluntarily released the victim in a safe place, the grade drops to 2nd degree.
§20.05 SMUGGLING OF PERSONS ▾

§20.05 – SMUGGLING OF PERSONS

Using a vehicle or other means to transport, harbor, or assist people being hidden from law enforcement or brought into the country illegally.

Ways to Commit (Tracks)

  • Track 1a — Transporting to Conceal from Police: Using a motor vehicle, aircraft, watercraft, or other conveyance to transport someone with the intent to conceal them from a peace officer or special investigator.
  • Track 1b — Transporting to Flee Police: Using a conveyance to transport someone while fleeing a peace officer or special investigator attempting a lawful arrest or detention.
  • Track 2 — Harboring Illegal Entrants: Encouraging or inducing a person to enter or remain in the U.S. illegally by concealing, harboring, or shielding them.
  • Track 3 — Assisting Trespassers on Agricultural Land: Assisting, guiding, or directing two or more individuals onto agricultural land without the owner's effective consent.

Punishment

  • Base offense: Felony of the 3rd Degree (minimum 10-year term)
  • Enhancement — Felony of the 2nd Degree (minimum 10 years): If the offense creates a substantial likelihood of serious bodily injury or death to the smuggled person; OR the smuggled person is a child under 18; OR committed for pecuniary benefit; OR a firearm was possessed during the offense; OR committed under Track 1b (fleeing).
  • Enhancement — Felony of the 1st Degree (minimum 10 years): If the smuggled person became a victim of sexual assault or aggravated sexual assault as a direct result; OR the smuggled person suffered serious bodily injury or death.

Cooperation Reduction

If the State certifies the defendant provided significant cooperation to law enforcement, the minimum term may be reduced to 5 years.

Affirmative Defense

Defense that the actor is related to the smuggled person within the second degree of consanguinity or affinity (e.g., parent, sibling, spouse) — except where the offense created substantial risk of injury/death or the person was sexually assaulted.

§20.06 CONTINUOUS SMUGGLING OF PERSONS ▾

§20.06 – CONTINUOUS SMUGGLING OF PERSONS

Repeated smuggling offenses over a period of at least 10 days — treated as a single, more serious offense.

Elements

During a period of 10 or more days, the suspect engages in conduct that would qualify as Smuggling of Persons (§20.05) two or more times. Jurors must unanimously agree on the overall pattern — they do not need to unanimously agree on which specific acts occurred on which dates.

Punishment

  • Base offense: Felony of the 2nd Degree (minimum 10 years)
  • Enhancement — Felony of the 1st Degree (minimum 10 years): If the smuggling created a substantial likelihood of serious bodily injury or death, OR the smuggled person was a child under 18.
  • Enhancement — Felony of the 1st Degree (25 years to life): If the smuggled person became a victim of sexual assault or aggravated sexual assault as a direct result, OR the smuggled person suffered serious bodily injury or death.

Double Jeopardy Protection

If the victim is the same as in a §20.05 charge, the defendant generally cannot be convicted of both in the same criminal action.

§20.07 OPERATION OF STASH HOUSE ▾

§20.07 – OPERATION OF STASH HOUSE

Knowingly allowing or renting property to be used to facilitate smuggling or trafficking operations.

Ways to Commit

  • Track 1 — Using Your Own Property: The suspect knowingly uses or permits their property (real estate, building, vehicle, boat, tent, etc.) to commit or facilitate an offense under §20.05 (Smuggling), §20.06 (Continuous Smuggling), §20A.02 (Trafficking), §20A.03 (Continuous Trafficking), §43.04 (Aggravated Promotion of Prostitution), or §43.05 (Compelling Prostitution).
  • Track 2 — Renting Property for That Purpose: The suspect rents or leases property to someone else, intending it to be used for the above purposes.

Punishment

  • Base offense: Felony of the 3rd Degree (minimum 5 years)
  • Enhancement — Felony of the 2nd Degree (minimum 5 years): If the property was used to facilitate continuous smuggling, continuous trafficking, or compelling prostitution; OR if a person was sexually assaulted, suffered serious bodily injury, or died as a direct result of the offense.

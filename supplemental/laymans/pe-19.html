§19.02 MURDER ▾

§19.02 – MURDER

Intentionally or knowingly causing someone's death, or causing death while committing a dangerous act during another felony — including deaths from fentanyl delivery.

Ways to Commit Murder (Tracks)

  • Track 1 — Intentional/Knowing Kill: The suspect intentionally or knowingly causes another person's death.
  • Track 2 — Dangerous Act with Intent to Injure: The suspect intends to cause serious bodily injury and takes an act clearly dangerous to human life, and that act kills someone.
  • Track 3 — Felony Murder: The suspect commits or attempts to commit any felony (other than manslaughter), and during that felony — or while fleeing it — takes an act clearly dangerous to human life that kills someone. The suspect does not need to have intended to kill.
  • Track 4 — Controlled Substance Delivery Death (Fentanyl): The suspect knowingly manufactures or delivers a Penalty Group 1-B controlled substance (e.g., fentanyl) in violation of the Health and Safety Code, and a person dies from using that substance — even mixed with other drugs.

Punishment

  • Base offense: Felony of the 1st Degree
  • Reduction — Felony of the 2nd Degree: At punishment, if the defendant proves by a preponderance of the evidence that the killing happened while the defendant was under the immediate influence of sudden passion arising from adequate cause (meaning provocation by the victim or someone acting with the victim, producing intense anger or terror that prevented calm reflection), the grade drops.

Key Terms

  • Adequate cause: A provocation that would make a person of ordinary temper incapable of cool reflection — not just being annoyed.
  • Sudden passion: Passion caused directly by the provocation, arising at the time of the offense — not old grudges.

Defense (Track 4 only)

It is a defense to prosecution under the drug-delivery-death track that the suspect's conduct in manufacturing or delivering the controlled substance was authorized by law (e.g., licensed pharmacist acting lawfully).

§19.03 CAPITAL MURDER ▾

§19.03 – CAPITAL MURDER

Murder plus an aggravating circumstance that makes the crime eligible for the death penalty or life without parole.

What It Is

Capital murder requires intentional knowing murder (§19.02(b)(1) only — not felony murder or the other tracks) PLUS at least one of the following circumstances:

  • The victim is a peace officer or firefighter acting in the lawful discharge of official duties, and the suspect knew that. (The suspect is presumed to have known if the victim wore a distinctive uniform or badge.)
  • The murder was committed during or in furtherance of kidnapping, burglary, robbery, aggravated sexual assault, arson, obstruction or retaliation, or certain terroristic threats.
  • The murder was committed for hire — either the suspect was paid to kill, or paid someone else to kill.
  • The murder occurred while the suspect was escaping or attempting to escape from a penal institution.
  • The suspect was already incarcerated when the murder occurred, and the victim was: (a) a prison employee, or (b) killed to further a criminal combination.
  • The suspect was already serving time for capital murder or certain other serious felonies (aggravated kidnapping, aggravated sexual assault, or aggravated robbery) and murdered someone.
  • The suspect murdered more than one person in the same transaction, or in separate transactions pursuant to the same scheme or course of conduct.
  • The victim was younger than 10 years of age.
  • The victim was 10 to 14 years of age.
  • The murder was committed in retaliation for the victim's service as a judge or justice of any Texas state court.

Punishment

  • Capital Felony — punishable by death or life imprisonment without parole.

Lesser Included Offense

If the jury does not find the suspect guilty of capital murder, they may still convict of regular murder or any lesser included offense.

§19.04 MANSLAUGHTER ▾

§19.04 – MANSLAUGHTER

Recklessly causing someone's death — not on purpose, but taking an unjustifiable risk that a reasonable person would not have taken.

Elements

The suspect recklessly causes the death of another person. "Reckless" means the person was aware of but consciously disregarded a substantial and unjustifiable risk.

Punishment

  • Base offense: Felony of the 2nd Degree
  • Enhancement — Felony of the 1st Degree: If it is shown at trial that the defendant also committed an offense under §28.09 (Racing on a Highway) and that racing caused the death.
§19.05 CRIMINALLY NEGLIGENT HOMICIDE ▾

§19.05 – CRIMINALLY NEGLIGENT HOMICIDE

Causing someone's death through criminal negligence — failing to be aware of a risk that a reasonable person would have recognized.

Elements

The suspect causes the death of another person by criminal negligence. Unlike recklessness, the suspect did not perceive the risk at all — but a reasonable person in their situation would have.

Punishment

  • State Jail Felony

Note: This is the lowest grade of criminal homicide. The mental state distinguishes it from manslaughter (reckless) and murder (intentional/knowing).

§19.06 APPLICABILITY TO CERTAIN CONDUCT ▾

§19.06 – APPLICABILITY TO CERTAIN CONDUCT

This chapter's homicide offenses do NOT apply to the death of an unborn child in certain circumstances.

Exceptions — Chapter 19 does NOT apply if the death of an unborn child results from:

  • Conduct by the mother of the unborn child.
  • A lawful medical procedure by a licensed provider with proper consent, where the death was the intended result of the procedure.
  • A lawful assisted reproduction procedure by a licensed provider with proper consent.
  • Dispensation or administration of a drug in accordance with law or as prescribed.

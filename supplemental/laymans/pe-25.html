§25.01 BIGAMY ▾

§25.01 – BIGAMY

Being married to one person while marrying or living as married with another — or knowingly marrying someone who is already married.

A person commits bigamy if they are already legally married and they:

  • Purport to marry or actually marry someone else, OR
  • Live with another person in this state while holding themselves out as married to that person.

It is also bigamy if a person knowingly marries or lives as married with someone they know is already married to another person.

Punishment

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — base offense.
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if the other person is 17 years old.
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — if the other person is 16 years old or younger.

Defense

It is a defense if the suspect reasonably believed their prior marriage was void or had ended (by death, divorce, or annulment), supported by a death certificate or court document. Note: the lawful spouse may testify for or against the suspect.

§25.02 PROHIBITED SEXUAL CONDUCT ▾

§25.02 – PROHIBITED SEXUAL CONDUCT

Sexual intercourse or deviate sexual intercourse between close family members (incest).

A person commits this offense by engaging in sexual intercourse or deviate sexual intercourse with someone they know is a close relative, including:

  • An ancestor or descendant (parent, grandparent, child, grandchild) — by blood or adoption
  • A current or former stepchild or stepparent
  • An aunt, uncle, niece, or nephew (whole or half blood, or by adoption)
  • A brother or sister (whole or half blood, or by adoption)
  • First cousins (children of a sibling)

Punishment

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — most relationships listed above.
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if the other person is a direct ancestor or descendant (e.g., parent–child, grandparent–grandchild).
§25.03 INTERFERENCE WITH CHILD CUSTODY ▾

§25.03 – INTERFERENCE WITH CHILD CUSTODY

Taking or keeping a child in violation of a custody order, or taking a child out of the country to deprive another person of their custody rights.

A person commits this offense if they take or keep a child under 18 years old in any of these ways:

  • Track 1: Taking or keeping the child while knowing it violates a court order (including temporary orders) about custody.
  • Track 2: When no custody order exists yet but a lawsuit has been filed — taking the child outside the judicial district without court permission and with intent to deprive the court of authority.
  • Track 3: Taking the child outside the United States with intent to deprive a person entitled to custody or visitation, without that person's permission.

A noncustodial parent also commits this offense by knowingly enticing or persuading the child to leave the custodial parent.

Punishment

  • State Jail Felony — all tracks.

Defenses

  • Track 2: Defense if the child was returned to the judicial district within 3 days.
  • Track 3: Affirmative defense if the taking was pursuant to a valid custody order, or if circumstances beyond the suspect's control caused the retention and reasonable notice was given.
  • Track 3 does not apply if the person was entitled to possession AND was fleeing family violence against the child or themselves.
  • If the conduct also qualifies as kidnapping (§20.03), the suspect can only be prosecuted under kidnapping.
§25.031 AGREEMENT TO ABDUCT FROM CUSTODY ▾

§25.031 – AGREEMENT TO ABDUCT FROM CUSTODY

Agreeing to be paid to snatch a child away from whoever legally has custody of them.

A person commits this offense if they agree, for money or other reward, to abduct a child under 18 years old by force, threat, deception, stealth, or unlawful entry — knowing the child is under the care and control of someone with a court-ordered custody arrangement.

Punishment

  • State Jail Felony
§25.04 ENTICING A CHILD ▾

§25.04 – ENTICING A CHILD

Luring a child away from a parent or guardian in order to interfere with lawful custody.

A person commits this offense if, with intent to interfere with lawful custody, they knowingly entice, persuade, or take a child under 18 from the custody of a parent, guardian, or someone standing in place of a parent.

Key difference from §25.03: This section covers situations where there is no court order — just interfering with natural parental custody.

Punishment

  • Class B Misdemeanor — base offense.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the suspect intended to commit a felony against the child.
§25.05 CRIMINAL NONSUPPORT ▾

§25.05 – CRIMINAL NONSUPPORT

Intentionally or knowingly failing to pay child support.

A person commits this offense if they intentionally or knowingly fail to provide support for their child under 18, or for a child they are under court order to support. "Child" includes children born out of wedlock if paternity has been acknowledged or legally established.

Punishment

  • State Jail Felony

Notable Points

  • A conviction can be based on the uncorroborated testimony of the other parent.
  • Affirmative defense: The suspect was genuinely unable to provide support.
  • A criminal prosecution does not prevent a civil court from also ordering support.
§25.06 HARBORING RUNAWAY CHILD ▾

§25.06 – HARBORING RUNAWAY CHILD

Knowingly sheltering a child who has run away from home or escaped from custody, without notifying authorities.

A person commits this offense if they knowingly harbor a child while being criminally negligent about whether the child:

  • Is younger than 18, AND
  • Has escaped from a peace officer, probation officer, the Texas Youth Council, or a detention facility — or is voluntarily absent from home without parental consent for a substantial time or without intent to return.

Punishment

  • Class A Misdemeanor

Defenses

  • The person was related to the child within the second degree (e.g., sibling, grandparent, aunt/uncle).
  • The person notified the agency the child escaped from, or law enforcement, within 24 hours of discovering the child.
§25.07 VIOLATION OF CERTAIN COURT ORDERS OR CONDITIONS OF BOND IN A FAMILY VIOLENCE, CHILD ABUSE OR NEGLECT, SEXUAL ASSAULT OR ABUSE, INDECENT ASSAULT, STALKING, OR TRAFFICKING CASE ▾

§25.07 – VIOLATION OF PROTECTIVE ORDER (FAMILY VIOLENCE / STALKING / TRAFFICKING)

Violating the terms of a protective order or bond condition related to family violence, sexual assault, stalking, or trafficking.

A person commits this offense by knowingly or intentionally violating a covered protective order or bond condition by doing any of the following:

  • Committing family violence or furthering an offense like trafficking, sexual assault, or stalking
  • Communicating directly with a protected person in a threatening or harassing manner, through a third party, or at all (if the order prohibits all contact)
  • Going to or near the protected person's home, workplace, or a child's school or daycare as described in the order
  • Possessing a firearm
  • Harming, threatening, or interfering with a pet, companion animal, or assistance animal of a protected person
  • Tampering with a GPS monitoring device
  • Tracking or monitoring the protected person or their vehicle without consent (including tracking apps or physical surveillance)

Punishment

  • Class A Misdemeanor — base offense.
  • State Jail Felony if:
    • Defendant violated a protective order issued after being convicted or placed on deferred adjudication for an offense against that same victim, OR
    • Defendant violated the order while possessing a deadly weapon (for most violation types).
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree if:
    • Defendant has been convicted two or more times under this section or §25.072 (or one each), OR
    • The violation involved committing an assault or stalking.

Notable Points

  • Reconciliation between the parties does not void the order or remove the duty to enforce it.
  • Officers cannot arrest a protected person for violating the same order.
  • Convictions from other states for substantially similar conduct count.
§25.071 VIOLATION OF PROTECTIVE ORDER PREVENTING OFFENSE CAUSED BY BIAS OR PREJUDICE ▾

§25.071 – VIOLATION OF PROTECTIVE ORDER (BIAS OR PREJUDICE)

Violating a protective order issued because of a hate crime — committing bias-motivated offenses or harassing a protected person covered by that order.

A person commits this offense by knowingly or intentionally violating a bias/prejudice protective order by:

  • Committing an offense against persons (Title 5) or certain property offenses (arson, criminal mischief, graffiti) because of bias or prejudice
  • Communicating directly or indirectly with the protected person in a threatening or harassing manner, or at all if the order prohibits all contact
  • Going near the protected person's home or workplace

Punishment

  • Class A Misdemeanor — base offense.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if convicted two or more times under this section, or if the violation involved committing an assault.
§25.072 REPEATED VIOLATION OF CERTAIN COURT ORDERS OR CONDITIONS OF BOND IN FAMILY VIOLENCE, CHILD ABUSE OR NEGLECT, SEXUAL ASSAULT OR ABUSE, INDECENT ASSAULT, STALKING, OR TRAFFICKING CASE ▾

§25.072 – REPEATED VIOLATION OF PROTECTIVE ORDER (FAMILY VIOLENCE / STALKING / TRAFFICKING)

A pattern offense: committing two or more protective order violations within a 12-month window.

A person commits this offense if, within a period of 12 months or less, they commit two or more acts that each constitute an offense under §25.07 (violation of a family-violence or similar protective order).

Punishment

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — base offense.
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if at least one of the violations was punishable as a state jail felony under §25.07(g)(1)(B) (i.e., committed while possessing a deadly weapon).

Notable Points

  • Jurors must unanimously agree the defendant committed at least two qualifying violations within the 12-month window.
  • The suspect cannot be convicted of both this offense and a §25.07 violation for the same conduct in the same criminal action (with limited exceptions).
§25.08 SALE OR PURCHASE OF CHILD ▾

§25.08 – SALE OR PURCHASE OF CHILD

Buying or selling a child for adoption — treating a child as a commodity.

A person commits this offense if they:

  • Sell side: Have custody or possession of a child under 18 and accept or agree to accept money or anything of value in exchange for delivering the child to another person for adoption purposes, OR
  • Buy side: Offer or give money or anything of value to obtain or maintain possession of a child for adoption.

Punishment

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — base offense.
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if the suspect intended to commit trafficking, promotion of prostitution, compelling prostitution, or sexual performance by a child.

Exceptions (not a crime)

  • Fees paid to a licensed child-placing agency as authorized by law
  • Attorney, social worker, or physician fees for normal legal or medical services
  • Reimbursement of legal/medical expenses for the child's benefit
  • Necessary pregnancy-related expenses paid by an agency as permitted by regulations
§25.081 UNREGULATED CUSTODY TRANSFER OF ADOPTED CHILD ▾

§25.081 – UNREGULATED CUSTODY TRANSFER OF ADOPTED CHILD

Handing off an adopted child to someone else without going through the court — bypassing the legal process designed to protect adopted children.

A person commits this offense if they knowingly:

  • Transfer permanent physical custody of an adopted child (under 18) without court approval as required by law, OR
  • Facilitate or participate in such a transfer — including transferring, recruiting, harboring, transporting, providing, soliciting, or obtaining an adopted child for this purpose.

Punishment

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — base offense.
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if committed with intent to commit trafficking, promotion of prostitution, compelling prostitution, sexual performance by a child, or possession/promotion of child pornography.

Exceptions (not a crime)

  • Placement with a licensed agency, DFPS, or an adult relative or close family friend
  • Temporary placement for vacations, school functions, or parental incapacity/military service
  • Out-of-state placements following proper legal procedures
  • Voluntary delivery under the safe-haven laws
§25.09 ADVERTISING FOR PLACEMENT OF CHILD ▾

§25.09 – ADVERTISING FOR PLACEMENT OF CHILD

Posting ads to place, provide, or obtain a child for adoption or permanent custody outside of licensed channels.

A person commits this offense if they advertise in public media (including online) that they will place, provide, or obtain a child for adoption or any other form of permanent physical custody.

Punishment

  • Class A Misdemeanor — first offense.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the person has previously been convicted under this section.

Exception

A licensed child-placing agency that identifies itself as such in the advertisement is exempt.

§25.10 INTERFERENCE WITH RIGHTS OF GUARDIAN OF THE PERSON ▾

§25.10 – INTERFERENCE WITH RIGHTS OF GUARDIAN OF THE PERSON

Taking, keeping, or hiding a ward from their legal guardian — similar to interference with child custody, but applies to incapacitated adults under a guardianship.

A person commits this offense if they take, retain, or conceal a ward (a person under legal guardianship) while knowing this interferes with the guardian's right to physical possession and legal domicile of that person.

Punishment

  • State Jail Felony

Exception

Does not apply to governmental entities taking action authorized by the Family Code or Human Resources Code (e.g., DFPS or APS investigations).

§25.11 CONTINUOUS VIOLENCE AGAINST THE FAMILY ▾

§25.11 – CONTINUOUS VIOLENCE AGAINST THE FAMILY

A pattern domestic assault offense: committing two or more assaults against family or household members within a 12-month period — charged as a single, more serious offense.

A person commits this offense if, within a period of 12 months or less, they commit two or more acts of bodily-injury assault (§22.01(a)(1)) against a person or persons who are:

  • A dating partner (current or former)
  • A family member (by blood, marriage, or adoption)
  • A member of the same household

Punishment

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree

Notable Points

  • Jurors do NOT need to agree unanimously on which specific incidents occurred — only that at least two assaults happened within 12 months against qualifying victims.
  • The suspect cannot be convicted in the same case of the individual underlying assaults that make up the pattern charge (to prevent double punishment), with limited exceptions.

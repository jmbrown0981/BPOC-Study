§39.02 ABUSE OF OFFICIAL CAPACITY ▾

§39.02 – ABUSE OF OFFICIAL CAPACITY

A public servant who uses their official position to benefit themselves, harm others, or misuse government property.

Track A – Violating a law relating to the public servant's job (§a(1))

  • Class A Misdemeanor — intentionally or knowingly violating a law relating to the public servant's office or employment, with intent to obtain a benefit or to harm/defraud another.

Track B – Misusing government property (§a(2))

Punishment scales with the value of the property misused:

  • Class C Misdemeanor — value less than $100
  • Class B Misdemeanor — value $100–$749
  • Class A Misdemeanor — value $750–$2,499
  • State Jail Felony — value $2,500–$29,999
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — value $30,000–$149,999
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — value $150,000–$299,999
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — value $300,000 or more

Notable exception: Airline miles and hotel/car rental rewards given for government travel are not considered government property for this purpose.

§39.03 OFFICIAL OPPRESSION ▾

§39.03 – OFFICIAL OPPRESSION

A public servant who abuses their authority to mistreat, oppress, or sexually harass someone.

The suspect must be a public servant acting under color of office — using their official position (real or claimed) to commit the conduct.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — intentionally subjecting someone to unlawful mistreatment, arrest, detention, search, seizure, dispossession, assessment, or lien they know is unlawful; intentionally denying or impeding a person's exercise of any right while knowing it's unlawful; or intentionally subjecting someone to sexual harassment (unwelcome sexual advances where submission is made a condition of any right, privilege, or benefit).
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the public servant acted with intent to impair the accuracy of data reported to the Texas Education Agency through PEIMS.
§39.04 VIOLATIONS OF THE CIVIL RIGHTS OF PERSON IN CUSTODY; IMPROPER SEXUAL ACTIVITY WITH PERSON IN CUSTODY OR UNDER SUPERVISION ▾

§39.04 – VIOLATIONS OF CIVIL RIGHTS OF PERSON IN CUSTODY / IMPROPER SEXUAL ACTIVITY WITH PERSON IN CUSTODY OR UNDER SUPERVISION

A correctional officer, facility employee, volunteer, or peace officer who abuses a prisoner's rights or has sex with someone in custody or under supervision.

Track A – Denying civil rights (§a(1))

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — intentionally denying or impeding a person in custody the exercise of any right, privilege, or immunity, knowing it is unlawful. (Note: one version of this statute classifies it as a Class A misdemeanor; the felony version reflects the more recent amendment.)

Track B – Sexual contact with a person in custody (§a(2))

  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — engaging in sexual contact, intercourse, or deviate sexual intercourse with a person in custody; or inducing/authorizing a juvenile offender to engage in sexual conduct.
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — if the victim is a juvenile in Texas Juvenile Justice Department custody, a juvenile placed in a juvenile facility, or a juvenile offender detained in a correctional facility.

Track C – Sex with a person under supervision but not in custody (§f)

Covers TDCJ, TJJD, juvenile probation, and community supervision employees who have sex with someone under supervision (not physically confined):

  • State Jail Felony

Affirmative defense (Track C only): The person under supervision was the employee's spouse at the time.

§39.041 IMPROPER SEXUAL ACTIVITY WITH COMMITTED PERSON ▾

§39.041 – IMPROPER SEXUAL ACTIVITY WITH COMMITTED PERSON

A civil commitment facility employee or volunteer who has sex with a sexually violent predator committed to the facility.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — an officer, employee, contractor, or volunteer of the Texas Civil Commitment Office who intentionally engages in sexual intercourse, deviate sexual intercourse, or sexual contact with a committed person.

Affirmative defense: The actor was the spouse of the committed person at the time.

§39.05 FAILURE TO REPORT DEATH OF PRISONER ▾

§39.05 – FAILURE TO REPORT DEATH OF PRISONER

A required official who fails to investigate and report an inmate's death as required by law.

  • Class B Misdemeanor — failing to conduct a required investigation into an inmate's death, failing to file the required report, or failing to include known facts in the report (under either CCP Art. 49.18 or Art. 49A.153); or failing to give required notice of inmate death or failing to investigate/report as required under §501.055, Government Code.
§39.06 MISUSE OF OFFICIAL INFORMATION ▾

§39.06 – MISUSE OF OFFICIAL INFORMATION

A public servant who uses inside, non-public government information to make money for themselves or others — or coerces others to keep quiet about it.

Track A – Trading on inside information (§a)

A public servant who, relying on nonpublic information they got through their job, acquires a financial interest in anything affected by that info, speculates on it, or coerces another into not reporting the info to law enforcement:

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — base (or if net gain is under $150,000)
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if net gain is $150,000–$299,999
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — if net gain is $300,000 or more

Track B – Disclosing nonpublic information (§b)

A public servant who uses or discloses nonpublic information for a non-governmental purpose, intending to benefit themselves or harm/defraud another:

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree (same enhancement for pecuniary gain as Track A)

Track C – Soliciting nonpublic information (§c)

Any person (not necessarily a public servant) who solicits or receives nonpublic info from a public servant, intending to benefit or harm/defraud another:

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree (same enhancement)
§39.07 FAILURE TO COMPLY WITH IMMIGRATION DETAINER REQUEST ▾

§39.07 – FAILURE TO COMPLY WITH IMMIGRATION DETAINER REQUEST

A sheriff, chief of police, constable, or jail administrator who knowingly refuses to hold someone subject to an ICE detainer request.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — a person who has primary authority for administering a jail who has custody of someone subject to a federal ICE immigration detainer and knowingly fails to comply with the detainer.

Exception: Not an offense if the detained person provided proof of U.S. citizenship or lawful immigration status (such as a Texas driver's license or similar government ID).

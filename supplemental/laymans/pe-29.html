§29.02 ROBBERY ▾

§29.02 – ROBBERY

Theft plus violence or threats — stealing from someone while hurting them or threatening to.

A person commits robbery if, in the course of committing theft and with intent to obtain or maintain control of the property, they:

  • Intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly cause bodily injury to another person, OR
  • Intentionally or knowingly threaten or place another in fear of imminent bodily injury or death.

"In the course of committing theft" includes attempts to commit theft, the theft itself, and the immediate flight after the attempt or commission.

Punishment

  • Felony of the 2nd Degree
§29.03 AGGRAVATED ROBBERY ▾

§29.03 – AGGRAVATED ROBBERY

Robbery with serious bodily injury, a deadly weapon, or against an elderly or disabled victim.

A person commits aggravated robbery if they commit robbery (§29.02) AND:

  • Cause serious bodily injury to another person, OR
  • Use or exhibit a deadly weapon, OR
  • Cause bodily injury, or threaten/place in fear of bodily injury or death, a person who is:
    • 65 years of age or older, OR
    • A disabled person (a person with a mental, physical, or developmental disability who is substantially unable to protect themselves from harm)

Punishment

  • Felony of the 1st Degree
§29.04 JUGGING ▾

§29.04 – JUGGING

"Jugging" is following someone from a bank or business with the intent to steal their cash, while carrying burglary/theft tools.

A person commits jugging if, with intent to steal another person's money, they:

  • Knowingly follow the same path or route as another person after they leave a commercial business or bank (without substantially deviating), AND
  • Are in possession of two or more criminal instruments (as defined by §16.01 — items made, adapted, or possessed for crime).

Punishment

  • State Jail Felony — base offense.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if, during the jugging, the suspect also commits burglary of a vehicle (§30.04).
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — if, during the jugging, the suspect also commits robbery (§29.02).

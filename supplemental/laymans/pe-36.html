§36.02 BRIBERY ▾

§36.02 – BRIBERY

Offering, giving, or accepting anything of value to influence how a public official, judge, party official, or voter exercises their official duties or vote.

The offense covers both sides of the bribe: the person offering/giving/promising the benefit AND the public servant/voter/party official soliciting/accepting/agreeing to accept it. The benefit must be tied to the exercise of official discretion.

  • Felony of the 2nd Degree – base and only grade

Not a defense: That the official wasn't yet in office, or lacked jurisdiction, or that the bribe was offered/accepted after the official act already occurred, or after the person left office.

Exception: Political contributions and lobbying expenditures reported under election or government ethics law do not qualify under subdivisions (1)–(3) — but can qualify under subdivision (4) only if there is direct evidence of an express agreement.

§36.03 COERCION OF PUBLIC SERVANT OR VOTER ▾

§36.03 – COERCION OF PUBLIC SERVANT OR VOTER

Pressuring or threatening a public official to act a certain way — or pressuring a voter to vote (or not vote) in a particular direction.

The suspect uses coercion to influence a public servant's specific official act or to get a public servant to violate their legal duty; or to influence a voter not to vote or to vote in a specific way.

  • Class A Misdemeanor – base grade
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – if the coercion involved a threat to commit a felony

Exception: Does not apply to a member of a governmental body's governing board who takes official action (including deliberating) to influence a public servant — that's considered part of the normal governance process.

§36.04 IMPROPER INFLUENCE ▾

§36.04 – IMPROPER INFLUENCE

Privately lobbying a judge or hearing officer about a pending case using arguments or considerations not allowed by law — "ex parte" communication to affect the outcome.

The suspect privately addresses a representation, argument, or communication to a public servant who is exercising or will exercise official discretion in an adjudicatory proceeding (court case or agency hearing), with intent to influence the outcome based on improper considerations.

  • Class A Misdemeanor – base and only grade
§36.05 TAMPERING WITH WITNESS ▾

§36.05 – TAMPERING WITH WITNESS

Bribing or threatening a witness or prospective witness to lie, hide evidence, skip court, or drop the case.

Two roles: (1) anyone who offers/gives a benefit to, or coerces, a witness/prospective witness in an official proceeding to testify falsely, withhold information, evade legal process, miss the proceeding, or drop/delay the prosecution; (2) a witness who solicits or accepts a benefit in exchange for any of those things.

Base + Enhancement Structure

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – base grade
  • Same category as most serious charge in the underlying case – if the official proceeding is part of a criminal prosecution
  • Felony of the 1st Degree – if the underlying criminal case is a capital felony
  • At least Felony of the 3rd Degree (or higher if underlying charge is higher) – if the underlying case involves family violence
  • At least Felony of the 2nd Degree – if the underlying case involves family violence AND the suspect has a prior family violence conviction

Defense (for the "drop prosecution" prong only): The benefit was reasonable restitution for the victim's damages and was negotiated with the help or approval of the state's attorney.

§36.06 OBSTRUCTION OR RETALIATION ▾

§36.06 – OBSTRUCTION OR RETALIATION

Harming or threatening a public servant, witness, informant, or crime reporter in retaliation for their role — or to stop them from doing it.

Two forms: (1) intentionally or knowingly harming or threatening (by unlawful act) someone in retaliation for their service as a public servant, witness, prospective witness, informant, or person who reported a crime — or to prevent/delay that service; (2) posting a public servant's home address or phone number on a public website with intent to cause harm to the public servant or their family in retaliation for their service.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – base grade
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree – if the victim was a juror targeted for their jury service, OR if the address-posting offense resulted in bodily injury to a public servant or their family member

Notable (address posting): If a public servant demands in writing that their address not be posted, and the suspect fails to remove it within 48 hours or reposts it within 4 years, that is prima facie evidence of intent to cause harm.

§36.07 ACCEPTANCE OF HONORARIUM ▾

§36.07 – ACCEPTANCE OF HONORARIUM

A public official accepting a speaking fee or payment for services that they were only invited to provide because of their government position.

A public servant solicits, accepts, or agrees to accept an honorarium (payment for services) that they would not have been asked to provide if not for their official position or duties.

  • Class A Misdemeanor – base and only grade

Exception: A public servant may accept transportation, lodging, and meals for a conference or event where they actually give a meaningful presentation or seminar (not just show up). Those items are also NOT considered political contributions.

§36.08 GIFT TO PUBLIC SERVANT BY PERSON SUBJECT TO HIS JURISDICTION ▾

§36.08 – GIFT TO PUBLIC SERVANT BY PERSON SUBJECT TO HIS JURISDICTION

A public official accepting any benefit from someone they regulate, investigate, supervise, sue, or are making decisions about.

Several categories of public servant are covered, each with its own relevant relationship: regulators/inspectors accepting from those they regulate; prison officials accepting from inmates; litigating attorneys accepting from adverse parties; officials with contract/payment discretion accepting from interested parties; judicial/administrative officials accepting from parties before them; legislators and their staff accepting from anyone; and hearing examiners in contested cases accepting from anyone appearing before their agency.

  • Class A Misdemeanor – for all categories of this offense

Notable: A public servant who receives an unsolicited prohibited gift may donate it to a government entity or to a recognized tax-exempt charitable organization rather than keeping it.

§36.09 OFFERING GIFT TO PUBLIC SERVANT ▾

§36.09 – OFFERING GIFT TO PUBLIC SERVANT

The other side of §36.08 — offering or giving something to a public official that the official is legally prohibited from accepting.

The suspect offers, gives, or agrees to give any benefit to a public servant that the suspect knows the public servant is prohibited by law from accepting.

  • Class A Misdemeanor – base and only grade

Relative & Escalator Offenses

These offenses don't have a fixed bracket of their own — their grade is derived entirely from an underlying offense, so they aren't shown as cards above.

Criminal Attempt (§15.01) — one category lower than the offense attempted. Exception: attempted capital murder of a peace officer is not reduced — it stays a 1st-degree felony with a 25-year mandatory minimum and no standard parole eligibility (eff. 9/1/2025).
Criminal Conspiracy (§15.02) — one category lower than the most serious felony that is the object of the conspiracy.
Criminal Solicitation of a Minor (§15.031) — generally one category lower than the offense solicited. Exception: soliciting a minor to commit capital murder, trafficking, or specified sexual offenses is a 1st-degree felony regardless of the underlying offense's grade.
Engaging in Organized Criminal Activity (§71.02) — one category higher than the most serious predicate offense committed or conspired to further gang or terrorist-organization activity (predicates include theft, robbery, burglary, drug offenses, fraud, and more).
Providing Material Support to Facilitate Terrorism (Ch. 72) — one category higher than the underlying offense the support facilitates.

General Enhancements (Apply on Top of Any Bracket)

These aren't offenses in their own right — they're Chapter 12 modifiers that raise the punishment range of whatever offense they attach to, without necessarily relabeling the offense's stated grade. See the General Enhancements tab on the Offense Reference for full text.

Repeat & Habitual Felony Offender (§12.42) — prior felony convictions raise the punishment range of a new felony (e.g., 3rd-degree conduct with 2 prior felonies can reach a 1st-degree range).
Repeat Misdemeanor Offender (§12.43) — prior misdemeanor convictions raise a Class B or C offense's punishment range, up to Class A.
Deadly Weapon Finding — an affirmative finding at trial doesn't change the offense's grade but restricts parole eligibility.
Bias or Prejudice Motivation (§12.47) — raises the punishment range one category if the offense was motivated by bias or prejudice based on race, religion, ancestry, national origin, disability, sexual orientation, or gender.
Drug-Facilitated Offense (§12.49) — enhancement for using a controlled substance to facilitate certain offenses.
Disaster or Evacuation Area (§12.50) — one-category enhancement for offenses committed in a declared disaster or evacuation area.
Weapon-Free School Zone (§46.11) — raises the minimum punishment for certain weapons offenses committed in or near a school zone.

§49.02 PUBLIC INTOXICATION ▾

§49.02 – PUBLIC INTOXICATION

Being drunk or impaired in public to a degree that you could be a danger to yourself or others.

A person commits this offense if they appear in a public place while intoxicated to a degree that they may endanger themselves or another person.

Bars and other premises licensed under the Alcoholic Beverage Code count as public places for this offense.

Grade: Class C Misdemeanor

If the person is under 21 years of age, the offense is punishable in the same manner as a minor alcohol offense under the Alcoholic Beverage Code (Tex. Alco. Bev. Code §106.071), which can include additional consequences such as community service, alcohol awareness classes, and driver's license suspension.

Defense

It is a defense that the alcohol or other substance was administered for therapeutic purposes as part of professional medical treatment by a licensed physician.

Important: Public Intoxication is not a lesser-included offense of DWI — they are separate and distinct charges. A person can be charged with Public Intoxication independently of DWI.

§49.031 POSSESSION OF ALCOHOLIC BEVERAGE IN MOTOR VEHICLE (Open Container) ▾

§49.031 – POSSESSION OF ALCOHOLIC BEVERAGE IN MOTOR VEHICLE (Open Container)

Having an open alcohol container in the passenger area of a vehicle while on a public road.

A person commits this offense if they knowingly possess an open container in the passenger area of a motor vehicle located on a public highway — whether the vehicle is moving, stopped, or parked.

Key definitions:

  • Open container: Any bottle, can, or receptacle that contains any amount of alcohol and is open, has been opened, has a broken seal, or has had some contents removed
  • Passenger area: The area designed for the driver and passengers — does NOT include a locked glove compartment, the trunk, or the area behind the last seat if the vehicle has no trunk
  • Public highway: Any public road, street, highway, or maintained way open for motor vehicle travel, including the right-of-way

Possessing multiple open containers in a single incident counts as one offense.

Grade: Class C Misdemeanor

Exceptions (Not a Crime)

  • The person was a passenger (not the driver) in a vehicle used for hired transportation — a bus, taxi, or limousine
  • The person was in the living quarters of a motorized house coach or motorized house trailer (RV, motor home, or self-contained camper)

Citation Procedure (Special Rule)

For this offense, a peace officer must issue a written citation and notice to appear rather than taking the person before a magistrate. The person is released after signing the citation in duplicate.

§49.04 DRIVING WHILE INTOXICATED ▾

§49.04 – DRIVING WHILE INTOXICATED (DWI)

Operating a motor vehicle while intoxicated — BAC of 0.08 or higher, or mentally/physically impaired by any substance.

A person commits this offense if they are intoxicated while operating a motor vehicle in a public place.

"Intoxicated" means: (1) not having the normal use of mental or physical faculties due to alcohol, a controlled substance, a drug, a dangerous drug, or any other substance; OR (2) having a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.08 or more (measured as grams of alcohol per 210 liters of breath, per 100 ml of blood, or per 67 ml of urine).

Enhancement Ladder

  • Class B Misdemeanor (base) — minimum 72 hours confinement
  • Class B Misdemeanor (open container in suspect's immediate possession at the time of the offense) — minimum 6 days confinement
  • Class A Misdemeanor — if a specimen analysis shows BAC was 0.15 or more at the time of analysis
  • State Jail Felony — if the person operated the vehicle in a school crossing zone during the time the reduced speed limit was in effect

Prior Conviction Enhancements (§49.09)

  • Class A Misdemeanor with minimum 30 days confinement — if the person has 1 prior conviction for any intoxication-related driving, flying, boating, or amusement ride offense
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the person has 2 or more prior convictions for such offenses, OR has 1 prior conviction for Intoxication Manslaughter (§49.08)

Note: Having a prescription for the substance used is not a defense to DWI (§49.10). Proof of intent is also not required — DWI is a strict liability offense (§49.11).

§49.045 DRIVING WHILE INTOXICATED WITH CHILD PASSENGER ▾

§49.045 – DRIVING WHILE INTOXICATED WITH CHILD PASSENGER

DWI with a child under 15 in the car — automatically a felony, regardless of BAC level.

A person commits this offense if:

  • They are intoxicated while operating a motor vehicle in a public place, AND
  • The vehicle is occupied by a passenger younger than 15 years of age

Grade: State Jail Felony

Enhancement (§49.09)

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the person has previously been convicted of Intoxication Manslaughter or has 2 or more prior intoxication-related convictions

Note: This charge can be filed in addition to a standard DWI charge. The child passenger element alone elevates the offense to a felony — no aggravated BAC or prior record is required.

§49.05 FLYING WHILE INTOXICATED ▾

§49.05 – FLYING WHILE INTOXICATED (FWI)

Operating an aircraft while intoxicated — the airborne equivalent of DWI.

A person commits this offense if they are intoxicated while operating an aircraft (any aircraft — plane, helicopter, etc.).

Grade: Class B Misdemeanor — minimum 72 hours confinement

Prior Conviction Enhancements (§49.09)

  • Class A Misdemeanor with minimum 30 days confinement — 1 prior intoxication-related operating offense
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — 2 or more prior such convictions, or 1 prior Intoxication Manslaughter conviction

Note: Having a prescription for the substance used is not a defense. No proof of intent is required.

§49.06 BOATING WHILE INTOXICATED ▾

§49.06 – BOATING WHILE INTOXICATED (BWI)

Operating a watercraft while intoxicated — the on-water equivalent of DWI.

A person commits this offense if they are intoxicated while operating a watercraft.

"Watercraft" means a vessel, water skis, an aquaplane, or any other device used for transporting a person on water — except devices propelled only by water current.

Grade: Class B Misdemeanor — minimum 72 hours confinement

Prior Conviction Enhancements (§49.09)

  • Class A Misdemeanor with minimum 30 days confinement — 1 prior intoxication-related operating offense
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — 2 or more prior such convictions, or 1 prior Intoxication Manslaughter conviction

Note: Unlike DWI, there is no "public place" requirement for BWI — operating a watercraft while intoxicated is the offense regardless of the body of water. Having a prescription for the substance used is not a defense. No proof of intent is required.

§49.061 BOATING WHILE INTOXICATED WITH CHILD PASSENGER ▾

§49.061 – BOATING WHILE INTOXICATED WITH CHILD PASSENGER

BWI with a child under 15 on the watercraft — automatically a felony, mirroring the DWI child passenger offense.

A person commits this offense if:

  • They are intoxicated while operating a watercraft, AND
  • The watercraft is occupied by a passenger younger than 15 years of age

Grade: State Jail Felony

Enhancement (§49.09)

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the person has previously been convicted of Intoxication Manslaughter or has 2 or more prior intoxication-related convictions

Note: Added in 2023, this section is the watercraft counterpart to §49.045 (DWI with Child Passenger). The child passenger element alone elevates the offense to a felony — no aggravated BAC or prior record is needed.

§49.065 ASSEMBLING OR OPERATING AN AMUSEMENT RIDE WHILE INTOXICATED ▾

§49.065 – ASSEMBLING OR OPERATING AN AMUSEMENT RIDE WHILE INTOXICATED

Running a carnival or amusement ride — or setting one up — while drunk or impaired.

A person commits this offense if they are intoxicated while:

  • Operating an amusement ride (a commercially operated ride as defined by state law), OR
  • Assembling a mobile amusement ride (a ride that is transported and set up at various locations, such as a traveling carnival ride)

Enhancement Ladder

  • Class B Misdemeanor (base) — minimum 72 hours confinement
  • Class B Misdemeanor (open container in the person's immediate possession at the time) — minimum 6 days confinement

Prior Conviction Enhancements (§49.09)

  • Class A Misdemeanor with minimum 30 days confinement — 1 prior intoxication-related operating offense
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — 2 or more prior such convictions, or 1 prior Intoxication Manslaughter conviction

Note: This offense covers both operating and assembling phases — a person assembling the ride while drunk is just as liable as one running it.

§49.07 INTOXICATION ASSAULT ▾

§49.07 – INTOXICATION ASSAULT

Accidentally causing serious bodily injury to another person while operating any vehicle or amusement ride while intoxicated.

A person commits this offense if, by accident or mistake while intoxicated, they cause serious bodily injury to another person while:

  • Operating a motor vehicle in a public place
  • Operating an aircraft
  • Operating a watercraft
  • Operating an amusement ride
  • Assembling a mobile amusement ride

"Serious bodily injury" means injury that creates a substantial risk of death, or causes serious permanent disfigurement, or causes the prolonged loss or impairment of a body part or organ.

Base Grade: Felony of the 3rd Degree

Enhancements

  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if the serious bodily injury was caused to a firefighter or EMS personnel who was in the actual discharge of an official duty
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if the serious bodily injury resulted in a traumatic brain injury causing a persistent vegetative state
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — if the serious bodily injury was caused to a peace officer or judge who was in the actual discharge of an official duty

Note: Sections 49.07 and 49.08 do not apply to injury or death of an unborn child if the conduct was committed by the mother of that unborn child (§49.12).

§49.08 INTOXICATION MANSLAUGHTER ▾

§49.08 – INTOXICATION MANSLAUGHTER

Accidentally killing someone while operating a vehicle or amusement ride while intoxicated.

A person commits this offense if they:

  • Operate a motor vehicle in a public place, operate an aircraft, a watercraft, or an amusement ride, or assemble a mobile amusement ride, AND
  • While intoxicated, cause the death of another person by accident or mistake

Base Grade: Felony of the 2nd Degree

Enhancements to Felony of the 1st Degree

The offense rises to a Felony of the 1st Degree if the person caused the death of:

  • A firefighter, EMS personnel, peace officer, or judge in the actual discharge of an official duty, OR
  • More than one person during the same criminal transaction (under one version of the 2025 law), OR
  • The person had a prior intoxication-related conviction and was in violation of Chapter 51 (monitoring/supervision conditions) at the time of the offense (under an alternate 2025 version)

Note: Intoxication Manslaughter does not apply to the death of an unborn child if the conduct was committed by the mother of that child (§49.12). Also, a prior Intoxication Manslaughter conviction can be used to enhance a subsequent DWI or BWI charge to a 3rd degree felony under §49.09.

Art. 5.01–.02 LEGISLATIVE STATEMENT & DEFINITIONS ▾

Art. 5.01 – LEGISLATIVE STATEMENT

Texas's official policy position: family violence victims get maximum protection, full stop.

The Legislature declares family violence a serious danger and threat to society. Victims are entitled to the maximum protection from harm, abuse, or threats the law allows. Critically: in any law enforcement, prosecutorial, or judicial response, the responding officer must protect the victim without regard to the relationship between the alleged offender and the victim.

Art. 5.02 – DEFINITIONS

"Family violence," "family," "household," and "member of a household" all borrow their meanings from Family Code Chapter 71 — this article doesn't redefine them, it just imports the Family Code's definitions into criminal procedure.

Art. 5.03 FAMILY OR HOUSEHOLD RELATIONSHIP DOES NOT CREATE AN EXCEPTION TO OFFICIAL DUTIES ▾

Art. 5.03 – FAMILY OR HOUSEHOLD RELATIONSHIP DOES NOT CREATE AN EXCEPTION TO OFFICIAL DUTIES

"It's a family matter" is not a legal basis to stand down.

A peace officer's or magistrate's general duty to prevent crime — including acts of family violence — is not waived or excepted just because the potential offender and victim are family or household members. This closes off the old assumption that domestic disputes are somehow outside normal law enforcement duty.

Art. 5.04 DUTIES OF PEACE OFFICERS ▾

Art. 5.04 – DUTIES OF PEACE OFFICERS

What an officer must do on a family violence call, beyond just showing up.

An officer investigating a family violence allegation, or responding to a disturbance call that may involve family violence, has these primary duties:

  • Protect any potential victim of family violence.
  • Enforce Texas law, including out-of-state protective orders recognized under Family Code Ch. 88.
  • Make lawful arrests of violators.
  • Check whether the address involved matches a licensed or verified foster home listed in the Texas Crime Information Center.
  • Advise any possible adult victim of all reasonable means to prevent further violence, including the state-mandated written notice — and may provide additional local-resource information.
Art. 5.045 STANDBY ASSISTANCE; LIABILITY ▾

Art. 5.045 – STANDBY ASSISTANCE; LIABILITY

Officers may stay to keep the peace while a victim collects belongings and leaves safely.

At the officer's discretion, they may stay with a family violence victim to protect them while the victim removes personal property (their own, or a child's in their care) to a safe location in an orderly manner. An officer who provides this standby assistance is not civilly liable for acts or omissions connected to providing (or deciding whether to provide) it, and is not civilly or criminally liable if the victim wrongfully takes property that turns out not to be theirs.

Art. 5.05 REPORTS AND RECORDS ▾

Art. 5.05 – REPORTS AND RECORDS

The mandatory written-report checklist for every family violence incident.

An officer investigating a family violence incident, or responding to a disturbance call that may involve it, must make a written report including at minimum:

  • The names of the suspect and complainant.
  • The date, time, and location of the incident.
  • Any visible or reported injuries.
  • A description of the incident and how it was resolved.
  • Whether the suspect is a member of state military forces or on active-duty status.

Additional duties layered onto this core report:

  • If the address matches a licensed or verified foster home, report to Family and Protective Services within 24 hours.
  • If the suspect is a servicemember, notify the relevant military legal office.
  • Each agency must maintain a departmental code for retrieving family violence reports — prosecutors get access; Family and Protective Services gets access for cases involving foster youth 14 or older.
  • Departments must have procedures so responding officers can check whether a protective order exists and who it covers.
  • A certified copy of a protective order is presumed valid unless it's expired by its own terms, more than a year old, or the clerk has notified the officer it was vacated.
  • On request, the victim is entitled to a free copy of the non-exempt parts of the report.
Ties to: Art. 2A.202 requires this same core report content for certain assault/sexual assault/terroristic threat offenses even outside the family violence context.

Art. 42.014 FINDING THAT OFFENSE WAS COMMITTED BECAUSE OF BIAS OR PREJUDICE ▾

Art. 42.014 – FINDING THAT OFFENSE WAS COMMITTED BECAUSE OF BIAS OR PREJUDICE

Texas's "hate crime" finding — a fact-finding made during the underlying prosecution, not a separate charge.

Applies to any offense under Penal Code Title 5 (offenses against the person), plus arson (PC §28.02), criminal mischief (§28.03), graffiti (§28.08), and false alarm or report (§42.0601). The trier of fact — judge or jury, at the guilt/innocence phase — must find beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant intentionally selected the victim, or the victim's property, because of bias or prejudice against a group defined by race, color, disability, religion, national origin or ancestry, age, gender, or sexual preference — or because the victim was a peace officer or judge.

If the trier of fact makes that finding, the judge must enter it as an affirmative finding in the judgment — it isn't discretionary once the underlying determination is made. The finding is what Penal Code §12.47 hooks into to bump the offense up one punishment category, and it's also what qualifies the offense for an Art. 7B.101 protective order.

Reading 42.014 alongside Ch. 7B: a victim seeking a Ch. 7B, Art. 7B.101 protective order for a bias-motivated offense is relying on this same finding, made on the record in the criminal case itself — not a standalone civil determination.

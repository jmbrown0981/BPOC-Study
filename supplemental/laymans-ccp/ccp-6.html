BPOC 9.5 — an officer's duty to act when a threat of injury reaches them, and the force limits on doing so.

Art. 6.05 Art. 6.06 Art. 6.07
Art. 6.05 DUTY OF PEACE OFFICER AS TO THREATS ▾

Art. 6.05 – DUTY OF PEACE OFFICER AS TO THREATS

An officer who learns of a threat — by any means — must act to prevent it.

When an officer is informed, in any manner, that one person has threatened to injure themselves, another person, or another person's property (including a spouse), the officer must act to prevent the threatened injury if it's within their power. To do this, they may call in any number of citizens in the county to help, and may take the same preventive measures the threatened person themselves could lawfully take.

Art. 6.06 PEACE OFFICER TO PREVENT INJURY ▾

Art. 6.06 – PEACE OFFICER TO PREVENT INJURY

If it's happening right in front of the officer, the duty to intervene is even more direct.

When, in an officer's presence or view, someone is about to commit an offense against another person or their property (or is about to injure themselves), the officer must prevent it — summoning citizens to help if needed. The officer may use only the amount of force necessary to prevent the offense, and no more.

Art. 6.07 CONDUCT OF PEACE OFFICER ▾

Art. 6.07 – CONDUCT OF PEACE OFFICER

The officer's use-of-force limits mirror what the potential victim could lawfully do themselves.

When preventing an offense that's about to be committed in their presence or view, an officer's conduct is regulated by the same rules that govern the person about to be injured — meaning the officer may use all the force necessary to repel the aggression, but is bound by the same self-defense/defense-of-others framework a private citizen would be.

Reading Art. 6.05–6.07 together: the duty to prevent a threatened injury applies whether the officer learns of it secondhand (6.05) or witnesses it directly (6.06) — and in both cases, the force the officer may use is capped at what's necessary, measured against the same rules that would apply to the person being protected (6.07).

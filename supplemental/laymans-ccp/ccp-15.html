Art. 15.051 – POLYGRAPH EXAMINATION OF COMPLAINANT PROHIBITED

Officers and prosecutors cannot pressure a sexual-offense complainant into a polygraph — and can't use a refusal against them.

A peace officer or prosecutor may not require, request, or administer a polygraph examination to a person who charges or seeks to charge, in a complaint, the commission of an offense under:

  • PC §21.02 — Continuous Sexual Abuse of a Young Child or Children
  • PC §21.11 — Indecency with a Child
  • PC §22.011 — Sexual Assault
  • PC §22.021 — Aggravated Sexual Assault
  • PC §25.02 — Prohibited Sexual Conduct (incest)

A complaint under these sections may not be dismissed solely because the complainant declined a polygraph, and may not be dismissed based on the results of a polygraph the complainant did take.

Why it matters on patrol: this is a bright-line rule — there's no "voluntary" workaround. Suggesting a polygraph to a sexual-assault complainant, even informally, risks violating this article and undermining the case regardless of intent.

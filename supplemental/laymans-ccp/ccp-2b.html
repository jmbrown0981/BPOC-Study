Art. 2B.0251 INTERVENTION REQUIRED FOR EXCESSIVE FORCE; REPORT REQUIRED ▾

Art. 2B.0251 – INTERVENTION REQUIRED FOR EXCESSIVE FORCE; REPORT REQUIRED

A peace officer has an affirmative duty to step in and stop another officer's excessive force.

A peace officer must intervene to stop or prevent another officer from using force against a suspect if all three of these are true:

  • The force exceeds what's reasonable under the circumstances, AND
  • The officer knows or should know the force violates state or federal law, AND puts the person at risk of bodily injury without being immediately necessary to prevent imminent bodily injury to an officer or someone else, AND
  • The force isn't required to apprehend the suspect.

An officer who witnesses excessive force by another officer must also promptly file a detailed written report with the offending officer's supervisor.

Why it matters: this converts bystander inaction into a statutory violation — an officer who sees excessive force and does nothing isn't just breaching policy, they're breaching a Code-imposed legal duty.
Art. 2B.0252 USE OF NECK RESTRAINTS DURING SEARCH OR ARREST PROHIBITED ▾

Art. 2B.0252 – USE OF NECK RESTRAINTS DURING SEARCH OR ARREST PROHIBITED

Choke holds and carotid restraints are banned except as a last resort against death or serious injury.

A peace officer may not intentionally use a choke hold, carotid artery hold, or similar neck restraint while searching or arresting someone — unless the restraint is necessary to prevent serious bodily injury to, or the death of, the officer or another person. It's a narrow exception, not a general option on the use-of-force continuum.

Art. 2B.0301 DUTY TO REQUEST AND RENDER AID ▾

Art. 2B.0301 – DUTY TO REQUEST AND RENDER AID

Officers who encounter an injured person on duty must call EMS and provide first aid within their training.

A peace officer who encounters an injured person while on duty must immediately, as necessary: request EMS, and — while waiting for EMS to arrive — provide first aid or treatment to the extent of the officer's own skill and training.

Two exceptions release the officer from this duty:

  • Requesting help or providing treatment would expose the officer or someone else to a risk of bodily injury, OR
  • The officer is themselves injured and physically unable to request help or provide treatment.

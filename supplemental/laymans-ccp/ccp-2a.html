Chapter 2A: Officers; Powers and Duties

Art. 2A.001–.008 WHO IS A PEACE OFFICER ▾

Art. 2A.001 – PEACE OFFICERS GENERALLY

The master list of who holds peace officer authority in Texas.

Article 2A.001 lists roughly three dozen categories of Texas peace officers. Rather than memorize the exhaustive list, group them by type:

  • Core patrol/investigative agencies — sheriffs, deputies, and reserve deputies; constables and deputy constables; municipal police officers (including reserves); DPS troopers, Rangers, and reserve officer corps members; DA/criminal DA/county attorney investigators.
  • Specialized state agencies — TABC agents, Parks & Wildlife game wardens, Texas Medical Board investigators, insurance commissioner investigators, state fire marshal officers, attorney general investigators, Texas Facilities Commission officers, comptroller security/investigators.
  • Campus, transit & institutional officers — school district/open-enrollment charter/junior-college police, school marshals, hospital district officers, transit authority officers, county park rangers.
  • Niche commissions — railroad peace officers, Texas and Southwestern Cattle Raisers Association special rangers, arson investigators, tribal-council-commissioned officers (Alabama-Coushatta, Kickapoo, Ysleta del Sur Pueblo), adjunct police officers at certain private colleges.
Key takeaway: "peace officer" in Texas is a defined statutory term, not a job title — a person only has peace officer authority if they fall into one of Article 2A.001's specific categories and hold the required license/commission.

Art. 2A.002 – SPECIAL INVESTIGATORS

Federal agents — not Texas peace officers, but with limited state arrest/search/seizure authority.

A long list of federal criminal investigators (FBI, Secret Service, ICE, ATF, DEA, Postal Inspection Service, IRS Criminal Investigation, Naval Criminal Investigative Service, U.S. Marshals, State Department Diplomatic Security, and several inspector general offices) are not Texas peace officers, but the Code gives them state-law powers of arrest, search, and seizure as to felony offenses only. A few other categories (Homeland Security officers protecting federal property, certain CBP/Border Patrol agents at ports of entry, National Park Service law enforcement officers, U.S. Forest Service agents, commercial nuclear plant security) get narrower, situation-specific state authority.

Art. 2A.003 – PEACE OFFICERS COMMISSIONED BY TRIBAL COUNCIL

The tribal councils of the Alabama-Coushatta Tribe, the Kickapoo Traditional Tribe, and the Ysleta del Sur Pueblo may commission their own peace officers to enforce state law on the tribe's reservation. These officers can arrest for state-law violations on the reservation, and — if summoned to assist another agency, or while assisting one — can arrest off the reservation too. They must meet the same TCOLE licensing standards as any other peace officer.

Art. 2A.004 – PEACE OFFICERS FROM ADJOINING STATES

A commissioned peace officer from a state that borders Texas has the same powers as a Texas peace officer while in Texas, but only in narrow situations: while transporting an inmate/defendant to or from a border-area medical facility, or while on regular duty in a municipality within one mile of the state line (and only if that municipality's governing body has voted to authorize it).

Art. 2A.005 – RAILROAD PEACE OFFICERS

DPS may commission up to 250 railroad-company employees as peace officers to protect railroad property, passengers, and employees. Their arrest authority is limited to offenses involving injury to railroad passengers/employees or damage to railroad property — they cannot issue traffic citations, and they don't receive state peace-officer benefits. The railroad company (not the state) is liable for their conduct.

Art. 2A.006 – SPECIAL RANGERS OF TEXAS AND SOUTHWESTERN CATTLE RAISERS ASSOCIATION

DPS may commission up to 50 Cattle Raisers Association employees as "special rangers" limited to investigating livestock theft and related property crimes. Same structure as railroad peace officers: no traffic-citation authority, no state benefits, association bears the liability.

Art. 2A.007 – ADJUNCT POLICE OFFICERS

A police chief or sheriff can deputize campus officers from certain small private colleges as "adjunct police officers," authorized to act only within a defined area on and immediately around the campus. The private institution — not the municipality/county — is liable for their conduct off-campus.

Art. 2A.008 – SCHOOL MARSHALS

A licensed school marshal, appointed by a school district, private school, or junior college board, may make arrests and exercise peace officer authority — but only as necessary to prevent or stop an offense threatening serious bodily injury or death to a student, staff member, or visitor on school property. No traffic-citation authority, no state benefits.

Art. 2A.051 GENERAL POWERS AND DUTIES OF PEACE OFFICERS ▾

Art. 2A.051 – GENERAL POWERS AND DUTIES OF PEACE OFFICERS

The baseline job description every peace officer in Texas operates under.

Every peace officer must:

  • Preserve the peace within their jurisdiction using all lawful means.
  • Interfere without a warrant, in every case the Code authorizes, to prevent or suppress crime.
  • Execute all lawful process (warrants, subpoenas, etc.) issued by a magistrate or court.
  • Notify the appropriate magistrate of offenses committed in their jurisdiction when they have good reason to believe a violation occurred.
  • When authorized by law, arrest an offender without a warrant so they can be taken before the proper magistrate or court.
  • Take possession of a child under specific Family Code circumstances, and execute certain civil-commitment emergency detention orders on request.
Art. 2A.055 FINE FOR FAILURE TO EXECUTE PROCESS ▾

Art. 2A.055 – FINE FOR FAILURE TO EXECUTE PROCESS

Officers can be held in contempt for willfully refusing to serve process they're required to serve.

A sheriff or other officer who willfully refuses or neglects to execute a summons, subpoena, attachment for a witness, or other legal process they have a duty to execute is liable for a contempt fine between $10 and $200, enforced the same way as a civil contempt fine.

Art. 2A.057 INVESTIGATION OF CERTAIN REPORTS ALLEGING ABUSE, NEGLECT, OR EXPLOITATION ▾

Art. 2A.057 – INVESTIGATION OF CERTAIN REPORTS ALLEGING ABUSE, NEGLECT, OR EXPLOITATION

Joint-investigation duty with Family and Protective Services for the highest-priority child abuse reports.

When a local law enforcement agency receives a report that Family and Protective Services (or another investigating agency) has assigned its highest priority — alleging an immediate risk of physical or sexual abuse that could result in a child's death or serious harm — a peace officer must investigate jointly with the department, and must accompany the department's investigator within 24 hours of being notified. A related duty applies to abuse/neglect/exploitation reports involving residents of nursing homes, assisted living facilities, and similar institutions.

Art. 2A.061 MISUSED IDENTITY NOTIFICATIONS ▾

Art. 2A.061 – MISUSED IDENTITY NOTIFICATIONS

What happens when an arrestee gives someone else's name.

If a local agency learns that an arrested person falsely gave someone else's identifying information as their own, the agency must notify the real person that their identity was misused, that they may file a declaration with DPS, and that they're entitled to expunction of the resulting records — and must also notify DPS of the misuse, the arrestee's actual identity (if known), and whether the real person was successfully notified.

Art. 2A.063–.065 SHERIFF'S DUTIES ▾

Art. 2A.063 – SHERIFF AS CONSERVATOR OF THE PEACE

The sheriff's baseline authority within the county.

A sheriff is a conservator of the peace countywide and must: arrest anyone who commits an offense in the sheriff's view or hearing and take them before the proper court; suppress assaults, affrays, insurrections, and unlawful assemblies; and apprehend and jail offenders until an examination or trial can be held.

Art. 2A.064 – SHERIFF'S DUTIES RELATED TO CUSTODY OF DEFENDANTS

A sheriff must place in jail a defendant committed by a magistrate's or court's warrant. The exception: a defendant committed for want of bail, or arrested in a bailable case, may be allowed to remain out of jail a reasonable time to procure bail — but the sheriff must guard against escape while that's happening.

Art. 2A.065 – DEPUTY OR OTHER OFFICER TO DISCHARGE SHERIFF'S DUTIES

A sheriff's deputy may perform any duty the Code imposes on the sheriff. If a county has no sitting sheriff, the sheriff's criminal-law duties fall to whoever is legally empowered to discharge that office's duties during the vacancy.

Art. 2A.201–.202 REQUIRED REPORTS — SENSITIVE INFO & ASSAULTIVE/TERRORISTIC OFFENSES ▾

Art. 2A.201 – PEACE OFFICERS: REPORT IN CONNECTION WITH CERTAIN OFFENSES INVOLVING SENSITIVE INFORMATION

Written-report requirement for financial-information and identity-related offenses.

When a violation of PC §31.17 (financial abuse/theft involving a sight order) or §32.51 (fraudulent use/possession of identifying information) is reported to a peace officer, the officer must make a written report to their own agency including the victim's name, the suspect's name (if known), the type of information/order involved, and the results of any investigation. The agency must give the victim a copy on request, with confidential information redacted.

Art. 2A.202 – PEACE OFFICERS: REPORT CONCERNING CERTAIN ASSAULTIVE OR TERRORISTIC OFFENSES

Written-report requirement for assault, sexual assault, and terroristic threat.

This applies specifically to assault (PC §22.01), aggravated assault (§22.02), sexual assault (§22.011), aggravated sexual assault (§22.021), and terroristic threat (§22.07). An investigating officer must prepare a written report containing the same core information required under Art. 5.05(a) (see Chapter 5) — and, on request, the agency must give the victim, at no cost, the non-exempt portions of that report.

Art. 2A.209 DUTIES OF LAW ENFORCEMENT AGENCY FILING CASE ▾

Art. 2A.209 – DUTIES OF LAW ENFORCEMENT AGENCY FILING CASE

The discovery-disclosure certification an agency owes the prosecutor when filing a case.

When an agency files a case with the prosecuting attorney, it must submit a written statement from an employee with knowledge of the case confirming that every document, item, and piece of information required to be disclosed to the defendant under Article 39.14 (Texas's "Michael Morton Act" discovery statute) has in fact been disclosed to the prosecutor. If the agency later discovers or acquires any additional discoverable material, it must promptly disclose that too.

Why it matters: this is the statutory hook that makes an agency's discovery compliance an ongoing duty, not a one-time box to check at filing — new evidence found after filing still has to go to the prosecutor.

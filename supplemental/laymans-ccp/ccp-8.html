Chapter 8: Suppression of Riots and Other Disturbances

← Guide Index

BPOC 9.6 — an officer's authority to summon aid, disperse a riot, and the force limits on doing so.

Art. 8.01 Art. 8.04–.06 Art. 8.07 Art. 8.09
Art. 8.01 OFFICER MAY REQUIRE AID ▾

Art. 8.01 – OFFICER MAY REQUIRE AID

An officer facing resistance while executing process can deputize bystanders on the spot.

When an officer authorized to execute process is resisted, or reasonably expects resistance, they may command as many county residents as they think proper to help. If needed, the sheriff may call on any military company in the county to help overcome the resistance and seize/arrest the resisters.

Art. 8.04–.06 DISPERSING A RIOT & PERMISSIBLE FORCE ▾

Art. 8.04 – DISPERSING RIOT

Every magistrate or peace officer must act to break up a riot — by order or by arrest.

Whenever people are assembled in a way that constitutes a riot under the Penal Code, it's the duty of every magistrate or peace officer to make them disperse — either by ordering them to disperse or, if necessary, by arresting the participants, with or without a warrant.

Art. 8.05 – OFFICER MAY CALL AID

To disperse a riot, the officer may call on the power of the county the same way they would to execute process (see Art. 8.01).

Art. 8.06 – MEANS ADOPTED TO SUPPRESS

Force is authorized, but capped at what's actually necessary.

An officer suppressing a riot — and anyone assisting — is authorized to adopt measures necessary to suppress it, but may not use any greater degree of force than is required to accomplish that.

Art. 8.07 UNLAWFUL ASSEMBLY ▾

Art. 8.07 – UNLAWFUL ASSEMBLY

Everything above applies equally to unlawful assemblies, not just full riots.

The riot-suppression articles in this chapter apply equally to an unlawful assembly and other unlawful disturbances as defined by the Penal Code — the officer isn't limited to acting only once a gathering has technically escalated to a "riot."

Art. 8.09 POWER OF SPECIAL CONSTABLE ▾

Art. 8.09 – POWER OF SPECIAL CONSTABLE

A special constable appointed to help with a disturbance has full peace officer authority for that assignment.

Special constables appointed under this chapter (see Art. 8.08, e.g. to suppress disturbances at elections) exercise the same powers and duties as any peace officer for the duration of their appointment.

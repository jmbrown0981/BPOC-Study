Plain-language walkthroughs of the specific CCP articles cited in BPOC Chapter 9 (Code of Criminal Procedure) — who's a peace officer, what officers and sheriffs are legally required to do, and the victim-rights and protective-order rules that govern the calls they run. Scoped to what BPOC actually teaches, not the full ~86-chapter Code — Chapter 10 (arrest/search/seizure) and its dedicated case law bank cover the Fourth/Fifth Amendment side separately.

How this differs from the Offense Reference style guides: the Penal Code guide walks through offenses, elements, and punishment ranges. This guide walks through procedure and duty — what an officer, sheriff, or magistrate is required (or prohibited) to do at each stage, from who counts as a peace officer through victim-notification duties. Where BPOC's own citations have drifted from current law (e.g. the old Chapter 13 "Art. 13.38" venue rule, now recodified as Art. 13A.203), this guide flags it explicitly rather than silently correcting it.

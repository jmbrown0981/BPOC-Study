Art. 56A.052 ADDITIONAL RIGHTS — SEXUAL, TRAFFICKING, OR BURGLARY VICTIMS ▾

Art. 56A.052 – ADDITIONAL RIGHTS OF VICTIMS OF CERTAIN SEXUAL, TRAFFICKING, OR BURGLARY OFFENSES

Beyond the general crime-victim rights, victims of these specific offenses get evidence-tracking and health-related rights.

A victim, guardian, or close relative of a deceased victim of indecency with a child, sexual assault, aggravated sexual assault, or prohibited sexual conduct (PC §21.02, 21.11, 22.011, 22.012, 22.021) is entitled, on request, to:

  • Disclosure of evidence collected during the investigation and its analysis status (unless disclosure would interfere with the investigation — in which case they get an estimated disclosure date instead).
  • Notification when evidence is submitted to a crime lab, when it's compared against a DNA database, and the results of that comparison (again, subject to the same investigation-interference exception).
  • Counseling regarding HIV/AIDS.
  • Being informed of, and able to share their views on, case disposition decisions — declining to file charges, dismissing charges, pretrial intervention, or a plea bargain.
  • For the victim specifically: HIV/AIDS testing, a forensic medical examination, and continuing medical care related to the assault for 30 days following that exam.

Anyone requesting these notifications must keep their contact information current with the prosecutor and investigating agency. Victims of trafficking or continuous trafficking (or a related aggravated-promotion offense) also have a separate right to be informed they may petition to have their own related criminal record nondisclosed, if they committed that offense solely as a result of being trafficked.

Victims of the offenses covered by Art. 7B.001 also get a bundle of rights specific to obtaining a protective order: being told they can apply (or that the prosecutor can apply on their behalf), being told which court to file in, and — if convicted/deferred-adjudicated — generally being entitled to have the prosecutor file the application on their behalf.

Art. 56A.0521 ADDITIONAL RIGHTS — FAMILY VIOLENCE, STALKING & PROTECTIVE ORDER/BOND VIOLATIONS ▾

Art. 56A.0521 – ADDITIONAL RIGHTS OF VICTIMS OF CERTAIN FAMILY VIOLENCE OFFENSES, STALKING, AND VIOLATION OF PROTECTIVE ORDER OR CONDITION OF BOND

A parallel evidence/disposition rights package for family violence and stalking victims.

Applies to offenses involving family violence, stalking (PC §42.072), or violations of protective orders/bond conditions (PC §25.07, 25.071, 25.072) that occurred through an assault, aggravated assault, sexual assault, or stalking. Covered victims are entitled to the same core rights as Art. 56A.052 — evidence disclosure, lab-submission notification, and being informed of/consulted on case disposition — plus an explicit right to be told the prosecutor does not represent the victim personally. If the offense is stalking, the victim also gets the full protective-order-notification rights described in Art. 56A.052(d). Where this article and Art. 56A.052 overlap or conflict, Art. 56A.052 controls.

Art. 56A.3515 PRESENCE OF SEXUAL ASSAULT PROGRAM ADVOCATE DURING LAW ENFORCEMENT INTERVIEW ▾

Art. 56A.3515 – PRESENCE OF SEXUAL ASSAULT PROGRAM ADVOCATE OR OTHER VICTIM'S REPRESENTATIVE DURING LAW ENFORCEMENT INTERVIEW

Before interviewing an adult sexual assault victim, the interviewing officer has to offer them an advocate.

Before an investigative interview with an adult sexual assault victim (this doesn't apply to minors, who are handled under separate Family Code procedures), the interviewing officer must offer the victim the chance to have a trained sexual assault program advocate present, if one is available. If no advocate is available, the officer must instead offer a crime victim liaison, a specially trained peace officer, or a victim's assistance counselor. The victim also has an independent right to have an attorney present. None of these support people may unreasonably delay or impede the interview — their role is limited to counseling/support and explaining the victim's rights, not participating in the substance of the interview.

Balancing act: Officers must make a good-faith effort to comply, but compliance can't be used as an excuse to unreasonably delay or derail the interview itself — the two duties (offer support, keep the investigation moving) run in parallel, not one blocking the other.
Art. 56A.403 DUTIES OF PEACE OFFICERS REGARDING VICTIMS OF SEXUAL ASSAULT ▾

Art. 56A.403 – DUTIES OF PEACE OFFICERS REGARDING VICTIMS OF SEXUAL ASSAULT

The specific checklist an officer must complete at first contact with a sexual assault victim.

An officer investigating a sexual assault, or responding to a disturbance call that may involve one, must provide the victim a written notice covering their rights under Art. 56A.052 and Chapter 58 (pseudonym/confidentiality rights). At the earliest possible point of contact, the officer must also:

  • Give the victim a written referral to the nearest sexual assault program, and information about the statewide electronic evidence-tracking system.
  • Offer to request a forensic medical examination on the victim's behalf.
  • Coordinate with the local sexual-assault response team for continuing care or further investigation.

The required written notice (developed with input from the local sexual assault program, in English and Spanish) must tell the victim, among other things, that they have the right to: a forensic medical exam within 120 hours (even without reporting to police); track evidence status anonymously; have an advocate present during the exam and the interview; ask the prosecutor to file charges; and — if a defendant is arrested — seek an emergency protective order, request a pseudonym under Chapter 58, and apply for a permanent protective order.

Ties together: this article is the operational checklist that pulls together the Art. 56A.052 rights, the Art. 56A.3515 advocate-presence right, and the Chapter 58 pseudonym right into one first-contact duty for the responding officer.

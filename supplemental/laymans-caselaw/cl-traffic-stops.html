Traffic Stops: Occupant Control

← Guide Index

Once a car is lawfully stopped, who can the officer order out of it — and why does officer safety, not suspicion of the individual, do the constitutional work?

Mimms Wilson
1977 PENNSYLVANIA v. MIMMS ▾

Pennsylvania v. Mimms, 434 U.S. 106 (1977)

Once a car is lawfully stopped, an officer may order the driver out — no extra justification required.

Facts: Officers stopped a car for an expired license plate. One officer asked the driver to step out and produce his license and registration. When the driver got out, the officer noticed a large bulge under his jacket, feared it was a weapon, and frisked him — finding a loaded revolver.

Holding: Once a driver has been lawfully stopped, an officer may order the driver out of the car without violating the Fourth Amendment — officer safety alone is a legitimate and weighty justification, and no additional suspicion about that specific driver is required. Once the bulge was observed, a Terry-style frisk for weapons was justified.

On patrol: the authority to order the driver out doesn't come from suspecting the driver of anything extra — the stop itself is enough. Officer safety is treated as inherent to every traffic stop.
1997 MARYLAND v. WILSON ▾

Maryland v. Wilson, 519 U.S. 408 (1997)

The Mimms rule extends to passengers too — not just the driver.

Facts: A trooper stopped a speeding car with no visible license tag. Noticing the passenger's apparent nervousness, the trooper ordered him out of the car as well. As the passenger got out, cocaine fell to the ground, leading to his arrest.

Holding: An officer making a lawful traffic stop may also order passengers out of the car pending completion of the stop, for the same officer-safety reasons recognized in Mimms — this doesn't require any individualized suspicion about the passenger either. (The lower Maryland court had held Mimms applied only to drivers; the Supreme Court reversed that limitation.)

Mimms + Wilson together: during any lawful traffic stop, every occupant of the vehicle — driver and passengers alike — may be ordered out for officer safety, without needing to justify it as to that specific person.

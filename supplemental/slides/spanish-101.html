Spanish Doesn't Translate
Word for Word

The BPOC card gives you phrases to memorize. Three ideas explain most of why they look so different from the English.

IDEA 1

The verb shows who

The ending on the verb tells you who is acting, so the pronoun is usually dropped: hablo = “I speak.”

IDEA 2

Two ways to say “you”

Formal usted uses the “he/she” verb form. That's the one you use on duty.

IDEA 3

Little words attach

“Him,” “me,” and “yourself” glue onto commands: siéntese, deme, golpéalo.

Running example: ¿Quiere que llame una grúa? is literally “Want (you) that I call a tow truck?” By slide 6 you'll see why that becomes “Do you want me to call a tow truck?”

Usted vs. Tú — and why usted uses the “he/she” verb

¿Habla inglés? could mean he, she, or you. Adding usted makes it clear: ¿Habla usted inglés?→ “Do you speak English?”

Why both appear on the card: commands to the public use usted (siéntese, no se mueva). The 26.3 “danger words” are tú forms (pégale, córrele) because they're what suspects say to each other.

Verbs: stem + ending

Take the dictionary form, drop -ar / -er / -ir, and add the ending for who is acting: trabajar→ trabaj-→ trabajo, trabajas, trabaja, trabajamos, trabajan.

Commands: the “opposite vowel” rule

Usted command = the yo form, drop the -o, add the opposite vowel (-ar → -e, -er/-ir → -a):

Verbyo formCommandBPOC phrase
firmar (sign)firmofirmeFirme aquí.
apagar (turn off)apagoapagueApague el motor.
salir (get out)salgosalgaSalga del vehículo.
mover (move)muevomuevaNo mueva la cabeza.
Pronoun placement: positive command, the pronoun attaches to the end and an accent keeps the stress: mueva + se → muévase. Negative command, the pronoun moves in front: ¡No se mueva! · Míreme (look at me) ↔ No me mire (don't look at me).

Idea 3

“Sit yourself” — why so many commands end in -se

English says “sit down.” Spanish says “seat yourself”: the verb is reflexive, and the usted “yourself” is se.

EnglishSpanishLiterally
Sit down.Siéntese.Seat yourself.
Get up.Levántese.Raise yourself.
Get on the ground.Acuéstese en el suelo.Lay yourself on the ground.
Turn around.Voltéese.Turn yourself.
Calm down. / Stay calm.Cálmese. / Quédese tranquilo.Calm yourself. / Stay yourself calm.
Get out of the car.Bájese del vehículo.Lower yourself from the vehicle.
Him vs. her: the danger words attach lo (him), la (her), or le (to him/her) instead: golpéalo = beat him, golpéala = beat her, quítale la pistola = take the gun from him/her. The ending tells you who the target is.

7

Nouns & adjectives

Every noun has a gender, and adjectives follow it

ARTICLES

el / la / los / las

Usually -o = masculine (el cuchillo), -a = feminine (la pistola), with exceptions: la mano, los pies. And el arma is feminine but takes el.

ADJECTIVES

After the noun, matching it

el carro rojo, la camisa roja, los zapatos negros. Also for the person: tranquilo / tranquila, arrestado / arrestada.

BODY PARTS

“The,” not “your”

No mueva la cabeza, Deme la otra mano, Cruce los pies. The verb already says whose.

“Of” replaces compound nouns: cinturón de seguridad (belt of safety), licencia de manejar (license of driving), lugar de nacimiento (place of birth). And Spanish has no ’s: ¿De quién es el carro? = “Of whom is the car?” = “Whose car is it?”

Quick checks

Why does ¿Entiende? mean “Do you understand?” with no word for “you”? Answer
The -e ending is the he/she/usted form, so “you” (usted) is built into the verb, and Spanish needs no “do” helper.
Make Siéntese negative. Answer
No se siente. The pronoun moves in front of the verb, and the accent drops because the stress is back in its normal place.
A suspect says ¡Golpéala! Who is the target? Answer
A woman: la = her. Golpéalo would be “beat him.”
Using the tow-truck pattern, say “Do you want me to call someone?” Answer
¿Quiere que llame a alguien? — plus the personal a, because alguien is a person.
Why is “You are under arrest” Está arrestado and not Es arrestado? Answer
Estar is for a condition someone is in; ser is for identity. Being under arrest is a condition.
What's wrong with the BPOC spelling sepáre las pies? Answer
Pie is masculine, so it's los pies, and separe needs no accent: Separe los pies.

BPOC 27.1 · 27.2 · 27.3

Critical Decision-Making Model — 5 Steps

  1. Collect information
  2. Assess situation, threats, and risks
  3. Consider police powers and agency policy
  4. Identify options and determine best course of action
  5. Act, review, and re-assess

After Step 5, loop back to Step 1 as the situation changes

Key principles — every step 27.1

  • Police ethics
  • The values of the police agency
  • Proportionality
  • The sanctity of human life

Why use it 27.3

  • Organizes the decision-making process
  • Helps officers make better decisions
  • Gives a structured, rational explanation of your actions afterward, which increases credibility
Source: BPOC Ch. 27 De-Escalation (PERF ICAT). Follow your instructors’ and agency’s guidance where it differs.

CDM Step 1 · BPOC 27.10 · 27.14 · 27.15 · 27.17

Collect Information

1Collect2Assess3Powers / policy4Options5Act / review

Ask

  • What kind of incident is this? Recognize its nature before you respond. 27.14
  • Is there an immediate threat? If not, take a tactical pause and slow the situation down. 27.15 · 27.17
  • What is the person thinking and feeling? 27.10

Do

  • Prepare yourself on the way and on arrival. 27.15
  • Listen 80%, talk 20%. Avoid distractions. 27.10
  • Watch body language, theirs and yours. 27.7
  • Communicate with the subject and the other officers and people involved. 27.14
LO 27.15: CDM in a critical incident starts with collecting information, the tactical pause, and preparing yourself.

CDM Step 2 · BPOC 27.5 · 27.8 · 27.17

Assess Situation, Threats, and Risks

1Collect2Assess3Powers / policy4Options5Act / review

Person in behavioral crisis? 27.5

  • Not responding to verbal commands
  • Lack of coherence
  • Agitated
  • Talking to themselves
  • Poor hygiene

Weigh

  • Emotional–rational scale: as emotions rise, rational thinking declines. Where is this person on it? 27.8
  • Close quarters + pressed for time = your options quickly dwindle. 27.17
  • Is there time to slow things down, or is immediate action required? 27.7 · 27.17
Re-assess constantly: threats and risks change as the incident develops (Step 5).

CDM Step 3 · BPOC 27.1 · 27.6

Consider Police Powers and Agency Policy

1Collect2Assess3Powers / policy4Options5Act / review

Ask

  • What legal authority do I have to act here?
  • What does agency policy require, allow, or limit?
  • Does my response fit the key principles: ethics, agency values, proportionality, sanctity of human life? 27.1

Person in crisis 27.6

  • Your mission is not to diagnose or treat/solve their issues.
  • Top priority: verbally defuse and stabilize the situation.
LO 27.2 names this step; the specific powers and policies come from law and your agency.
4

CDM Step 4 · BPOC 27.7 · 27.12 · 27.16 · 27.17

Identify Options & Determine Best Course of Action

1Collect2Assess3Powers / policy4Options5Act / review

Create options 27.17

  • Distance + cover = time. With all three, your options multiply.
  • Tactical repositioning: keep a position of advantage and a safety zone.
  • Request backup and specialized help. 27.7
  • Don’t rush, unless immediate action is required. 27.7

As a team 27.16

  • Everyone has a key role and knows it.
  • Only one officer communicates with the subject.
  • Others as they arrive: cover, less-lethal, perimeter.
Give them options tooOffering the person choices helps gain voluntary compliance. 27.12
Pick the option that best fits Steps 1–3, then act on it (Step 5).
5

CDM Step 5 · BPOC 27.6 · 27.7 · 27.14 · 27.18 · 27.19

Act, Review, and Re-Assess

1Collect2Assess3Powers / policy4Options5Act / review

During

  • Act on your chosen option using sound tactics. 27.14
  • Continually assess and re-assess; when anything changes, go back to Step 1. 27.7
  • Goal with a person in crisis: get them to where they can function and reason, so voluntary compliance can be achieved. 27.6

After: after-action review 27.18 · 27.19

  • Uncovers and documents strengths and weaknesses of the response.
  • Purpose: improve future performance; part of continuous learning.
  • AARs answer the questions in the CDM; the model you used to decide is the model you use to explain.
  • Include implicit bias in the review.
LO 27.19: the CDM guides thinking during the incident and examines decisions after it.
TALK

Every step · BPOC 27.7 · 27.8 · 27.10 · 27.11

Listening & Body Language

Used in Step 1 to collect information and in Step 5 to act and re-assess

Active listening 27.10

  • 80/20 rule: listen 80% of the time, talk 20%.
  • Goal: understand what the person is thinking and feeling.
  • Pay attention to words and gestures and body language.
  • Avoid distractions; show you are listening carefully.
  • Use silence to your advantage.

Non-verbal 27.11

  • Project the right body language.
  • Eye contact and open-handed gestures.
  • Modulate your tone of voice.
  • Watch body language, theirs and yours. 27.7
WhyAs emotions rise, rational thinking declines. Lowering emotions helps the person think and decide better. It won’t always work, but it’s always worth a try. 27.8
Focus on calming the situation and minimizing the stress level (27.7).
TALK

Every step · BPOC 27.7 · 27.12 · 27.13

Words That De-Escalate

Used in Step 1 to collect information and in Step 5 to act and re-assess

Verbal skills 27.12

  • Establish rapport; use the team concept.
  • Ask questions or make requests clearly, one at a time.
  • Ask open-ended “what” and “how” questions.
  • Encourage conversation, but don’t dominate it.
  • Provide options.
  • Emotional contagion: emotions spread, so your calm can spread too.

Say this, not that 27.13

“What’s your problem?”→“What can I do to help you?” “Calm down.”→“I see that you are upset. Please tell me about it.”

Always 27.7

  • Communicate, communicate, communicate.
  • Be respectful, never dismissive.
LO 27.13: practice alternatives to adversarial phrases for situations you will meet in policing.

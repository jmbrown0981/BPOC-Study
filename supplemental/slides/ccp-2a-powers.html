Peace Officers vs. Federal Special Investigators — Powers in Texas

← Hub

BPOC 9.2–9.3 — what a Texas peace officer can do under state law that a federal agent can't, and where the two overlap. Follows the Arts. 2A.001 & 2A.002 slides.

1

The framework

Three sources of arrest authority under Texas law

Peace officer

  • On the Art. 2A.001 list (or made one by other law)
  • Penal Code definition: PC 1.07(a)(36)
  • Has duties (Art. 2A.051) and the full set of powers the Code gives “peace officers”

Federal special investigator

  • Listed in Art. 2A.002 — expressly not a peace officer
  • Penal Code definition: PC 1.07(a)(46-b)
  • Has only the specific powers 2A.002 grants (usually felonies only, or only in certain places)

Any person

  • Citizen's arrest, Art. 14.01(a)
  • Only for a felony or an offense against the public peace committed in the person's presence or view
  • Applies to everyone, including federal agents and off-duty officers
Two separate legal systems. These slides cover Texas law only. A federal agent enforcing a federal crime acts under federal law, which 2A.002 neither grants nor limits.
STATUTE TEXT — PC 1.07(a)(36), (46-b); CCP 14.01(a)

PC 1.07(a)(36) "Peace officer" means a person elected, employed, or appointed as a peace officer under Article 2A.001, Code of Criminal Procedure, Section 51.212 or 51.214, Education Code, or other law.

PC 1.07(a)(46-b) "Federal special investigator" means a person described by Article 2A.002, Code of Criminal Procedure.

CCP Art. 14.01(a) A peace officer or any other person, may, without a warrant, arrest an offender when the offense is committed in his presence or within his view, if the offense is one classed as a felony or as an offense against the public peace.

2

“Shall” vs. “may”

Peace officers have duties; special investigators have powers

Art. 2A.051 — each peace officer SHALL:

  1. Preserve the peace in their jurisdiction by all lawful means
  2. Step in without a warrant to prevent or suppress crime, where the Code allows
  3. Execute all lawful process issued to them by a magistrate or court
  4. Notify a magistrate of offenses committed in their jurisdiction
  5. Arrest without a warrant when the law allows, so the offender can be brought before a magistrate or court
  6. Take possession of a child under Art. 63.00905(g)
  7. Execute a Civil Commitment Office emergency detention order on request

Art. 2A.002 — special investigators “HAVE THE POWERS”

The article grants powers of arrest, search, and seizure. It doesn't impose any of the 2A.051 duties. Texas law doesn't require a federal agent to keep the peace or serve state process.

Other mandatory duties (peace officers only)

  • Must arrest for a protective-order violation committed in their presence 14.03(b)
  • Must stay at the scene when needed to verify a protective-order or family-violence allegation 14.03(c)
STATUTE TEXT — CCP 2A.051; 14.03(b)–(c)

Art. 2A.051. GENERAL POWERS AND DUTIES OF PEACE OFFICERS. Each peace officer shall:

(1) preserve the peace within the officer's jurisdiction using all lawful means;

(2) in every case authorized by this code, interfere without a warrant to prevent or suppress crime;

(3) execute all lawful process issued to the officer by a magistrate or court;

(4) give notice to an appropriate magistrate of all offenses committed in the officer's jurisdiction, where the officer has good reason to believe there has been a violation of the penal law;

(5) when authorized by law, arrest an offender without a warrant so the offender may be taken before the proper magistrate or court and be tried;

(6) take possession of a child under Article 63.00905(g); and

(7) on a request made by the Texas Civil Commitment Office, execute an emergency detention order issued by that office under Section 841.0837, Health and Safety Code.

Art. 14.03(b) A peace officer shall arrest, without a warrant, a person the peace officer has probable cause to believe has committed an offense under Section 25.07, Penal Code, if the offense is committed in the presence of the peace officer.

Art. 14.03(c) If reasonably necessary to verify an allegation of a violation of a protective order or of the commission of an offense involving family violence, a peace officer shall remain at the scene of the investigation to verify the allegation and to prevent the further commission of the violation or of family violence.

CCP Chapters 15 & 18

Texas warrants are addressed to peace officers

Arrest warrants

  • Directed to “a peace officer or some other person specially named” 15.01
  • Warrants from a clerk or magistrate (other than a mayor) reach the whole state, and any peace officer holding one may execute it in any county 15.06

Search warrants

  • Issued by a magistrate and “directed to a peace officer” 18.01(a)
  • The peace officer who receives it must execute it without delay and return it 18.06(a)
Peace officerCan execute Texas arrest & search warrants anywhere the warrant reaches
vs
Federal agentOnly National Park Service officers are expressly given power to execute Texas warrants, and only inside a national park or recreation area 2A.002(d)(2)(A)
Federal agents get federal warrants from federal judges for federal crimes. When a Texas warrant is needed, a Texas peace officer is the one it's addressed to (unless a magistrate names someone else in an arrest warrant under 15.01).
STATUTE TEXT — CCP 15.01, 15.06, 18.01(a), 18.06(a), 2A.002(d)(2)

Art. 15.01. A "warrant of arrest" is a written order from a magistrate, directed to a peace officer or some other person specially named, commanding him to take the body of the person accused of an offense, to be dealt with according to law.

Art. 15.06. A warrant of arrest, issued by any county or district clerk, or by any magistrate (except mayors of an incorporated city or town), shall extend to any part of the State; and any peace officer to whom said warrant is directed, or into whose hands the same has been transferred, shall be authorized to execute the same in any county in this State.

Art. 18.01(a) A "search warrant" is a written order, issued by a magistrate and directed to a peace officer, commanding him to search for any property or thing and to seize the same and bring it before such magistrate …

Art. 18.06(a) A peace officer to whom a search warrant is delivered shall execute the warrant without delay and shall return the warrant to the proper magistrate.

Art. 2A.002(d)(2) [A commissioned law enforcement officer of the National Park Service has,] within the boundaries of a national park or national recreation area: (A) the power to execute any arrest warrant or search warrant issued under the laws of this state; and (B) the powers granted to a peace officer by Chapters 573 and 574, Health and Safety Code.

Art. 2A.002(b)–(g)

Where federal officers get broader Texas powers

Outside the felony-only baseline in 2A.002(a), the broader grants depend on where the officer is or what duty they're on.

(a) BASELINE · ANYWHERE

18 federal investigative agencies

Arrest, search & seizure for Texas felonies only.FBI, DEA, ATF, U.S. Marshals, Secret Service, ICE, IRS-CI, Postal Inspectors, and more
(g) ON PROTECTIVE DUTY

Secret Service

Adds Texas misdemeanors and any federal crime while protecting a protectee or investigating a threat.
(b) FEDERAL PROPERTY

DHS property-protection officers

Any Texas offense, for duty protecting federal property and the people on it.e.g. federal courthouses and office buildings
(d) NATIONAL PARKS & REC. AREAS

National Park Service

Any Texas offense committed in the park; execute Texas warrants; mental-health emergency detention powers.e.g. Big Bend National Park, Lake Meredith and Amistad National Recreation Areas
(e) NATIONAL FORESTS

U.S. Forest Service

Any Texas offense committed in the National Forest System.e.g. Sam Houston, Davy Crockett, Angelina, Sabine National Forests
(c) / (c-1) BORDER PORTS & CHECKPOINTS

CBP, Border Patrol, ICE officers

Detain only for intoxication offenses until handed to a peace officer; trained Border Patrol agents also get felonies, incident to a federal detainment.land ports of entry from Mexico; permanent checkpoints
(f) NUCLEAR PLANT SITES

Nuclear plant security

Arrest, search & seizure, including PC 9.51 force, on the plant site or in nearby areas covered by an agreement with local police.e.g. Comanche Peak (Glen Rose), South Texas Project
Even in these zones, the officer is still not a peace officer. They get only the listed powers, not the rest of the Code's peace-officer powers and duties.

STATUTE TEXT — PC 9.51(a)–(d), 38.03(a), 46.15(a)(1); CCP 14.03(h)(1)

PC 9.51(a) A peace officer, or a person acting in a peace officer's presence and at his direction, is justified in using force against another when and to the degree the actor reasonably believes the force is immediately necessary to make or assist in making an arrest or search, or to prevent or assist in preventing escape after arrest, if: (1) the actor reasonably believes the arrest or search is lawful or, if the arrest or search is made under a warrant, he reasonably believes the warrant is valid; and (2) before using force, the actor manifests his purpose to arrest or search and identifies himself as a peace officer or as one acting at a peace officer's direction, unless he reasonably believes his purpose and identity are already known by or cannot reasonably be made known to the person to be arrested.

(b) A person other than a peace officer (or one acting at his direction) is justified in using force against another when and to the degree the actor reasonably believes the force is immediately necessary to make or assist in making a lawful arrest, or to prevent or assist in preventing escape after lawful arrest if, before using force, the actor manifests his purpose to and the reason for the arrest or reasonably believes his purpose and the reason are already known by or cannot reasonably be made known to the person to be arrested.

(c) A peace officer is justified in using deadly force against another when and to the degree the peace officer reasonably believes the deadly force is immediately necessary to make an arrest, or to prevent escape after arrest, if the use of force would have been justified under Subsection (a) and: (1) the actor reasonably believes the conduct for which arrest is authorized included the use or attempted use of deadly force; or (2) the actor reasonably believes there is a substantial risk that the person to be arrested will cause death or serious bodily injury to the actor or another if the arrest is delayed.

(d) A person other than a peace officer acting in a peace officer's presence and at his direction is justified in using deadly force against another when and to the degree the person reasonably believes the deadly force is immediately necessary to make a lawful arrest, or to prevent escape after a lawful arrest, if the use of force would have been justified under Subsection (b) and: (1) the actor reasonably believes the felony or offense against the public peace for which arrest is authorized included the use or attempted use of deadly force; or (2) the actor reasonably believes there is a substantial risk that the person to be arrested will cause death or serious bodily injury to another if the arrest is delayed.

PC 38.03(a) A person commits an offense if he intentionally prevents or obstructs a person he knows is a peace officer or a person acting in a peace officer's presence and at his direction from effecting an arrest, search, or transportation of the actor or another by using force against the peace officer or another.

PC 46.15(a) Sections 46.02 and 46.03 do not apply to: (1) peace officers or special investigators under Article 2A.002, Code of Criminal Procedure, and neither section prohibits a peace officer or special investigator from carrying a weapon in this state, including in an establishment in this state serving the public, regardless of whether the peace officer or special investigator is engaged in the actual discharge of the officer's or investigator's duties while carrying the weapon; …

CCP 14.03(h)(1) A peace officer who is acting in the lawful discharge of the officer's official duties may disarm a person at any time the officer reasonably believes it is necessary for the protection of the person, officer, or another individual. …

FBI agent sees a $50 shoplift

Class C theft, so not a felony and not an offense against the public peace. No Texas arrest power. Call local police.

DEA agent sees an aggravated robbery

A felony committed in view. Can arrest under 2A.002(a), and as any person under 14.01(a).

Drunk driver at the Laredo port of entry

CBP officer has probable cause of DWI (PC 49.04). Detain only, then hand to a Texas peace officer without unnecessary delay.

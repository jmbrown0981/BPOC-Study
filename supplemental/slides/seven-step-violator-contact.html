The Seven Step Violator Contact

  1. 1

    Greeting & identify your agency Greet

    • Greet naturally: introduce yourself, or a simple “Good morning.” It is a courtesy owed to every driver.
    • Always name yourself and your agency, even in uniform and a marked car. Many drivers are from out of town and don’t know the uniform.
    • Business courtesy: put the driver at ease, no superiority or deference. Smile; normal tone.

    Say“Good morning. I’m Officer ____ with the ____ Police Department.”

  2. 2

    State the violation Violation

    • Tell the driver at once why they were stopped, and stress that it is serious.
    • Use statements, not questions; don’t ask whether they know what they did.
    • Exception, speeding: give them a chance to explain. Circumstances may justify the speed to a reasonable person; if none exist, the question puts them in the position of admitting it.

    Ask“Is there any reason for your excessive rate of speed?”

  3. 3

    Identify the driver; check driver & vehicle ID

    • Ask every violator for their driver’s license and compare the person to its description.
    • No license: ask for other ID, preferably with a description. None at all: write down age, height, weight, eyes, hair, identifying marks, and address.
    • License in a wallet or purse: have them take it out themselves, so no one can claim money or papers went missing.
    • Call the driver by name for the rest of the contact.
  4. 4

    State the action to be taken Action stated

    • Clear, firm, and calm, leaving no doubt about what will happen.
    • Say “you,” not “I.” “I am going to…” shifts the focus to the officer and lets the driver blame you instead of the violation.

    Say“You will be charged with the offense of speeding in the Justice of the Peace Court in Austin.”

    Say“You will be warned for the violation you have committed.”

  5. 5

    Take that action Act

    • Do what you said: write the citation, take the violator into custody, or give the warning by pointing out how serious the violation is and its possible consequences.
  6. 6

    Explain what the violator must do Explain

    • Exactly what they must do: get in the patrol car, follow the patrol car, appear at a certain court by a certain time before a certain magistrate, or not repeat the violation.
    • Short and clear, and make sure they understand. They don’t know the courts and locations the way you do.
    • A little extra time here means more on-time appearances and fewer warrants to serve.

    Say“You will be given ten (10) days in which to answer this charge.” (state your agency’s procedure)

  7. 7

    Leave Leave

    • As firm and impersonal as the approach. No gloating.
    • A brief parting comment, sincere but businesslike, is enough. It can leave a feeling of friendliness.
    • Tell the driver they are free to leave, then break contact and return to the patrol car right away.

“You,” not “I”

Keep the emphasis on the driver and the violation. “You will be charged…” avoids the unpleasantness that “I am going to…” invites.

Why it’s expected

TCOLE academies must teach this method. Under the Community Safety Education Act it is also taught in high schools, driver’s ed, and defensive driving, so drivers expect officers to use it.

Greet → Violation → ID → Action stated → Act → Explain → Leave

Source: BPOC Ch. 37 Civilian Interaction Training, LO 37.3 (summarized). Follow your instructors’ and agency’s procedures where they differ.

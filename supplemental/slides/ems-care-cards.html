40.8

BPOC 40.8 · Respiration / Breathing

Occlusive DressingPenetrating chest, abdomen, or back injury

Recognize

  • For use with penetrating injury to the chest, abdomen, and/or back.

Do

  • Do not use if major evisceration (intestines coming out of the wound).
  • Preferentially use a commercial vented chest seal.
  • Monitor for development of tension pneumothorax and “burp”/remove the seal if indicated.
!Rapidly refer to medical personnel, as this is a life-threatening event.
40.8

BPOC 40.8 · Respiration / Breathing

Tension Pneumothorax

Recognize

  • Injury to the chest/abdomen/back with severe difficulty breathing and signs of shock.

Do

  • Early recognition during assessment is the key.
!Rapidly refer to medical personnel, as this is a life-threatening event.
40.8

BPOC 40.8 · Respiration / Breathing

Allergic ReactionSevere = Anaphylaxis

Recognize

  • Often caused by ingestion/injection of an allergen (food, drink, bite, sting); can occur with or without a known history of allergic reaction.
  • Diffuse skin rash, hives, difficulty breathing, difficulty speaking, difficulty swallowing, whistling sound in throat, wheezing sound in lungs.

Do

  • If the casualty has a prescribed epinephrine device (nasal spray or auto-injector such as an EpiPen™), encourage self-administration.
!Rapidly refer to medical personnel, as this is a life-threatening event.
40.8

BPOC 40.8 · Respiration / Breathing

Asthma AttackSevere = Status Asthmaticus

Recognize

  • Often caused by inhaling an allergen (mold, pollen, smoke, pollutant) and/or intense physical exercise, usually with a known history of asthma.
  • Difficulty breathing, difficulty speaking, wheezing sound in lungs, tripod positioning, anxious appearance.

Do

  • If the casualty has a prescribed inhaler/nebulizer (Albuterol), encourage self-administration.
!Rapidly refer to medical personnel, as this is a life-threatening event.
40.8

BPOC 40.8 · Respiration / Breathing

Opioid OverdoseNarcotic overdose

Recognize

  • Often caused by inhalation/injection/ingestion of codeine, fentanyl, heroin, morphine, oxycodone, hydrocodone.
  • Altered level of consciousness, slow/shallow breathing, weak pulse, pale or blue skin, pinpoint pupils.

Do

  • Provide rescue breathing if indicated and able to do so.
  • Administer naloxone / Narcan™ if indicated, available, and able/authorized to do so.
40.8

BPOC 40.8 · Respiration / Breathing

Rescue Breathing

Do

  • Use/follow current American Heart Association or American Red Cross recommendations/guidelines, as part of the integrated course in CPR/AED.
  • Must include Adult, Child, and Infant.
40.9

BPOC 40.9 · Circulation

Heart AttackAcute Myocardial Infarction

Recognize

  • Generally caused by a blockage in an artery that feeds blood/oxygen to the heart muscle.
  • Chest pain/pressure, jaw pain, left arm pain, shortness of breath, indigestion, nausea, pale/sweaty skin.

Do

  • Do not allow the casualty to exert themselves.
  • Place the casualty in a position of comfort.
!Rapidly refer to medical personnel, as this is a life-threatening event.
40.9

BPOC 40.9 · Circulation

ShockPhysiological, not psychological

Recognize

  • Inadequate blood flow to the body tissues and organs: altered level of consciousness, pale/cool skin, rapid/weak/absent pulse.
  • Many causes: hemorrhage/bleeding, tension pneumothorax, heat/dehydration, severe infection, severe allergic reaction, etc.

Do

  • Place the casualty in the recovery position.
  • Try to obtain/maintain normal body temperature.
!Rapidly refer to medical personnel, as this is a life-threatening event.
40.10

BPOC 40.10 · Environmental

Cold EmergencyHypothermia

Do

  • Move the victim to a warm place.
  • Remove wet clothing and dry the victim.
  • Warm with blankets and/or heat packs to the neck, armpits, groin. Do not apply heat packs directly to skin; use a barrier such as a cloth.
  • Keep all trauma patients warm.
40.10

BPOC 40.10 · Environmental

Heat EmergencyHyperthermia

Do

  • Get the victim out of the heat.
  • Loosen tight clothing.
  • Cool with cold wet clothes on skin and/or ice packs to the neck, armpits, groin.
  • If the casualty is conscious, give them water to drink.
40.11

BPOC 40.11 · Burns

Thermal Burns

Recognize

  • Generally caused by a heat source: sun, fire/flame, steam, stove, boiling water.
  • Red painful skin, blisters, black charred skin, white waxy skin.

Do

  • Stop the burning process and remove the casualty from the source; do not expose yourself.
  • Remove constricting clothing/jewelry and cover burns with dry dressings.
!Rapidly refer to medical personnel, as this can be a life-threatening event.
40.11

BPOC 40.11 · Burns

Chemical Burns

Recognize

  • Generally caused by a substance: bleach, acid, drain cleaner, paint thinner.
  • Red painful skin, blisters, skin peeling, swelling.

Do

  • Stop the burning process and remove the casualty from the source; do not expose yourself.
  • Consider beginning decontamination with copious amounts of water, if safe to do so.
!Rapidly refer to medical personnel, as this can be a life-threatening event.
40.11

BPOC 40.11 · Burns

Electrical Burns

Recognize

  • Generally caused by direct exposure to a car battery, wall outlet, exposed wire, lightning.
  • Altered level of consciousness, wounds at entry and exit, red painful skin, blisters, swelling, weakness, abnormal heartbeat, cardiac arrest.

Do

  • Stop the burning process and remove the casualty from the source; do not expose yourself.
  • Begin CPR/AED if no pulse.
  • Remove constricting clothing and jewelry and cover burns with dry dressings.
!Rapidly refer to medical personnel, as this can be a life-threatening event.
40.12

BPOC 40.12 · Medical

Diabetic EmergenciesBlood sugar too low or too high

Too LOW — Hypoglycemia

“Cool and Clammy = Give ’em Candy”

Recognize

  • Altered mental status
  • Symptoms like alcohol intoxication
  • Irritability
  • Pale / cool / clammy skin

Do

  • Maintain airway.
  • If able to eat/drink, feed sugary food/drink and/or carbs.

Too HIGH — Hyperglycemia

“Hot and Dry = Sugar High”

Recognize

  • Altered mental status
  • Deep / fast breathing
  • Fruity / acetone breath
  • Extreme thirst
  • Headache
  • Warm skin

Do

  • Maintain airway.
  • If able to drink, give large amounts of water.
  • Avoid sugar/carbs.
!Rapidly refer to medical personnel, as this can be a life-threatening event.
40.12

BPOC 40.12 · Medical

Seizure

Recognize

  • Can have many causes.
  • Altered mental status, full-body general seizure activity, isolated extremity seizure activity, and/or seizure activity isolated to rapid eye jerking.

Do

  • Place the casualty in the recovery position with a pillow under the head.
  • Do not attempt to “hold down” or restrict movement.
  • If seizure activity is due to heat stroke, attempt to cool the patient.
!Rapidly refer to medical personnel, as this can be a life-threatening event.
40.12

BPOC 40.12 · Medical

Stroke

Recognize

  • Generally caused by a blockage or tear of a blood vessel in the brain.
  • Altered level of consciousness, headache, facial droop, one-sided body weakness, slurring of speech, unequal pupils, confusion.

Do

  • Maintain airway and continuously reassess the casualty.
  • Keep head/shoulders elevated ~30°, if able.
!Rapidly refer to medical personnel, as this can be a life-threatening event.
40.13

BPOC 40.13 · Injuries

Bandaging & SplintingBroken bones and severe sprains (arms, hands, legs, feet)

Recognize

  • Pain, weakness, swelling, bruising, bleeding, inability to move the extremity, closed fracture, open fracture.

Do

  • Immobilize the affected arm or leg above and below the site of injury.
  • Preferentially use a commercial splint and/or sling; common items also work: defensive baton, large flashlight, long board, rolled magazines, belt.
  • Re-assess circulation in the affected arm or leg after immobilization.
!If no pulse in the affected arm or leg, rapidly refer to medical personnel.
40.14

BPOC 40.14 · Childbirth

Childbirth — Normal / Routine

Recognize

  • “Water broke,” contractions close in interval, urge to push, infant’s head crowning (visible from the outside).

Do

  • Place mother in a slightly seated position on her back, knees pulled toward chest.
  • Simply assist the natural delivery; do not pull the baby.
  • Support the baby’s head as it delivers.
  • Guide delivery of the upper shoulder, then the lower shoulder.
  • Support the body and legs as they deliver; keep baby level with mother.
  • Warm / dry / stimulate baby until awake, color changing, and likely crying.
  • Baby on mother’s chest, skin contact, encourage breastfeeding, keep warm.
  • If baby does not appear awake or breathing, consider CPR.
40.14

BPOC 40.14 · Childbirth

Childbirth — Abnormal

Recognize

  • Severe sharp/tearing pain, bleeding from the birth canal, presentation of an arm, leg, or buttocks (visible from the outside).

Do

  • Do not encourage delivery.
!Rapidly refer to medical personnel, as this can be a life-threatening event.

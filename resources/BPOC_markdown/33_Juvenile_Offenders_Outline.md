## Purpose and Interpretation (Family Code 51.01)
 - to provide for the protection of the public and public safety
 - consistent with the protection of the public and public safety promote the concept of punishment for criminal acts
 - consistent with the protection of the public and public safety remove, where appropriate, the taint of criminality from children committing certain unlawful acts
 - consistent with the protection of the public and public safety provide treatment, training, and rehabilitation that emphasizes the accountability and responsibility of both the parent and the child for the child's conduct
 - to provide for the care, the protection, and the wholesome moral, mental, and physical development of children coming within its provisions
 - to protect the welfare of the community and to control the commission of unlawful acts by children
 - to achieve the foregoing purposes in a family environment whenever possible, separating the child from the child's parents only when necessary for the child's welfare or in the interest of public safety and when a child is removed from the child's family, to give the child the care that should be provided by parents
 - to provide a simple judicial procedure through which the provisions of this title are executed and enforced and in which the parties are assured a fair hearing and their constitutional and other legal rights recognized and enforced

## Definitions
 - Child - a person who is ten years of age or older and under 17 years of age or seventeen years of age or older and under 18 years of age who is alleged or found to have engaged in delinquent conduct or conduct indicating a need for supervision as a result of acts committed before becoming 17 years of age.
 - Custodian - the adult with whom the child resides
 - Guardian - the person who, under court order, is the guardian of the person of the child or the public or private agency with whom the child has been placed by a court
 - Judge or Juvenile Court Judge - the judge of a juvenile court
 - Juvenile Court - a court designated under Family Code Section 51.04 (Jurisdiction) of this code to exercise jurisdiction over proceedings under this title
 - Non-offender - a child who is subject to jurisdiction of a court under abuse, dependency, or neglect statutes under Title 5  for reasons other than legally prohibited conduct of the child or has been taken into custody and is being held solely for deportation out of the United States.
 - Parent - means the mother or the father of a child, but does not include a parent whose parental rights have been terminated
 - Referral to Juvenile Court - the referral of a child or a child's case to the office or official, including an intake officer or probation officer, designated by the juvenile board to process children within the juvenile justice system
 - Secure Correctional Facility - any public or private residential facility, including an alcohol or other drug treatment facility, that includes construction fixtures designed to physically restrict the movements and activities of juveniles or other individuals held in lawful custody in the facility and is used for the placement of any juvenile who has been adjudicated as having committed an offense, any nonoffender, or any other individual convicted of a criminal offense.
 - Secure Detention Facility - means any public or private residential facility that includes construction fixtures designed to physically restrict the movements and activities of juveniles or other individuals held in lawful custody in the facility and is used for the temporary placement of any juvenile who is accused of having committed an offense, any nonoffender, or any other individual accused of having committed a criminal offense.
 - Status Offender - a child who is accused, adjudicated, or convicted for conduct that would not, under state law, be a crime if committed by an adult, including running away from home under Section 51.03(b), a fineable only offense under Section 51.03(b)(1) transferred to the juvenile court under Section 51.08(b), but only if the conduct constituting the offense would not have been criminal if engaged in by an adult, a violation of standards of student conduct as described by Section 51.03(b)(4), a violation of a provision of the Alcoholic Beverage Code applicable to minors only or a violation of any other fineable only offense under Section 8.07(a)(4) or (5), Penal Code, but only if the conduct constituting the offense would not have been criminal if engaged in by an adult.
 - Traffic Offense - a violation of a penal statute cognizable under Chapter 729, Transportation Code, except for conduct for which the person convicted may be sentenced to imprisonment or confinement in jail or a violation of a motor vehicle traffic ordinance of an incorporated city or town in this state.
 - Valid Court Order - a court order entered under Section 54.04 concerning a child adjudicated to have engaged in conduct indicating a need for supervision as a status offender.
 - Delinquent conduct - conduct, other than a traffic offense, that violates a penal law of this state or of the United States punishable by imprisonment or by confinement in jail; conduct that violates a lawful order of a court under circumstances that would constitute contempt of that court in a justice or municipal court, a county court for conduct punishable only by a fine, or a truancy court; conduct that violates Section 49.04 (DWI), 49.05 (FWI), 49.06 (BWI), 49.07 (Intoxication Assault), or 49.08 (Intoxication Manslaughter), Penal Code; or conduct that violates Section 106.041, Alcoholic Beverage Code, relating to driving under the influence of alcohol by a minor
 - Conduct indicating a need for supervision - conduct, other than a traffic offense, that violates the penal laws of this state of the grade of misdemeanor that are punishable by fine only or the penal ordinances of any political subdivision of this state unless the child has not been referred to the juvenile court; the voluntary absence of a child from the child's home without the consent of the child's parent or guardian for a substantial length of time or without intent to return (runaway); conduct prohibited by city ordinance or by state law involving the inhalation of the fumes or vapors of paint and other protective coatings or glue and other adhesives and the volatile chemicals (huffing); an act that violates a school district's previously communicated written standards of student conduct for which the child has been expelled; conduct described by Section 43.02 (Prostitution) or 43.021 (Solicitation of Prostitution) Penal Code, or conduct that violates section 43.261 (Electronic Transmission of Certain Visual Material Depicting Minor) Penal Code, or conduct that violates section 42.0601 (False Report to Induce Emergency Response) Penal Code if the child has not previously been adjudicated as having engaged in conduct violating that section.
 - Dual-system child - A child, who at any time before the child's 18th birthday, was referred to the juvenile justice system and was involved in the child welfare system by being placed in the temporary or permanent managing conservatorship of the Department of Family and Protective Services (DFPS), the subject of a family-based safety services case with the Department of Family and Protective Services (DFPS), an alleged victim of abuse or neglect in an active case being investigated by the Department of Family and Protective Services (DFPS), or a victim in a case in which the Department of Family and Protective Services (DFPS) investigation concluded to believe that abuse or neglect occurred.
 - Dual-status child - A dual-system child who is involved with both the child welfare and juvenile justice systems at the same time
 - ad litem - "ad litem" is a Latin phrase meaning "for the suit."
 - Adduce - to bring forward proof, evidence, or reasons to support an argument or claim

## Adult Case Terminology vs. Juvenile Case Terminology
 - Criminal Offense (Adult) is closest to Delinquent Conduct (Juvenile)
 - Code of Criminal Procedure is to adults what the Family Code is to juveniles
 - Arrest Warrant (Adult) is closest to Directive to Apprehend (Juvenile)
 - Indictment (Adult) is closest to Juvenile Petition
 - Adults are defendants; juveniles are respondents
 - Conviction (Adult) is closest to Adjudication (Juvenile)
 - Sentencing Hearing (Adult) is closest to Disposition Hearing (Juvenile)
 - Adults will plea guilty/not guilty; juveniles will plea true/not true
 - County Jail (Adult) is similar to Juvenile Processing Center (Juvenile)
 - Arraignment (Adult) is similar to Detention (not Disposition) Hearing (Juvenile)
 - Motion to Revoke Probation (Adult) is similar to Motion to Modify Disposition (Juvenile)

## Jurisdiction 
- Jurisdiction - Each county has a juvenile board. That board SHALL designate 1 or more district, criminal district, domestic relations, juvenile, county courts, county courts at law as the juvenile court.
- Jurisdiction mandates
  - Who handles juveniles can be changed by authorized boards or judges
  - There must be a juvenile court designated for each county at all times
  - If a judge or alternate juvenile court is not available, any magistrate MAY make detention release determination or conduct detention hearings
  - There can also be a referee to conduct detention release / hearings. This person SHALL be a licensed attorney in Texas.

## Waiver of Rights (Family Code Chapter 51.09)
- Any right granted to a child by Title 3 of the constitution or the laws of Texas or U.S. MAY be waived if
  - A waiver is made by the child and the attorney of the child
  - The child and attorney waiving the right are informed of and understand the right and possible consequence of waiving it
  - it is voluntary
  - The waiver is in writing or in court proceedings that are recorded

## Admissibility of a statement of a child (Family Code Chapter 51.095)
- Written statements
  - Written statement was given after being given magistrate warnings (Miranda rights for juveniles) by a magistrate
  - Written statement was signed in the presence of a magistrate with NO law enforcement officer present
  - Magistrate is fully convinced the child understands the nature and contents, and is signing voluntarily
  - The child knowingly, intelligently, and voluntarily waives the rights before and during the making of the statement and signs in the presence of the magistrate
  - Magistrate certifies that the magistrate has examined the child independently from law enforcement or prosecutor and determined the child understands the nature of the statement.
- Oral statements
  - Statement is of facts or circumstances that are found to be true and tend to establish the child's guilt, such as finding stolen property or the instrument with which the offense was committed
  - Res Gestae (excited utterances) statement of the delinquent conduct or conduct indicating a need for supervision
  - Statement is made in open court at child's adjudication hearing or before a grand jury considering a petition in a preliminary hearing
- Audio/Video statements
  - Magistrate warnings MUST be a part of the recording and the child must waive each right stated in the recording
  - Each voice/person must be identified
  - Not later than the 20th day of the proceeding, the defense attorney is entitled to a copy of the recording
- All statements
  - If the child is not in custody and not being interrogated, "voluntariness" is evaluated. Courts will look at the totality of circumstances including child's age, maturity, experience, background, intelligence, and education level
  - If the child is not in custody, an officer can interview and take statements without a magistrate, similar to a consensual encounter involving an adult
  - A child is NOT in custody when the child is notified that they are not in custody, the child is notified that they are free to leave at any time, AND the child is actually allowed to leave after the taking of the statement
- Important considerations for juvenile statements
  - If a crime occurs after 17 years of age, all rules/proceedings will follow ADULT rules
  - If the crime occurred while the person was under 17 but they are now older than 18, ADULT rules apply
  - If age of the suspect cannot be determined and it is possible that the suspect is a juvenile, it is safer to conduct any interrogation as though they are a juvenile. Procedures concerning juveniles will satisfy necessary procedures for an adult, but not vice-versa
  - Recorded statements must be one continuous statement; the recording device cannot be paused or stopped for any duration of the statement
  - Audio / video will allow the defense to hear and deploy their own interpreter if languages other than English are involved

## Statements that MUST be recorded
- Statements must be recorded if they involve these case types
  - Capital Murder
  - Murder
  - Kidnapping
  - Aggravated Kidnapping
  - Trafficking
  - Continuous Trafficking
  - Sexual Assault
  - Aggravated Sexual Assault
  - Continuous Sexual Assault of a Child
  - Sexual Performance of a Child

## Family Code Sec. 51.11
- Guardian Ad Litem
  - If a child appears before the juvenile court without a parent or guardian, the court shall appoint a guardian ad litem to protect the interests of the child.
    - The juvenile court need not a guardian ad litem if a parent or guardian appears with the child.
  - In any case in which it appears that the child's parent or guardian is unable or unwilling to make decisions in the best interest of the child with respect to proceedings under this title, the court MAY appoint a guardian ad litem to protect the interests of the child in the proceedings.
  - An attorney for a child MAY also be his guardian ad litem
  - A law-enforcement officer, probation officer, or other employee of the juvenile court MAY NOT be appointed guardian ad litem
  - The juvenile court MAY appoint the guardian ad litem appointed under Chapter 107 (Special Appointments, Child Custody Evaluations, and Adoption Evaluations) for a child in a suit affecting parent-child relationship filed by the Department of Family and Protective Services (DFPS) to serve as the guardian ad litem for the child in a proceeding held under this title
  - A non-attorney guardian ad litem in a case involving a dual-system child MAY NOT investigate any charges involving a dual-status child that are pending with the juvenile court or offer testimony concerning the guilt or innocence of a dual-status child

## Family Code Sec. 51.13
- Effect of Adjudication or Disposition
  - An order of adjudication disposition is a proceeding under this title is not a conviction of a crime, unless specified otherwise in this list
  - Except as provided by Chapter 841, Health and Safety Code (Civil Commitment of Sexually Violent Predators), an order of adjudication or disposition does not impose any civil disability ordinarily resulting from a conviction or operate to disqualify the child in any civil service application or appointment
  - The adjudication or disposition of a child or evidence adduced in a hearing under this title may be used only in subsequent proceedings under this title in which the child is a party; sentencing proceedings in a criminal court against the child to the extent permitted by the Texas Code of Criminal Procedure, 1965; or civil commitment proceedings under Chapter 841, Health and Saftey Code (Civil Commitment of Sexually Violent Predators)
  - A child may not be committed or transferred to a penal institution or other facility used primarily for the execution of sentences of persons convicted of crime, except for temporary detention in a jail or lockup pending juvenile court hearing or disposition under conditions meeting the requirements of Section 51.12 (Place and Conditions of Detention) (juvenile processing office, place of nonsecure custody, etc.); after transfer for prosecution in a criminal court under Section 54.02(h) (Waiver of Jurisdiction and Discretionary Transfer to Criminal Court, (h) deals specifically with the transfer of a juvenile to criminal court jurisdiction, thus trying the juvenile as an adult); after transfer from the Texas Juvenile Justice Department (TJJD) under section 245.151(c), Human Resources Code (Termination of Control, section (c) specifically dealing with transfers to the Texas Department of Criminal Justice TDCJ); or after transfer from a post-adjudication secure correctional facility, as that term is defined under Section 54.04011 (Trafficked Persons Program)
  - An adjudication under Section 54.03 (Adjudication Hearing) that constitutes a felony offense resulting in commitment to the Texas Juvenile Justice Department (TJJD) if the child engaged in delinquent conduct equating to a felony and MAY commit the child to the Texas Juvenile Justice Department, whether the petition was approved 54.04(d)(3) or not 54.04(d)(2); the child engaged in habitual felony conduct 54.04(m); or if the child after a hearing to modify disposition finds by a preponderance of the evidence that the child violated a reasonable and lawful order of the court 54.05(f) or commitment to a post-adjucation secure correctional facility is a final felony convition only for the purposes of Penal Code sections 12.42(a), (b), or (c)(1) (previous convictions resulting in felony grades incrementing, e.g. a felony 3 becoming a felony 2 and a felony 1 having an increased minimum sentence) and 12.425 (two state jail felony convictions equals a felony 3).

## Very, very brief note on Chapter 841, Health and Saftey Code (Civil Commitment of Sexually Violent Predators)
- This chapter covers everything from defining a sexually violent predator to how the state must manage diagnoisis and commitment of the predator. The commitment can include one in which the predator lives outside of a secure correctional facility and has myriad restrictions, check-ins, and duties in order to remain in this probation-like status.

## Family Code Sec. 51.12 
- Place and Conditions of Detention
  - Child may be detained ONLY in a
    - juvenile processing office - Office or room, which may be located in a police facility or sheriff's office. May not be a cell or holding facility used for detentions other than juvenile processing office detentions
    - place of nonsecure custody - An unlocked, multipurpose area such as a lobby, office, or interrogation room if not part of a secure detention area
    - certified juvenile detention facility (CJDF) - If in the same building as a jail, lockup, or other secure confinement, the CJDF SHALL be separated by sight and sound from adults detained in the same building
    - secure detention facility
    - county jail or other facility
      - Only when facilities previously mentioned in this list are not available in the county in which the child was taken into custody or an adjoining county. 
      - Facility must also separate the child by sight and sound from adults detained in the same building. 
      - The child may not interact with management or direct-care staff of the adult facility. 
      - The facility must also be designated as the juvenile detention facility by the county juvenile board, and they must, along with each judge of the juvenile court, inspect the facility at least annually and certify it in writing to the TJJD.
    - nonsecure correctional facility
      - Only when facilities previously mentioned in this list are not available in the county in which the child was taken into custody or an adjoining county AND a secure detention facility is not available in the county in which the juvenile was taken into custody. 
      - Facility must be appropriately registered and certified. 
      - Facility must comply with standards adopted by the TJJD
      - Must be designated by the county juvenile board for the county in which the facility is located
  - The juvenile board SHALL control the conditions and terms of detention and detention supervision and SHALL permit visitation with the child at reasonable times.
  - Pre-adjudication secure detention facilities may only be operated by a government unit or a private entity under contract with a government unit.
  - Each judge of the juvenile court and members of the juvenile board SHALL personally inspect all public or private juvenile pre-adjudication detention facilities at least annually, and SHALL certify in writing to operational/funding sources whether the facilities are suitable or unsuitable for the detention of children. Considerations include:
    - Current monitoring and inspection reports and any non-compliant citation reports
    - Current governmental inspector certification regarding compliance with local fire codes
    - Current building inspector certification regarding compliance with local building codes
    - for the 12-month period preceeding the inspection, total number of allegations of abuse, neglect, or exploitation and a summary of the findings of each allegation
  - The TJJD SHALL inspect each public or private juvenile pre-adjudication SECURE detention facilities and provide a report to each juvenile court judge indicating whether the facility is suitable or unsuitable for the detention of children based on adult/juvenile sight/sound separation requirements outlined elsewhere in this section and standards promulgated by the American Correctional Association. 

## Family Code Sec. 52.025
- Designation of Juvenile Processing Office
  - Office or room, which may be located in a police facility or sheriff's office
  - May not be a cell or holding facility used for detentions other than juvenile processing office detentions
  - A child may be detained in a juvenile processing office only for
    - The release of the child to a parent, guardian, custodian of the child, or other responsible adult upon that person's promise to bring the child before the juvenile court as requested by the court
    - the completion of essential forms and records
    - Photographing and fingerprinting if authorized by this title
    - Issuance of warnings to the child as required or permitted by this title
    - Receipt of a statement from the child
  - A child MAY NOT be left unattended in a juvenile processing office
  - A child is entitled to be accompanied by the child's parent, guardian, other custodian, or the child's attorney
  - A child MAY NOT be detained in a juvenile processing office for longer than SIX hours.

## Fanily Code Sec. 52.026
- Responsibility for Transporting Juvenile Offenders
  - It SHALL be the duty of the law enforcement officer to transport the child to the appropriate detention facility or to the school campus if the child is not released to a parent, guardian, or custodian.
  - If the juvenile detention facility is located outside the county in which the child is taken into custody, it SHALL be the duty of the law enforcement officer that took the child into custody, or, if authorized by the commissioners court of the county, the duty of the sheriff of the county to transport the child to the appropriate juvenile detention facility unless the child is detained in a secure detention facility or released to the parent, guardian, or custodian of the child.
  - On adoption of an order by the juvenile board and approval of the juvenile board's order by record vote of the commissioners court, it SHALL be the duty of the sheriff of the county in which the child is taken into custody to transport the child to and from all scheduled juvenile court proceedings and appearances and other activities ordered by the juvenile court.

## Family Code Sec. 58.001
- Law Enforcement Collection and Transmittal of Records of Children
  - Law enforcement officers and other juvenile justice personnel SHALL collect information described by section 58.104 (Types of Information Collected) as a part of the juvenile justice information system.
  - Law enforcement agency SHALL forward information, including fingerprints, relating to a child who has been taken into custody to the Department of Public Safety (DPS) only if the child is referred to the juvenile court on or before the 10th day after the child has been taken into custody.

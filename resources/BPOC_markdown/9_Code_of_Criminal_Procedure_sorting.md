- Objects of this code (CCP 1.03)
  - To adopt measures for preventing the commission of crime
  - To exclude the offender from all hope of escape
  - To insure a trial with as little delay as is consistent with the ends of justice
  - To bring the investigation of each offense on the trial with all the evidence tending to produce conviction or acquittal
  - To insure a fair and impartial trial
  - The certain executio of the sentence of the law when declared

- Powers and Characteristics of Special Investigators (CCP 2A.002)
  - are not peace officers
  - have the powers of arrest, search, and seizure under the laws of this state as to felony offenses only
  - An officer or agent designated by the Secretary of Homeland Security is not a peace officer but has the powers of arrest, search, and seizure as to any offense under the laws of this state
  - A customs and border protection officer or border patrol agent of United States Customs and Border Protection or an immigration enforcement agent or deportation officer of the Department of Homeland Security is not a peace officer under the laws of this state but, on the premises of a port facility designated by the commissioner of United States Customs and Border Protection as a port of entry for arrival in the United States by land transportation from the United Mexican States into this state or at a permanent established border patrol traffic checkpoint, has the authority to detain a person pending transfer without unnecessary delay to a peace officer if the agent or officer has probable cause to believe that the person has engaged in conduct that is a violation of Section 49.02, 49.04, 49.07, or 49.08, Penal Code, regardless of whether the violation may be disposed of in a criminal proceeding or a juvenile justice proceeding

- Powers and Characteristics of customs and border protection officers or border patrol agents (CCP 2A.002)
  - not a peace officer under the laws of this state
  - on the premises of a port facility designated by the commissioner of United States Customs and Border Protection as a port of entry for arrival in the United States by land transportation from the United Mexican States into this state or at a permanent established border patrol traffic checkpoint, has the authority to detain a person pending transfer without unnecessary delay to a peace officer if the agent or officer has probable cause to believe that the person has engaged in conduct that is a violation of Section 49.02, 49.04, 49.07, or 49.08, Penal Code, regardless of whether the violation may be disposed of in a criminal proceeding or a juvenile justice proceeding
  - if completed the training program described by Section 411.02093, Government Code, has the powers of arrest and search and seizure as to any felony offense under the laws of this state if the arrest, search, or seizure occurs on the premises of a port facility designated by the commissioner of the United States Customs and Border Protection as a port of entry or at a border patrol traffic checkpoint and is incident to a detainment under federal law

- Powers and Characteristics of a commissioned law enforcement officer of the National Park Service (CCP 2A.002)
  - not a peace officer under the laws of this state
  - has the powers of arrest, search, and seizure as to any offense under the laws of this state committed in a national park or national recreation area
  - within the boundaries of a national park or national recreation area, has the power to execute any arrest warrant or search warrant issued under the laws of this state and the powers granted to a peace officer by Chapters 573 (Emergency Detention) and 574 (Court-Ordered Mental Health Services), Health and Safety Code

- Powers and Characteristics of a special agent or law enforcement officer of the United States Forest Service (CCP 2A.002)
  - not a peace officer under the laws of this state
  - has the powers of arrest, search, and seizure as to any offense under the laws of this state committed in the National Forest System

- Powers and Characteristics of a special agent of the Secret Service protecting or investigating a threat against a person (CCP 2A.002)
  - not a peace officer
  - has the powers of arrest, search, and seizure under the laws of this state as to felony offenses
  - has the powers of arrest, search, and seizure as to a misdemeanor offense under the laws of this state
  - has the powers of arrest, search, and seizure as to any criminal offense under federal law

- Peace officers commissioned by tribal council (CCP 2A.003)
  - Alabama-Coushatta Tribe of Texas, the Kickapoo Traditional Tribe of Texas, or the Ysleta del Sur Pueblo
  - has all the powers, privileges, and immunities of a peace officer
  - may within the tribe's reservation arrest without a warrant in accordance with Chapter 14 any person who violates a law of the state
  - may within the tribe's reservation enforce all traffic laws on streets and highways
  - may outside the tribe's reservation, arrest any person who violates any law of the state if the officer is summoned by another law enforcement agency to provide assistance
  - may outside the tribe's reservation, arrest any person who violates any law of the state if the officer is assisting another law enforcement agency
  - not entitled to state benefits normally provided by the state to a peace officer
  - A peace officer commissioned under this article must meet the minimum standards required of peace officers by the Texas Commission on Law Enforcement relating to competence, reliability, education, training, morality, and physical and mental health
  - A peace officer commissioned under this article must meet all standards for licensing as a peace officer by the Texas Commission on Law Enforcement
  - A peace officer commissioned under this article and assigned to duty shall take and file the oath required of a peace officer
  - A peace officer commissioned under this article and assigned to duty shall execute and file a good and sufficient bond in the sum of $1,000, payable to the governor, with two or more good and sufficient sureties, conditioned that the officer will fairly, impartially, and faithfully perform the officer's duties as may be required by law. This bond may be sued on in the name of the person injured until the whole amount is recovered.

- Peace officers from adjoining states (CCP 2A.004)
  - while the officer is in this state, has the same powers, duties, and immunities as a peace officer of this state who is acting in the discharge of an official duty under certain conditions
  - must be in a municipality some part of the municipal limits of which are within one mile of the boundary between this state and the adjoining state
  - must be regularly assigned to duty in a county, parish, or municipality that adjoins this state
  - a condition when the peace officer has the same powers, duties, and immunities as a peace officer of this state is when transporting the inmate or defendant from a county in the adjoining state that is on the border between the two states to a hospital or other medical facility in a county in this state that is on the border between the two states
  - a condition when the peace officer has the same powers, duties, and immunities as a peace officer of this state is when returning the inmate or defendant from a hospital or facility to the county in the adjoining state from which the inmate or defendant was transported
  - a peace officer from an adjoing state may only exercise powers to the extent to maintain physical custody of an inmate or criminal defendant while transporting the inmate or defendant or regain physical custody of an inmate or criminal defendant if the inmate or defendant escapes while being transported
  - a peace officer from an adjoing state may also enforce the ordinances of a municipality in this state described by that subdivision if the governing body of the municipality authorizes that enforcement by majority vote at an open meeting

- Railroad peace officers (CCP 2A.005)
  - not more than 250 railroad peace officers may be employed by a railroad company
  - a railroad peace officer may make arrests and exercise all authority given peace officers under this code when necessary to prevent or abate the commission of an offense involving injury to passengers or employees of the railroad, or damage to railroad property
  - may protect railroad property or property in the custody or control of the railroad
  - may not issue a traffic citation for a violation of Chapter 521, Transportation Code, or Subtitle C, Title 7, Transportation Code
  - not entitled to state benefits normally provided by the state to a peace officer
  - Texas Railroad Association must submit the person's application for appointment
  - director of the department must issue the person a certificate of authority to act as a railroad peace officer
  - the person must meet minimum standards required of peace officers by the commission relating to competence, reliability, education, training, morality, and physical and mental health
  - the person must meet all standards for licensing as a peace officer by the commission
  - License can be revoked by the Texas Commission on Law Enforcement for good cause
  - railroad company is liable for any act or omission by a person serving as a railroad peace officer for the company that occurs within the scope of the person's employment; the state is NOT liable.
  - A railroad company that employs a railroad peace officer shall pay all expenses associated with granting or revoking the certificate of authority to act as a railroad peace officer
  - A railroad peace officer who is a member of a railroad craft may not perform the duties of a member of any other railroad craft during a strike or labor dispute

§46.02 UNLAWFUL CARRYING WEAPONS ▾

§46.02 – UNLAWFUL CARRYING WEAPONS

Carrying a handgun (or location-restricted knife) when you're not allowed to — based on age, criminal history, or circumstances like being intoxicated.

Carrying a handgun off your property while under 21 or with recent assault conviction (§a)

  • Class A Misdemeanor — base: intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly carrying a handgun on or about your person while under 21, or within 5 years of a family violence/assault/disorderly firearm conviction, and not on your own premises or inside/en route to your own vehicle.

Carrying a handgun in plain view or while committing a crime in your vehicle (§a-1)

  • Class A Misdemeanor — carrying a handgun in your own vehicle while it's in plain view (unless you're 21+ or LTC holder with the gun holstered); or while engaged in criminal activity (not just a traffic offense); or while legally prohibited from possessing a firearm.

Location-restricted knife (§a-4)

  • Class C Misdemeanor — a person under 18 carrying a location-restricted knife (blade over 5.5 inches) not on their own premises, vehicle, or under direct parental supervision.

Displaying handgun in plain view in public (§a-5)

  • Class A Misdemeanor — intentionally displaying a handgun in plain view in a public place. (Exception: holstered.)

Carrying while intoxicated (§a-6)

  • Class A Misdemeanor — carrying a handgun while intoxicated and not on your own property or in your own vehicle.

Prohibited person carrying (§a-7)

  • Felony of the 2nd Degree (minimum 5 years) — carrying a handgun while prohibited under §46.04(a) (felony conviction).
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — carrying a handgun while prohibited under §46.04(b) (family violence assault conviction) or §46.04(c) (active protective order).
§46.03 PLACES WEAPONS PROHIBITED ▾

§46.03 – PLACES WEAPONS PROHIBITED

Carrying a firearm, club, location-restricted knife, or prohibited weapon into certain off-limits locations.

Prohibited places include:

  1. Schools and educational institution grounds/vehicles
  2. Polling places on election days
  3. Government courts and court offices
  4. Racetracks
  5. Secured areas of airports
  6. Within 1,000 feet of a scheduled execution site (with notice)
  7. Businesses that derive 51%+ of income from alcohol sales on-premises
  8. Sporting event premises (high school, collegiate, professional)
  9. Correctional facilities
  10. Civil commitment facilities
  11. Licensed hospitals or nursing facilities (without written authorization)
  12. Mental hospitals (without written authorization)
  13. Amusement parks (meeting specific criteria)
  14. Open meetings of governmental entities
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — general offense for most prohibited places listed above.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — sporting events, civil commitment facilities, hospitals, amusement parks, and LTC holder violations on higher-education campuses (§a-2, a-3, a-4).
  • Class C Misdemeanor — location-restricted knife at most locations (escalates to Felony of the 3rd Degree on school premises).

Defense (most locations): Armed forces/national guard, correctional guards, and court officers while in actual discharge of duty. LTC holders who promptly leave after being given notice are also protected.

§46.04 UNLAWFUL POSSESSION OF FIREARM ▾

§46.04 – UNLAWFUL POSSESSION OF FIREARM

A felon, domestic violence convict, or person under a protective order who possesses a firearm.

Track A – Felony conviction (§a)

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — a convicted felon who possesses a firearm:
    • At any time within 5 years of release from prison or supervision; or
    • At any place other than their own residence after that 5-year period.

Track A-1 – Criminal street gang member in vehicle (§a-1)

  • Class A Misdemeanor — a criminal street gang member who intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly carries a handgun in a motor vehicle or watercraft.

Track B – Family violence misdemeanor conviction (§b)

  • Class A Misdemeanor — a person convicted of a Class A family violence assault (domestic assault) who possesses a firearm within 5 years of release from confinement or community supervision.

Track C – Active protective order (§c)

  • Class A Misdemeanor — a person (not a peace officer on duty) who is subject to a protective order and possesses a firearm after receiving notice of the order and before it expires.
§46.041 UNLAWFUL POSSESSION OF METAL OR BODY ARMOR BY FELON ▾

§46.041 – UNLAWFUL POSSESSION OF METAL OR BODY ARMOR BY FELON

A convicted felon who possesses a bulletproof vest or body armor.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — a person previously convicted of a felony who possesses metal or body armor (any covering designed to protect against gunfire) after conviction.
§46.05 PROHIBITED WEAPONS ▾

§46.05 – PROHIBITED WEAPONS

Possessing, making, transporting, repairing, or selling certain especially dangerous or illegal weapons.

Prohibited items include:

  • Explosive weapons (unless federally registered NFA item or curio/relic)
  • Machine guns (unless NFA registered)
  • Armor-piercing ammunition
  • Chemical dispensing devices (not small commercial personal-protection sprays)
  • Zip guns
  • Tire deflation devices (spike strips)
  • Improvised explosive devices (IEDs)
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — for most prohibited weapons listed above.
  • State Jail Felony — for tire deflation devices.

Defense: Conduct incidental to official duties of the military, government law enforcement, or correctional facilities is a defense. Antique/curio collecting and authorized law enforcement supply are affirmative defenses for tire deflation devices and armor-piercing ammo.

§46.06 UNLAWFUL TRANSFER OF CERTAIN WEAPONS ▾

§46.06 – UNLAWFUL TRANSFER OF CERTAIN WEAPONS

Selling, lending, or giving a firearm or other weapon to someone who shouldn't have one — like a drunk person, a felon, a child, or someone under a protective order.

Prohibited transfers include:

  • Giving/lending a handgun to someone you know intends to use it unlawfully
  • Selling/giving a firearm, club, or location-restricted knife to anyone under 18
  • Recklessly selling a firearm or ammo to an intoxicated person
  • Knowingly selling a firearm/ammo to a felon within 5 years of their release
  • Giving/lending a handgun to someone subject to an active protective order
  • Buying/receiving a handgun while you yourself are under an active protective order
  • Making a material false statement on a federal firearms form while prohibited from possession
  • Class A Misdemeanor — most violations listed above.
  • State Jail Felony — selling or giving a handgun to a minor; or making a false statement on a federal firearms form.
§46.08 HOAX BOMBS ▾

§46.08 – HOAX BOMBS

Making or possessing a fake bomb with the intent to make people think it's real or cause emergency responders to react.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — knowingly manufacturing, selling, purchasing, transporting, or possessing a hoax bomb (a device that reasonably appears to be an explosive or that by its design causes public safety alarm reactions) with intent to deceive or to cause an alarm/emergency response.
§46.09 COMPONENTS OF EXPLOSIVES ▾

§46.09 – COMPONENTS OF EXPLOSIVES

Possessing bomb-making parts with the intent to assemble them into an explosive for use in a crime.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — knowingly possessing components of an explosive weapon with the intent to combine them into a complete explosive weapon for use in a criminal endeavor.
§46.10 DEADLY WEAPON IN PENAL INSTITUTION ▾

§46.10 – DEADLY WEAPON IN PENAL INSTITUTION

An inmate who carries or hides a deadly weapon while inside a jail or prison.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — a person confined in a penal institution who intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly carries a deadly weapon on or about their person, or possesses and conceals a deadly weapon in the institution.

Affirmative defense: The conduct was authorized by a penal institution employee at the time.

§46.11 PENALTY IF OFFENSE COMMITTED WITHIN WEAPON-FREE SCHOOL ZONE ▾

§46.11 – PENALTY IF OFFENSE COMMITTED WITHIN WEAPON-FREE SCHOOL ZONE

A penalty enhancement — not a standalone offense — that bumps up the punishment for any Chapter 46 weapons offense committed near a school.

  • One category increase — any Chapter 46 offense committed within 300 feet of a school's premises, or on premises where an official school function or UIL event is taking place, is bumped up to the next higher level of offense.

Exception: Does not apply to §46.03(a)(1) (already specifically addresses schools).

§46.13 MAKING A FIREARM ACCESSIBLE TO A CHILD ▾

§46.13 – MAKING A FIREARM ACCESSIBLE TO A CHILD

Leaving a loaded gun where a child (under 17) can get to it through criminal negligence.

  • Class C Misdemeanor — base offense: with criminal negligence, failing to secure a readily dischargeable firearm (loaded) or leaving it where a child gains access.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — if the child discharges the firearm and it causes death or serious bodily injury to anyone.

Affirmative defenses: Access for supervised hunting/sporting; lawful defense by the child; child accessed the gun by trespassing; or the actor was engaged in agricultural activities at the time.

Note: Officers may not arrest within 7 days of the offense if the actor is a family member of the child and the child was killed or seriously injured.

§46.14 FIREARM SMUGGLING ▾

§46.14 – FIREARM SMUGGLING

Running a business of moving guns that were illegally acquired — transporting or transferring them more than once or for profit.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — base: knowingly engaging in the business of transporting or transferring firearms that were acquired in violation of state or federal law (i.e., on more than one occasion, or for profit/remuneration).
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if the offense involved 3 or more firearms in a single criminal episode.

Exception: Peace officers in the actual discharge of official duties are exempt.

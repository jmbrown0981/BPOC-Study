Chapter 47: Gambling

§47.02 GAMBLING ▾

§47.02 – GAMBLING

Making a bet on a game, contest, political outcome, or playing a gambling device for money or something of value.

A person commits this offense by doing any of the following:

  • Betting on the partial or final outcome of a game or contest, or on a participant's performance in a game or contest
  • Betting on the result of a political nomination, appointment, or election — or on how well a candidate or appointee does
  • Playing and betting money (or anything of value) at any card game, dice game, ball game, or any other gambling device

Grade: Class C Misdemeanor

Defenses

It is a defense to prosecution if all three of the following are true:

  • The gambling happened in a private place (a place not open to the public — not a street, restaurant, bar, school, hospital, hotel common area, etc.)
  • Nobody received any economic benefit other than their own personal winnings
  • All participants had the same odds of winning (equal risk for everyone, except for the advantage of skill or luck)

It is also a defense if the suspect reasonably believed their conduct was authorized under state bingo, pull-tab, or charitable gaming laws; the Texas State Lottery; licensed horse or dog racing under the Texas Racing Act; or a Parks & Wildlife Department drawing for a hunting, fishing, or other recreational event.

It is also a defense if the person played for something of value (other than money) using an amusement-only machine excluded from the definition of a gambling device — for example, a prize-redemption arcade machine that pays out merchandise worth no more than $5 per play.

§47.03 GAMBLING PROMOTION ▾

§47.03 – GAMBLING PROMOTION

Running, financing, or actively participating in a gambling operation — one step up from just placing a bet yourself.

A person commits this offense if they intentionally or knowingly do any of the following:

  • Operate or share in the earnings of a gambling place
  • Engage in bookmaking — receiving and recording more than 5 bets in 24 hours, handling bets totaling more than $1,000 in 24 hours, or running a 3-or-more-person scheme to receive or forward bets
  • Act as a custodian (hold the money or valuables) for bets — for profit
  • Sell chances on any game, contest, or political race outcome
  • Set up or promote a lottery, or sell or possess lottery tickets — for profit

Grade: Class A Misdemeanor

§47.04 KEEPING A GAMBLING PLACE ▾

§47.04 – KEEPING A GAMBLING PLACE

Knowingly allowing your property to be used for illegal gambling.

A person commits this offense if they knowingly:

  • Use, or permit another person to use, any property they own or control as a gambling place, OR
  • Rent or lease property with the view or expectation that it will be used for gambling

This applies to any type of property — real estate, buildings, rooms, tents, vehicles, boats, or anything else.

Grade: Class A Misdemeanor

Affirmative Defense

The suspect can raise an affirmative defense if all three of the following are true:

  • The gambling occurred in a private place
  • Nobody received any economic benefit other than their own personal winnings
  • All participants had an equal chance of winning (same risk for everyone, aside from skill or luck)
§47.05 COMMUNICATING GAMBLING INFORMATION ▾

§47.05 – COMMUNICATING GAMBLING INFORMATION

Spreading betting tips or running communication equipment to help illegal gambling operations.

A person commits this offense if they, with the intent to further gambling, knowingly:

  • Communicate information about bets, betting odds, or changes in betting odds, OR
  • Provide, install, or maintain equipment used to transmit or receive such information

Grade: Class A Misdemeanor

Exception

This does not apply if the information is intended for placing a lawful wager (for example, under authorized horse racing regulations) and is not communicated in violation of the Texas Racing Act.

§47.06 POSSESSION OF GAMBLING DEVICE, EQUIPMENT, OR PARAPHERNALIA ▾

§47.06 – POSSESSION OF GAMBLING DEVICE, EQUIPMENT, OR PARAPHERNALIA

Owning, making, moving, or possessing gambling machines, cheating devices, or bookkeeping tools — with intent to further illegal gambling.

This section covers three separate tracks:

Track A – Gambling Devices (§47.06(a))

A person commits this offense if, with intent to further gambling, they knowingly own, manufacture, transfer, or possess any gambling device — or any essential component/subassembly of such a device — knowing it is designed for gambling purposes. Examples include electronic slot machines, video poker machines, and similar devices.

Track B – Altered Gambling Equipment (§47.06(b))

A person commits this offense if, with intent to further gambling, they knowingly own, manufacture, commercially transfer, or possess any altered gambling equipment knowing it is designed for gambling. Examples include shaved dice, loaded dice, magnetic dice, mirror rings, electronic sensors, shaved cards, and marked cards. Note: this track requires commercial transfer — not just any transfer.

Track C – Gambling Paraphernalia (§47.06(c))

A person commits this offense if, with intent to further gambling, they knowingly own, manufacture, commercially transfer, or possess gambling paraphernalia — items such as bet-recording books, instruments, scratch sheets, tickets, certificates, or other materials used to run bookmaking, lotteries, or numbers games.

Grade (all tracks): Class A Misdemeanor

Defenses

  • For Tracks A and C only: it is a defense that the device or paraphernalia is intended solely for use in gambling occurring entirely in a private place, where no one profits beyond personal winnings and all participants have equal odds.
  • For Tracks A and C only: it is also a defense that the person possessed it solely for the purpose of shipping it to another jurisdiction where possession or use is legal.
  • Track B (altered equipment) has no private-place or transit defense — there is no legal exception for cheating devices.

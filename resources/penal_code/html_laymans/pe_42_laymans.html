TX Penal Code — Layman's Guide

Chapter 42: Disorderly Conduct and Related Offenses

§42.01 DISORDERLY CONDUCT ▾

§42.01 – DISORDERLY CONDUCT

Creating a public disturbance — from fighting and flashing to firing guns and peeking in windows.

A person commits the offense if they intentionally or knowingly engage in any of the following in a public place:

  • Using abusive, indecent, profane, or vulgar language that tends to incite immediate breach of the peace
  • Making offensive gestures or displays that tend to incite a breach of the peace
  • Creating a noxious and unreasonable odor by chemical means
  • Abusing or threatening a person in an obviously offensive manner
  • Making unreasonable noise in or near a place they have no right to occupy
  • Fighting in a public place
  • Discharging a firearm in a public place (not on a public road or sport shooting range)
  • Displaying a firearm or deadly weapon in a manner calculated to alarm
  • Discharging a firearm on or across a public road
  • Exposing genitals or anus in public while reckless about whether someone will be offended
  • Peeping through windows of a dwelling, hotel room, or privacy area (restroom, changing room) for a lewd or unlawful purpose
  • Class C Misdemeanor — most of the above.
  • Class B Misdemeanor — discharging a firearm in a public place (§a(7)) or displaying a firearm/deadly weapon in a manner calculated to alarm (§a(8)).

Defense: A significant provocation defense is available for the "abusing or threatening" subsection (§a(4)). Discharging a firearm out of reasonable fear of a dangerous wild animal is also a defense to §a(7) or (9).

§42.02 RIOT ▾

§42.02 – RIOT

Knowingly participating in a mob of 7 or more people whose combined conduct creates danger, obstructs law enforcement, or deprives someone of their rights.

  • Class B Misdemeanor — base offense: knowingly participating in a riot.
  • Same grade as highest offense committed by any rioter — if anyone in the riot commits a higher-grade offense in furtherance of the riot's purpose, or in a way that should have been anticipated, all participants can be charged at that higher grade.

Defense: If the assembly started lawfully and the suspect left when someone manifested an intent to engage in riot conduct, that is a defense.

§42.03 OBSTRUCTING HIGHWAY OR OTHER PASSAGEWAY ▾

§42.03 – OBSTRUCTING HIGHWAY OR OTHER PASSAGEWAY

Blocking a road, sidewalk, hallway, or any other passage — or refusing to move when ordered by an officer or authorized person.

  • Class B Misdemeanor — base offense: intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly obstructing a highway, street, sidewalk, waterway, aisle, hallway, entrance, or exit without legal authority; or disobeying a reasonable order to move from a peace officer, firefighter, or authorized person to prevent obstruction or maintain safety.
  • State Jail Felony — if the blockage prevents passage of an authorized emergency vehicle with lights/sirens active, or blocks access to a hospital or emergency care facility; OR if the person was operating a motor vehicle in a "reckless driving exhibition" (street spinning/drifting spectacle) AND had a prior conviction, was intoxicated, or someone was injured.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — if the person was engaged in a reckless driving exhibition (donut-spinning spectacle) at the time of the obstruction.
§42.05 DISRUPTING MEETING OR PROCESSION ▾

§42.05 – DISRUPTING MEETING OR PROCESSION

Intentionally breaking up a lawful meeting, march, or gathering — whether in person or online.

  • Class B Misdemeanor — intentionally disrupting a lawful meeting, procession, or gathering (including virtual ones) through physical action, verbal utterance, or electronic disturbance such as hacking into an online meeting.
§42.055 FUNERAL SERVICE DISRUPTIONS ▾

§42.055 – FUNERAL SERVICE DISRUPTIONS

Protesting or picketing too close to a funeral — within 1,000 feet during the 3-hour window before and after the service.

  • Class B Misdemeanor — engaging in picketing (carrying signs, loud chanting/singing/yelling, or blocking access) within 1,000 feet of a funeral facility or cemetery during the period beginning 3 hours before and ending 3 hours after a funeral service.
§42.06 FALSE ALARM OR REPORT ▾

§42.06 – FALSE ALARM OR REPORT

Calling in a fake bomb threat, false fire report, or other phony emergency.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — knowingly initiating, communicating, or circulating a report of a false bombing, fire, offense, or other emergency that would cause emergency responders to act, place someone in fear of serious injury, or prevent use of a building or vehicle.
  • State Jail Felony — if the false report involves a school (public or private K–12 or higher education), public communications, transportation, water, gas, power, or other public service.
§42.0601 FALSE REPORT TO INDUCE EMERGENCY RESPONSE ▾

§42.0601 – FALSE REPORT TO INDUCE EMERGENCY RESPONSE (SWATTING)

Making a false police or emergency report that you know could recklessly result in bodily injury to someone when responders show up — commonly called "swatting."

  • Class A Misdemeanor — base offense: making a knowingly false report to police, 9-1-1, or emergency services that causes an emergency response, while reckless as to whether that response could directly result in bodily injury to another.
  • State Jail Felony — if the suspect has two or more prior convictions for this offense.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the false report was of a criminal offense to which law enforcement responded, and a person suffered serious bodily injury or death as a direct result of lawful conduct arising from that response.
§42.061 SILENT OR ABUSIVE CALLS TO 9-1-1 SERVICE ▾

§42.061 – SILENT OR ABUSIVE CALLS TO 9-1-1 SERVICE

Calling 9-1-1 when there is no emergency — staying silent or verbally abusing the dispatcher.

  • Class B Misdemeanor — calling 9-1-1 with no emergency and intentionally remaining silent or making abusive/harassing statements to a PSAP (dispatch) employee; also applies to knowingly permitting another person to misuse your device this way.
§42.062 INTERFERENCE WITH EMERGENCY REQUEST FOR ASSISTANCE ▾

§42.062 – INTERFERENCE WITH EMERGENCY REQUEST FOR ASSISTANCE

Stopping someone from calling 9-1-1 or getting emergency help — like blocking a victim from calling police during a domestic violence situation.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — base offense: knowingly preventing or interfering with another person's ability to place an emergency call or request emergency assistance; or recklessly rendering a phone or device unusable that another person would otherwise use to call for help in an emergency.
  • State Jail Felony — if the suspect has a prior conviction for this offense.
§42.07 HARASSMENT ▾

§42.07 – HARASSMENT

Repeatedly contacting, messaging, or tracking someone with the intent to harass, annoy, alarm, torment, or embarrass them.

The offense requires intent to harass, annoy, alarm, abuse, torment, or embarrass. Prohibited conduct includes:

  • Initiating obscene communications
  • Threatening bodily injury or a felony in a manner likely to alarm the recipient
  • Conveying a false report that someone died or was seriously injured
  • Making repeated anonymous phone calls or calls likely to annoy/harass
  • Failing to hang up after calling
  • Permitting another to use your phone for harassment
  • Sending repeated electronic communications likely to harass or embarrass
  • Repeatedly posting on social media/websites in a manner likely to cause emotional distress (unless matters of public concern)
  • Tracking or monitoring someone's property or vehicle without consent (including GPS apps or physically following them)
  • Making threatening/obscene calls from throwaway/internet phone numbers
  • Class B Misdemeanor — base offense.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — if the suspect has a prior conviction; or if the victim is a utility worker, court employee, or a child under 18 targeted online with intent to make them commit suicide or self-harm.
  • State Jail Felony — if the victim is a judge, or a court employee with a prior conviction for harassment.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the victim is a judge and the suspect has a prior harassment conviction.
§42.072 STALKING ▾

§42.072 – STALKING

A repeated pattern of conduct — on more than one occasion — that threatens someone and causes them reasonable fear of injury, death, or criminal acts against them, their family, or their property.

Three elements must all be present:

  1. The conduct constitutes harassment (§42.07) or the suspect reasonably knows the other person will regard it as threatening bodily injury/death or commission of an offense against them, their family/household/dating partner, or their property;
  2. The conduct places the other person (or their family/household/dating partner) in actual fear or causes them to feel harassed, terrified, intimidated, etc.; and
  3. A reasonable person in the victim's circumstances would similarly fear bodily injury, death, offenses against their family/property, or feel harassed/terrorized.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — base offense.
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if the suspect has a prior stalking conviction in Texas, another state, or under tribal or federal law.
§42.074 UNLAWFUL DISCLOSURE OF RESIDENCE ADDRESS OR TELEPHONE NUMBER ▾

§42.074 – UNLAWFUL DISCLOSURE OF RESIDENCE ADDRESS OR TELEPHONE NUMBER

Posting someone's home address or phone number online with the intent to cause them harm or threats.

  • Class B Misdemeanor — posting on a publicly accessible website or disclosing through an electronic communication someone's residence address or phone number with intent to cause harm or a threat of harm to that person or their family/household.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — if the disclosure results in bodily injury to the victim or a member of their family/household.

Exception: Public servants acting within the scope of state or federal law are exempt.

§42.075 DISCLOSURE OF CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION REGARDING FAMILY VIOLENCE OR VICTIMS OF TRAFFICKING SHELTER CENTER ▾

§42.075 – DISCLOSURE OF CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION REGARDING FAMILY VIOLENCE OR TRAFFICKING SHELTER

Revealing the location or layout of a domestic violence or trafficking victim shelter with intent to endanger the residents.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — a person who, with intent to threaten the safety of shelter residents, discloses or publicizes the location or physical layout of a family violence shelter center or victims of trafficking shelter center.
§42.08 ABUSE OF CORPSE ▾

§42.08 – ABUSE OF CORPSE

Disturbing, damaging, trafficking, or disrespecting a dead body or a person's burial space.

  • State Jail Felony — knowingly, without legal authority, disinterring, disturbing, damaging, dissecting, carrying away, or treating a human corpse in an offensive manner; concealing an illegally disinterred corpse; buying or selling a corpse; or transporting a corpse out of state.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — vandalizing, damaging, or treating in an offensive manner the space where a corpse is interred or permanently laid to rest.
§42.09 CRUELTY TO LIVESTOCK ANIMALS ▾

§42.09 – CRUELTY TO LIVESTOCK ANIMALS

Torturing, neglecting, abandoning, or fighting livestock animals (cattle, horses, sheep, pigs, goats, poultry, and similar animals).

  • Class A Misdemeanor — failing to provide necessary food/water/care, abandoning, transporting or confining in a cruel manner, or seriously overworking a livestock animal. Escalates to a State Jail Felony with two prior animal cruelty convictions (under this section or §42.092, in any combination).
  • State Jail Felony — torture, poisoning another's livestock (other than cattle, horses, sheep, swine, or goats), causing animals to fight, using a live animal as a race lure, or horse tripping. Escalates to a Felony of the 3rd Degree with two prior convictions.

Exception: Lawful hunting, wildlife management, and standard agricultural practices are not offenses.

§42.091 ATTACK ON ASSISTANCE ANIMAL ▾

§42.091 – ATTACK ON ASSISTANCE ANIMAL

Attacking, injuring, or killing a service animal (guide dog, hearing dog, etc.) or directing your animal to do so.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — attacking an assistance animal or inciting/permitting your own animal to attack one.
  • State Jail Felony — injuring an assistance animal or permitting your animal to injure one.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — killing an assistance animal or permitting your animal to kill one.

Note: Courts must order restitution for vet bills, replacement/retraining costs, and other expenses.

§42.092 CRUELTY TO NONLIVESTOCK ANIMALS ▾

§42.092 – CRUELTY TO NONLIVESTOCK ANIMALS

Torturing, neglecting, abandoning, fighting, or otherwise abusing pets and domesticated non-farm animals.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — failing to provide necessary food, water, care, or shelter; abandoning; confining or transporting in a cruel manner; causing bodily injury (without owner's consent); or seriously overworking an animal. Escalates to a State Jail Felony with two prior convictions.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — torturing an animal or killing/causing serious bodily injury in a cruel manner; or killing/seriously injuring another person's animal without consent. Escalates to a Felony of the 2nd Degree with a prior conviction for these specific acts.
  • State Jail Felony — causing animals (other than dogs) to fight, or using a live animal as a race lure. Escalates to a Felony of the 3rd Degree with a prior conviction.
§42.10 DOG FIGHTING ▾

§42.10 – DOG FIGHTING

Causing, hosting, betting on, or attending dogfights — or owning/training dogs for fighting.

  • Class A Misdemeanor — owning or possessing dog-fighting equipment with fighting intent; owning or training a dog for fighting; attending as a spectator.
  • State Jail Felony — causing a dog to fight another dog; participating in the earnings of or operating a dog-fighting facility; using or permitting property to be used for dog fighting.

Defense: Causing a dog to fight in defense of livestock or other property against another dog is a defense to §(a)(1).

§42.105 COCKFIGHTING ▾

§42.105 – COCKFIGHTING

Causing roosters to fight, hosting cockfights, or breeding/training roosters for fighting.

  • Class C Misdemeanor — attending a cockfight as a spectator (escalates to Class A Misdemeanor with a prior conviction for attending). Exception: spectators 15 and under are exempt.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — manufacturing, buying, selling, or possessing gaffs/slashers (fighting spurs); using or permitting property for cockfighting; owning or training cocks for fighting.
  • State Jail Felony — causing a cock to fight another; participating in the earnings of a cockfight.
§42.107 POSSESSION OF ANIMAL BY PERSON CONVICTED OF ANIMAL CRUELTY ▾

§42.107 – POSSESSION OF ANIMAL BY PERSON CONVICTED OF ANIMAL CRUELTY

A person with a recent animal cruelty conviction who tries to own or control an animal again within five years.

  • Class C Misdemeanor — possessing or exercising control over any animal within 5 years of a conviction under §42.091, §42.092, or §42.10 (or substantially similar offenses in other jurisdictions).
  • Class B Misdemeanor — if the suspect has a prior conviction under this section.
§42.11 DESTRUCTION OF FLAG ▾

§42.11 – DESTRUCTION OF FLAG

Intentionally or knowingly damaging, defacing, mutilating, or burning the U.S. or Texas flag.

  • Class A Misdemeanor

Exception: Disposing of a damaged flag in accordance with federal or state law (proper retirement) is not an offense.

Note: "Flag" means an actual flag capable of being flown — not a printed or painted depiction on paper, clothing, or jewelry.

§42.12 DISCHARGE OF FIREARM IN CERTAIN MUNICIPALITIES ▾

§42.12 – DISCHARGE OF FIREARM IN CERTAIN MUNICIPALITIES

Recklessly firing a gun inside the city limits of a large municipality (100,000 or more population).

  • Class A Misdemeanor — recklessly discharging a firearm inside the corporate limits of a municipality with a population of 100,000 or more.
§42.13 USE OF LASER POINTERS ▾

§42.13 – USE OF LASER POINTERS

Knowingly shining a laser pointer at a uniformed officer, security guard, firefighter, EMT, or other uniformed public safety worker.

  • Class C Misdemeanor — base offense: knowingly directing a laser pointer at a uniformed safety officer (peace officer, security guard, firefighter, EMS worker, or other uniformed municipal/state/federal officer).
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the laser causes bodily injury to the officer.
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — if the laser causes serious bodily injury to the officer.
§42.14 ILLUMINATION OF AIRCRAFT BY INTENSE LIGHT ▾

§42.14 – ILLUMINATION OF AIRCRAFT BY INTENSE LIGHT

Shining a laser or intense light at a plane or helicopter in a way that could impair the pilot's ability to control the aircraft.

  • Class C Misdemeanor — intentionally directing a laser or other intense light at an aircraft, even if it doesn't impair the pilot.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — if the light is intense enough to impair the operator's ability to control the aircraft.

Affirmative defense: Using the light to send an emergency distress signal.

§42.15 OPERATION OF UNMANNED AIRCRAFT OVER AIRPORT, MILITARY INSTALLATION, OR SPACEPORT ▾

§42.15 – OPERATION OF UNMANNED AIRCRAFT OVER AIRPORT, MILITARY INSTALLATION, OR SPACEPORT

Flying a drone over an airport, military base, or spaceport in a way that interferes with operations.

  • Class B Misdemeanor — intentionally or knowingly operating a drone over an airport, military installation, or spaceport; allowing the drone to make contact with the facility; or operating it in a manner that interferes with or causes a disturbance to the facility.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — if the suspect has a prior conviction under this section.

Exceptions: Government agencies, persons with authorization from facility operators or the FAA, law enforcement, and property owners/occupants are exempt.

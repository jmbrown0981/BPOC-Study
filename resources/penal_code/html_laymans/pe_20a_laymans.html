§20A.02 TRAFFICKING OF PERSONS ▾

§20A.02 – TRAFFICKING OF PERSONS

Transporting, recruiting, harboring, or obtaining a person through force, fraud, or coercion to exploit them for labor or sex — or doing any of this to a child or disabled individual regardless of consent.

Eight Ways to Commit (summarized)

  • (1) Forced Labor Trafficking — Adult: Trafficking another person with the intent that they engage in forced labor or services.
  • (2) Benefiting from Forced Labor Venture: Receiving a benefit from participating in a venture involving forced labor — including knowingly receiving forced labor or services.
  • (3) Sex Trafficking — Adult (with coercion): Trafficking another person and, through force, fraud, or coercion, causing them to engage in prostitution, promotion of prostitution, or compelling prostitution.
  • (4) Benefiting from or Engaging in Sex Trafficking Venture: Receiving a benefit from a coercive sex trafficking venture, or having sex with a person trafficked in that manner.
  • (5) Forced Labor Trafficking — Child or Disabled: Trafficking a child or disabled individual with the intent they engage in forced labor. It does not matter whether the suspect knew the victim's age or disability status.
  • (6) Benefiting from Child/Disabled Forced Labor Venture: Receiving a benefit from a venture that traffics a child or disabled person for forced labor.
  • (7) Sex/Abuse Trafficking — Child or Disabled (any means): Trafficking a child or disabled individual — by ANY means (no force/coercion required) — and causing them to engage in or become a victim of a long list of offenses including sexual assault, aggravated sexual assault, indecency with a child, prostitution, child pornography, etc. Age/disability knowledge is not required.
  • (8) Benefiting from or Engaging in Child/Disabled Sex Trafficking Venture: Receiving a benefit from or having sex with a child or disabled person trafficked in the manner described above.

Punishment

  • Base: Generally Felony of the 2nd Degree (but see note on conflicting 2025 legislation below)
  • Enhancement — Felony of the 1st Degree: If the offense involves a child or disabled individual (subsections 5–8); if it results in serious bodily injury or death; if it results in death of an unborn child; or if the actor used a deadly weapon, strangled the victim, or recruited the victim from a shelter, treatment center, or correctional facility.
  • Enhanced Felony of the 1st Degree (25 years to life): If any part of the offense occurred on or within 1,000 feet of a school, college, juvenile detention facility, shelter, community youth center, child-care facility, or correctional facility, or at a school function.

Note: 2025 legislation created two conflicting versions of the base punishment. One version (SB 806) makes all offenses under this section a Felony of the 1st Degree. Officers should be aware courts may apply the harsher version.

Notable Rules

  • A trafficked child who was directed into prostitution is not a defense that the child lacked intent or didn't complete the act.
  • Can be charged under this section AND another applicable section (dual prosecution is allowed).
§20A.03 CONTINUOUS TRAFFICKING OF PERSONS ▾

§20A.03 – CONTINUOUS TRAFFICKING OF PERSONS

Repeated trafficking offenses over a period of at least 30 days — treated as a single, more serious charge carrying a 25-year minimum.

Elements

During a period of 30 or more days, the suspect engages in conduct that constitutes Trafficking of Persons (§20A.02) two or more times against one or more victims. The jury must unanimously agree on the overall pattern of conduct — not necessarily the specific acts or exact dates.

Punishment

  • Felony of the 1st Degree, punishable by 25 years to 99 years or life in prison.

Double Jeopardy Protection

If the same victim is also a victim in a §20A.02 charge, the defendant generally cannot be convicted of both in the same criminal action unless the §20A.02 offense occurred outside the continuous trafficking period or is charged in the alternative.

§20A.04 ACCOMPLICE WITNESS; TESTIMONY AND IMMUNITY ▾

§20A.04 – ACCOMPLICE WITNESS; TESTIMONY AND IMMUNITY

A participant in a trafficking offense can be required to testify and receives immunity from prosecution for what they testify about.

Key Points for Officers

  • A party to a trafficking offense may be compelled to testify about that offense.
  • In exchange, they cannot be prosecuted for any offense about which they are required to provide evidence or testify (except aggravated perjury).
  • A trafficking conviction can be based solely on the uncorroborated testimony of a co-participant — corroboration is not required.

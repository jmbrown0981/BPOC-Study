Chapter 31: Theft

§31.03 THEFT ▾

§31.03 – THEFT

Taking someone's property without their consent and with intent to deprive them of it — the core theft offense covering shoplifting, pickpocketing, embezzlement, receiving stolen goods, and more.

A person commits theft if they unlawfully appropriate property with intent to deprive the owner of it. Appropriation is unlawful if it is:

  • Without the owner's effective consent, OR
  • The property is stolen and the suspect knows it was stolen, OR
  • Property that law enforcement represented as stolen and the suspect believed it was stolen.

Punishment Scale (by value of property stolen)

  • Class C Misdemeanor — under $100
  • Class B Misdemeanor — $100 to under $750; OR under $100 with a prior theft conviction; OR stealing a driver's license or state ID
  • Class A Misdemeanor — $750 to under $2,500
  • State Jail Felony — $2,500 to under $30,000; OR stolen from a person or a corpse/grave (any value); OR stealing a firearm (any value); OR under $2,500 with two or more prior theft convictions; OR stealing an official election ballot; OR stealing aluminum, bronze, copper, or brass under $20,000; OR stealing a catalytic converter (replacement cost under $30,000); OR stealing in a declared disaster area as a fake volunteer (under $30,000)
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — $30,000 to under $150,000; OR cattle, horses, or exotic livestock under $150,000; OR 10+ head of sheep, swine, or goats under $150,000; OR controlled substances under $150,000 from a pharmacy, clinic, hospital, nursing facility, warehouse, or drug distributor vehicle
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — $150,000 to under $300,000; OR any amount under $300,000 for stealing an ATM or its contents/components
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — $300,000 or more

Enhancements (bumps to NEXT higher category)

  • Suspect was a public servant and the property came into their control through that position
  • Suspect was in a government contract and the property came through the contract
  • Victim was an elderly individual or a nonprofit organization
  • Suspect was a Medicare provider exploiting that relationship
  • Suspect set off or deactivated a fire exit alarm or used a shielding/deactivation instrument to defeat a retail theft detector
  • Stolen property was a catalytic converter (state jail felony or higher) AND the suspect had a firearm during the offense — bumped to next level
  • Stolen property was copper or brass from a critical infrastructure facility (state jail felony or higher) — bumped to next level
§31.04 THEFT OF SERVICE ▾

§31.04 – THEFT OF SERVICE

Getting services without paying for them — through deception, threats, keeping rental property too long, or simply skipping out on the bill.

A person commits theft of service by obtaining services they know are only provided for compensation, using any of these methods:

  • Securing services through deception, threat, or false token
  • Diverting another's services to their own benefit without authorization
  • Keeping rental property past the rental period without consent, thereby depriving the owner of further rentals
  • Agreeing to pay for services, having them performed, then failing to pay after receiving a demand notice

Presumptions of Intent to Avoid Payment

  • Walking out without paying at a restaurant, hotel, etc.
  • Failing to pay within 10 days of receiving written notice demanding payment
  • Failing to return rental property within 5 days (under $2,500), 3 days ($2,500–$10,000), or 2 days ($10,000+) after notice

Punishment Scale (by value of service)

  • Class C Misdemeanor — under $100
  • Class B Misdemeanor — $100 to under $750
  • Class A Misdemeanor — $750 to under $2,500
  • State Jail Felony — $2,500 to under $30,000
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — $30,000 to under $150,000
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — $150,000 to under $300,000
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — $300,000 or more

Defense

It is a defense if the suspect gave a post-dated check and the payee presented it early (before the date on the check).

§31.05 THEFT OF TRADE SECRETS ▾

§31.05 – THEFT OF TRADE SECRETS

Stealing, copying, or sharing a company's confidential technical secrets — formulas, designs, processes, or other protected proprietary information.

A person commits this offense if, without the owner's effective consent, they knowingly:

  • Steal a trade secret
  • Copy an item that embodies a trade secret (writing, recording, drawing, sample, blueprint, etc.)
  • Communicate or transmit a trade secret to others

A "trade secret" is any scientific or technical information, design, process, procedure, formula, or improvement that has value and that the owner has taken steps to keep secret from unauthorized persons.

Punishment

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — base offense.
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — if the suspect intended to benefit a foreign agent, foreign government, or foreign instrumentality (i.e., espionage-adjacent).
§31.06 PRESUMPTION FOR THEFT BY CHECK OR SIMILAR SIGHT ORDER ▾

§31.06 – PRESUMPTION FOR THEFT BY CHECK OR SIMILAR SIGHT ORDER

Writing a bad check (insufficient funds) creates a legal presumption of intent to steal — this section establishes when that presumption kicks in.

This section is a rules/presumption provision supporting prosecution of theft (§31.03) and theft of service (§31.04) when payment was made by check. Intent to steal is presumed (but rebuttable) if:

  • The issuer had no account at the bank when writing the check, OR
  • The check was returned for insufficient funds within 30 days of issue, AND the issuer failed to pay in full within 10 days of receiving written notice of the refusal.

A separate presumption applies if the issuer stopped payment and then failed to pay or return the property after demand. Written notice is presumed received within 5 days of mailing. Partial restitution does not remove the presumption.

§31.07 UNAUTHORIZED USE OF A VEHICLE ▾

§31.07 – UNAUTHORIZED USE OF A VEHICLE

Joyriding — operating someone else's vehicle, boat, or airplane without their permission, even if you don't intend to keep it.

A person commits this offense if they intentionally or knowingly operate another person's boat, airplane, or motor-propelled vehicle without the effective consent of the owner. Unlike theft, there is no requirement to intend to permanently deprive — just operating it without permission is enough.

Punishment

  • State Jail Felony
§31.11 TAMPERING WITH IDENTIFICATION NUMBERS ▾

§31.11 – TAMPERING WITH IDENTIFICATION NUMBERS

Removing or altering serial numbers or other permanent markings on property — typically to hide that something is stolen.

A person commits this offense if they:

  • Remove, alter, or obliterate the serial number or other permanent ID marking on tangible personal property, OR
  • Possess, sell, or offer to sell property knowing (or where a reasonable person would know) the serial number or permanent ID has been removed, altered, or obliterated.

Punishment

  • Class A Misdemeanor

Affirmative Defenses

  • The person was the owner or acting with the owner's effective consent
  • A peace officer acting in official duties
  • Acting under authority of TxDOT or TxDMV with respect to a vehicle identification number
§31.12 THEFT OF OR TAMPERING WITH MULTICHANNEL VIDEO OR INFORMATION SERVICES ▾

§31.12 – THEFT OF OR TAMPERING WITH MULTICHANNEL VIDEO OR INFORMATION SERVICES

Stealing cable TV or other video/information services by making unauthorized connections, attaching devices, or tampering with a provider's equipment.

Without the service provider's authorization, a person commits this offense if they intentionally or knowingly:

  • Make or maintain a connection to a cable, wire, or receiver attached to a multichannel video/information services system
  • Attach a device to such a system to receive services without paying
  • Tamper with or modify provider-installed equipment
  • Tamper with or use an access device (signal decoder, etc.) to get services without authorization

Each connection, attachment, modification, or act of tampering is a separate offense.

Punishment

  • Class C Misdemeanor — first offense.
  • Class B Misdemeanor — one prior conviction.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — two or more prior convictions; OR if committed for pay (for remuneration), regardless of priors.
  • Class A Misdemeanor with minimum $2,000 fine and 180 days — committed for pay AND two or more prior convictions.
§31.13 MANUFACTURE, DISTRIBUTION, OR ADVERTISEMENT OF MULTICHANNEL VIDEO OR INFORMATION SERVICES DEVICE ▾

§31.13 – MANUFACTURE, DISTRIBUTION, OR ADVERTISEMENT OF MULTICHANNEL VIDEO OR INFORMATION SERVICES DEVICE

Making, importing, selling, or advertising cable TV piracy devices for profit.

A person commits this offense if, for payment, they intentionally or knowingly manufacture, assemble, modify, import, export, distribute, advertise, or offer for sale any device, kit, or plan designed to decode encrypted cable/video/information signals — with intent to help someone commit cable theft (§31.12).

Punishment

  • Class A Misdemeanor
§31.14 SALE OR LEASE OF MULTICHANNEL VIDEO OR INFORMATION SERVICES DEVICE ▾

§31.14 – SALE OR LEASE OF MULTICHANNEL VIDEO OR INFORMATION SERVICES DEVICE

Selling or renting cable piracy devices with the intent to help someone steal cable service.

A person commits this offense if they intentionally or knowingly sell or lease a device, kit, or plan designed to decode cable/video/information services — with intent to aid in cable theft (§31.12).

Punishment

  • Class A Misdemeanor
§31.15 POSSESSION, MANUFACTURE, OR DISTRIBUTION OF CERTAIN INSTRUMENTS USED TO COMMIT RETAIL THEFT ▾

§31.15 – POSSESSION, MANUFACTURE, OR DISTRIBUTION OF RETAIL THEFT INSTRUMENTS

Having or dealing in booster bags or other devices specifically designed to defeat anti-theft systems in stores.

A person commits this offense if, with intent to use the instrument to commit theft, they:

  • Possess a shielding or deactivation instrument (e.g., a foil-lined bag designed to defeat retail theft detectors, or a tool for removing security tags), OR
  • Knowingly manufacture, sell, offer for sale, or distribute such an instrument.

Punishment

  • Class A Misdemeanor
§31.16 ORGANIZED RETAIL THEFT ▾

§31.16 – ORGANIZED RETAIL THEFT

Coordinated shoplifting — working with others, repeat theft from merchants within 180 days, benefiting from organized theft, or swarming a store's security.

A person commits organized retail theft if they:

  • Acting in concert with one or more others, steal retail merchandise, money, or property from a merchant, OR
  • Steal from a merchant on two or more occasions within 180 days, OR
  • Knowingly benefit from organized retail theft committed by someone else, OR
  • Knowingly act with others to overwhelm security (including peace officers) to commit or avoid detection of retail theft.

Punishment Scale (by total value involved)

  • Class B Misdemeanor — under $100
  • Class A Misdemeanor — $100 to under $750
  • State Jail Felony — $750 to under $2,500
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — $2,500 to under $30,000
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — $30,000 to under $150,000
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — $150,000 or more

Notable Points

  • It is NOT a defense that other participants weren't charged or identified.
  • Altering price tags or transferring merchandise to different packaging creates a presumption of intent to steal.
§31.17 UNAUTHORIZED ACQUISITION OR TRANSFER OF CERTAIN FINANCIAL INFORMATION ▾

§31.17 – UNAUTHORIZED ACQUISITION OR TRANSFER OF CERTAIN FINANCIAL INFORMATION

Skimming — using a device to capture someone's check, credit card, or debit card information without their knowledge, or passing that stolen data to someone else.

A person commits this offense if, knowing they are not entitled to the information, they:

  • Obtain financial sight order or payment card information (check data, credit/debit card numbers from the front, back, or magnetic strip) using an electronic, photographic, visual imaging, recording, or other device, OR
  • Transfer information obtained that way to a third party.

Punishment

  • Class B Misdemeanor — for acquiring/skimming the information.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — for transferring/selling the skimmed information to another.
§31.18 CARGO THEFT ▾

§31.18 – CARGO THEFT

Receiving, concealing, selling, or dealing in stolen commercial freight shipments — or a contracted driver who diverts cargo or breaks its seal.

A person commits cargo theft if they:

  • Knowingly or intentionally receive, possess, conceal, store, barter, sell, abandon, or dispose of stolen cargo (or cargo represented as stolen), OR
  • Are a contracted driver for a shipment and, with intent to facilitate the above, fail to deliver the entire cargo to its destination, or break the seal on the vehicle or container.

"Cargo" means commercial freight moving in commerce — from origin to final destination, including stops.

Punishment Scale (by total cargo value, including any vehicle stolen/damaged)

  • State Jail Felony — $1,500 to under $10,000
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — $10,000 to under $100,000
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — $100,000 to under $200,000
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — $200,000 or more

Enhancement

For state jail felony through 2nd degree: bumped to next higher category if the suspect organized, supervised, financed, or managed one or more others in the theft activity.

§31.19 THEFT OF PETROLEUM PRODUCT OR OIL AND GAS EQUIPMENT ▾

§31.19 – THEFT OF PETROLEUM PRODUCT OR OIL AND GAS EQUIPMENT

Stealing crude oil, natural gas, condensate, or oilfield equipment — including pipeline tapping and unauthorized transport.

There are two tracks:

Track A — Petroleum Product Theft

Unlawfully appropriating crude oil, natural gas, or condensate by possessing, removing, delivering, receiving, purchasing, selling, moving, concealing, transporting it — or making unauthorized connections to pipelines or tanks. Also includes transporting to disposal without knowing the original owner, buying from an unauthorized seller, or storing/trading through unauthorized methods.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — total value under $10,000
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — $10,000 to under $100,000
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — $100,000 or more

Track B — Oil and Gas Equipment Theft

Unlawfully appropriating machinery, drilling equipment, welding equipment, pipeline equipment, pumps, vehicles, or other oilfield equipment.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — total value under $10,000
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — $10,000 to under $100,000
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — $100,000 or more
§31.20 MAIL THEFT ▾

§31.20 – MAIL THEFT

Stealing mail from porches, mailboxes, or in transit — whether for the contents, to steal checks/negotiable instruments, or to commit identity theft.

A person commits this offense if they intentionally appropriate mail without the addressee's consent and with intent to:

  • Deprive the addressee of the mail, OR
  • Steal a negotiable instrument contained in the mail.

Presumptions: Possessing mail from 5+ addressees creates a rebuttable presumption of theft intent. Possessing mail containing 5+ negotiable instruments creates a presumption of intent to steal and to commit fraud.

Punishment (Basic — by number of addressees)

  • Class A Misdemeanor — mail from fewer than 10 addressees
  • State Jail Felony — 10 to fewer than 30 addressees
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — 30 or more addressees

Enhanced Punishment — If committed with intent to commit identity theft (§32.51)

  • State Jail Felony — fewer than 10 addressees
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — 10 to fewer than 20 addressees
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — 20 to fewer than 50 addressees
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — 50 or more addressees

Enhanced Punishment — If committed with intent to steal negotiable instruments and commit fraud (Ch. 32)

  • State Jail Felony — 5 or fewer negotiable instruments
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — 6 to 9 instruments
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — 10 to 49 instruments
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — 50 or more instruments

Further Enhancement

Identity theft or negotiable instrument offenses (state jail through 2nd degree): bumped one level higher if the victim was a disabled or elderly individual.

§31.21 UNAUTHORIZED POSSESSION OF CATALYTIC CONVERTER ▾

§31.21 – UNAUTHORIZED POSSESSION OF CATALYTIC CONVERTER

Having a catalytic converter that has been cut off a vehicle, unless you're the vehicle owner or in the legitimate recycling/scrap business.

A person commits this offense if they intentionally or knowingly possess a catalytic converter that has been removed from a motor vehicle and are not authorized to possess it.

A person is presumed authorized if they are:

  • The owner of the vehicle it was removed from, OR
  • A business in the ordinary course of their work (including licensed metal recycling entities).

The presumption does NOT apply to business owners who know the converter was unlawfully obtained.

Punishment

  • State Jail Felony — base offense.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree if:
    • Prior conviction under this section, OR
    • The offense involved conspiracy to commit criminal mischief or theft related to a catalytic converter, OR
    • Suspect possessed a firearm during the offense.
§31.22 UNAUTHORIZED POSSESSION OF CERTAIN COPPER OR BRASS MATERIAL ▾

§31.22 – UNAUTHORIZED POSSESSION OF CERTAIN COPPER OR BRASS MATERIAL

Possessing copper or brass wire, cable, or fittings without being an authorized user — targeting scrap metal theft.

A person commits this offense if they intentionally or knowingly possess copper or brass material without being authorized to do so. Authorized persons include the owner, utilities, telecoms, cable providers, manufacturers, licensed metal recyclers, construction/demolition workers acquiring material in scope of work, and their agents.

The authorization does NOT apply to anyone who knows the material was unlawfully obtained.

Punishment

  • State Jail Felony — base offense.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree if:
    • The material was stolen from a critical infrastructure facility, OR
    • Suspect has a prior conviction under this section or prior theft/mischief conviction involving copper or brass, OR
    • Offense involved conspiracy or criminal organization activity, OR
    • Suspect possessed a firearm during the offense.
§31.23 REAL PROPERTY THEFT ▾

§31.23 – REAL PROPERTY THEFT

Deed fraud — forging or falsifying the transfer of real estate to steal land or a home from its rightful owner, or fraudulently selling property you don't own.

There are two tracks:

Track 1 — Stealing Real Property (Deed Fraud)

Bringing about or attempting a transfer of real property, title, or a nonpossessory interest in real property without the owner's effective consent and with intent to deprive the owner.

  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — market value under $300,000
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — market value $300,000 or more

Track 2 — Fraudulent Sale of Another's Property

Selling or encumbering real property or a nonpossessory interest without the true owner's consent and in exchange for a benefit (such as selling someone else's house and pocketing the proceeds).

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — benefit value under $30,000
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — $30,000 to under $150,000
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — $150,000 or more

Enhancement (bumped one level higher) if victim was:

  • An elderly individual, a disabled individual, or a nonprofit organization, OR
  • The property was subject to a homestead property tax exemption.

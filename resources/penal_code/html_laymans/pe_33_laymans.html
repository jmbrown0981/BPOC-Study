Chapter 33: Computer Crimes

§33.02 BREACH OF COMPUTER SECURITY ▾

§33.02 – BREACH OF COMPUTER SECURITY

Hacking — accessing someone else's computer, network, or system without permission; elevated when done to defraud, harm, or steal data.

Track A – Simple Unauthorized Access (sub.(a))

  • Class B Misdemeanor – base grade
  • State Jail Felony – if the suspect has 2 or more prior convictions under Chapter 33, OR the computer belongs to a government entity or critical infrastructure facility (power plants, water treatment, refineries, telecom hubs, etc.)

Track B – Unauthorized Access WITH Intent to Defraud, Harm, or Alter/Delete Data (sub.(b-1))

This also covers employees/contractors who violate a clear company policy or their contractual agreement to access systems and steal proprietary data.

  • Class C Misdemeanor – aggregate harm less than $100
  • Class B Misdemeanor – $100 to $749
  • Class A Misdemeanor – $750 to $2,499
  • State Jail Felony – $2,500 to $29,999
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – $30,000 to $149,999
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree – $150,000 to $299,999; OR any amount under $300,000 if government/critical infrastructure is involved; OR suspect obtained another person's identifying information from one system
  • Felony of the 1st Degree – $300,000 or more; OR suspect obtained identifying information from more than one system

Defenses: (1) Law enforcement lawful seizure or search; (2) security professional acting under contract with the owner to assess system security.

§33.021 ONLINE SOLICITATION OF A MINOR ▾

§33.021 – ONLINE SOLICITATION OF A MINOR

Using the internet or electronic messages to send sexual content to a minor, or to arrange a meeting with a minor for sexual contact.

A "minor" is someone under 17 — or someone the suspect believes to be under 17. The suspect must be 17 or older.

Track A – Sexually Explicit Communication or Distribution to a Minor (sub.(b))

Communicating in a sexually explicit manner with a minor, or sending/distributing sexually explicit material to a minor, via internet, email, text, or commercial online service — with intent to commit a listed sexual offense.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – base grade
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree – if the minor is under 14 (or the suspect believes them to be under 14)

Track B – Soliciting a Minor to Meet for Sexual Contact (sub.(c))

Knowingly soliciting a minor online to meet with intent to engage in sexual contact, intercourse, or deviate sexual intercourse. It is NOT a defense that no meeting actually occurred.

  • Felony of the 2nd Degree – base grade

School-Hours Enhancement (Both Tracks)

  • Bumped to the next higher category if the offense was committed during regular school hours and the suspect knew (or reasonably should have known) the minor was enrolled in school.

Defenses (Track B only): Actor was married to the minor; actor was no more than 3 years older than the minor and the minor consented.

§33.022 ELECTRONIC ACCESS INTERFERENCE ▾

§33.022 – ELECTRONIC ACCESS INTERFERENCE

Intentionally shutting down or blocking access to someone's computer system or network without their consent — essentially a cyberattack denial-of-service offense.

The suspect intentionally interrupts or suspends access to a computer system or network without the owner's effective consent. Does not apply to network/online service providers acting for a legitimate business purpose (routine maintenance, etc.).

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – base and only grade

Defense: Law enforcement lawful seizure, search, or access.

§33.023 ELECTRONIC DATA TAMPERING ▾

§33.023 – ELECTRONIC DATA TAMPERING

Secretly altering data in transit between computers, or deploying ransomware to extort victims.

Track A – Base Offense (no intent to defraud)

  • Class C Misdemeanor – base grade

Track B – With Intent to Defraud or Harm (scale by aggregate harm)

  • Class C Misdemeanor – less than $100, or amount unknown
  • Class B Misdemeanor – $100 to $749
  • Class A Misdemeanor – $750 to $2,499
  • State Jail Felony – $2,500 to $29,999
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – $30,000 to $149,999
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree – $150,000 to $299,999
  • Felony of the 1st Degree – $300,000 or more

Track C – Restricting Access to Privileged Information (medical, legal, or accounting records)

  • State Jail Felony – aggregate amount less than $2,500
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – $2,500 to $29,999, OR a client/patient of a victim suffered harm attributable to the offense
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree – $30,000 to $149,999, OR a client/patient suffered bodily injury
  • Felony of the 1st Degree – $150,000 or more, OR a client/patient suffered serious bodily injury or death

Note: Normal subscription paywalls or authentication-locked content are NOT ransomware under this section.

§33.024 UNLAWFUL DECRYPTION ▾

§33.024 – UNLAWFUL DECRYPTION

Breaking or bypassing someone's encryption to access their private information — using deception and without legitimate business purpose.

The suspect intentionally decrypts encrypted private information through deception without a legitimate business purpose.

Track A – Base Offense

  • Class C Misdemeanor

Track B – With Intent to Defraud or Harm (scale by aggregate value)

  • Class C Misdemeanor – less than $100 or unknown
  • Class B Misdemeanor – $100 to $749
  • Class A Misdemeanor – $750 to $2,499
  • State Jail Felony – $2,500 to $29,999
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – $30,000 to $149,999
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree – $150,000 to $299,999
  • Felony of the 1st Degree – $300,000 or more

Track C – Decrypting Privileged Information (medical, legal, accounting records)

  • State Jail Felony – less than $2,500
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – $2,500 to $29,999, OR a client/patient suffered harm
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree – $30,000 to $149,999, OR a client/patient suffered bodily injury
  • Felony of the 1st Degree – $150,000 or more, OR a client/patient suffered serious bodily injury or death

Defense: Actor was authorized under a contract with the owner to assess the security of the information or provide security-related services.

§33.05 TAMPERING WITH DIRECT RECORDING ELECTRONIC VOTING MACHINE ▾

§33.05 – TAMPERING WITH DIRECT RECORDING ELECTRONIC VOTING MACHINE

Hacking into a computerized voting machine to change, prevent, or fabricate votes.

The suspect knowingly accesses a computer or system that is part of a direct recording electronic (DRE) voting system and, through that access: prevents someone from casting a lawful vote, changes a lawfully cast vote, prevents a lawful vote from being counted, or causes an unlawfully cast vote to be counted.

  • Felony of the 1st Degree – base and only grade

Notable: Criminal attempt to commit this offense is a Felony of the 3rd Degree. The offense does not require that the tampered votes actually affected the official election outcome.

§33.07 ONLINE IMPERSONATION ▾

§33.07 – ONLINE IMPERSONATION

Using someone else's name or identity online to harm, threaten, or defraud — or faking emails/messages as if they came from that person.

Track A – Creating Fake Social Media Profiles or Posts (sub.(a))

The suspect, without the real person's consent and with intent to harm, defraud, intimidate, or threaten someone, uses another person's name or persona to create a web page or post/send messages on social media or other websites.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree

Track B – Sending Fake Emails or Messages Impersonating Another Person (sub.(b))

The suspect sends a message that references another person's name, address, phone number, or other identifying information — without their consent, intending the recipient to believe the real person sent it — with intent to harm or defraud.

  • Class A Misdemeanor – base grade
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – if the suspect sent it with intent to solicit an emergency response (fake 911 call, fake emergency report)

Defenses: The suspect is a social media platform, ISP, interactive computer service, telecom provider, or cable/video provider — or an employee acting within that role.

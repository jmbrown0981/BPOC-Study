Chapter 33A: Telecommunications Crimes

§33A.02 §33A.03 §33A.04 §33A.05 §33A.051
§33A.02 UNAUTHORIZED USE OF TELECOMMUNICATIONS SERVICE ▾

§33A.02 – UNAUTHORIZED USE OF TELECOMMUNICATIONS SERVICE

An insider at a phone company secretly using or diverting phone service for their own benefit or someone else's.

The suspect must be an officer, shareholder, partner, employee, agent, or independent contractor of a telecommunications service provider. They knowingly use or divert phone service without authorization.

Scale by Value of Service Used or Diverted

  • Class B Misdemeanor – less than $500
  • Class A Misdemeanor – $500 to $1,499; OR less than $500 with one prior conviction under Chapter 33A
  • State Jail Felony – $1,500 to $19,999; OR less than $1,500 with two or more prior convictions under Chapter 33A
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – $20,000 to $99,999
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree – $100,000 to $199,999
  • Felony of the 1st Degree – $200,000 or more

Notable: Amounts from a continuing scheme may be aggregated to determine the grade.

§33A.03 MANUFACTURE, POSSESSION, OR DELIVERY OF UNLAWFUL TELECOMMUNICATIONS DEVICE ▾

§33A.03 – MANUFACTURE, POSSESSION, OR DELIVERY OF UNLAWFUL TELECOMMUNICATIONS DEVICE

Making, possessing, selling, or advertising cloned phones, modified devices, or instructions to build them for the purpose of stealing phone service.

Two forms: (1) making/possessing/delivering/advertising a counterfeit telecommunications device or a device intended to steal service or hide its origin; (2) delivering or advertising plans, instructions, or materials to build such a device.

  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – base and only grade

Defense: The suspect was a law enforcement officer, agent, or employee of a telecom provider gathering information for a law enforcement investigation under this chapter.

§33A.04 THEFT OF TELECOMMUNICATIONS SERVICE ▾

§33A.04 – THEFT OF TELECOMMUNICATIONS SERVICE

Using stolen, fake, or unauthorized devices or schemes to get free phone service — stealing it from the provider.

The suspect knowingly obtains or tries to obtain telecommunications service to avoid paying for it, using: someone else's access device without authorization; a counterfeit device; a fraudulent scheme; or a false/altered/stolen ID.

Scale by Value of Service Obtained or Attempted

  • Class B Misdemeanor – less than $500
  • Class A Misdemeanor – $500 to $1,499; OR less than $500 with one prior conviction under Chapter 33A
  • State Jail Felony – $1,500 to $19,999; OR less than $1,500 with two or more prior convictions under Chapter 33A
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – $20,000 to $99,999
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree – $100,000 to $199,999
  • Felony of the 1st Degree – $200,000 or more
§33A.05 PUBLICATION OF TELECOMMUNICATIONS ACCESS DEVICE ▾

§33A.05 – PUBLICATION OF TELECOMMUNICATIONS ACCESS DEVICE

Carelessly broadcasting phone access codes or device numbers designed to be used to steal phone service.

The suspect, with criminal negligence, publishes (communicates to others) a telecommunications access device or counterfeit device that is designed to be used to commit theft of telecommunications service.

  • Class A Misdemeanor – base grade
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree – if the suspect has a prior conviction under Chapter 33A
§33A.051 FALSE CALLER IDENTIFICATION INFORMATION DISPLAY ▾

§33A.051 – FALSE CALLER IDENTIFICATION INFORMATION DISPLAY

Spoofing caller ID — using technology to make a call appear to come from someone else, with intent to defraud or cause harm.

The suspect, with intent to defraud or cause harm, makes a call or engages in conduct using any technology that causes another person's device to display caller ID data that misrepresents the suspect's identity or phone number.

  • Class A Misdemeanor – base and only grade

Defenses: Blocking caller ID entirely (not falsifying it); peace officers or federal agents lawfully on duty; federal intelligence/security agency personnel on duty; telecom providers acting in their normal service capacity; private investigators lawfully conducting a licensed investigation.

Important note: A conviction under this section cannot be used to enhance any other offense under Chapter 33A.

Chapter 30: Burglary and Criminal Trespass

§30.02 §30.03 §30.04 §30.05 §30.06 §30.07
§30.02 BURGLARY ▾

§30.02 – BURGLARY

Breaking into or entering a building or home without permission, with intent to commit a crime inside — or committing a crime once inside.

A person commits burglary without the owner's effective consent if they:

  • Enter a habitation, or a building not open to the public, with intent to commit a felony, theft, or assault, OR
  • Remain concealed in a building or habitation with intent to commit a felony, theft, or assault, OR
  • Enter a building or habitation and actually commit or attempt a felony, theft, or assault.

"Enter" includes any intrusion of any part of the body or any physical object connected to the body (e.g., reaching a tool through a window).

Punishment

  • State Jail Felony — burglary of a building other than a habitation (e.g., a store or office).
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if the premises are a commercial building where controlled substances are generally stored (pharmacy, clinic, hospital) and the suspect entered with intent to steal controlled substances; OR if committed during human smuggling (§20.05(a)(2)).
  • Felony of the 2nd Degree — burglary of a habitation (a home or dwelling).
  • Felony of the 1st Degree — burglary of a habitation where any party entered with intent to commit a felony other than felony theft, or actually committed/attempted such a felony (e.g., sexual assault, kidnapping).
§30.03 BURGLARY OF COIN-OPERATED OR COIN COLLECTION MACHINES ▾

§30.03 – BURGLARY OF COIN-OPERATED OR COIN COLLECTION MACHINES

Breaking into vending machines, parking meters, arcade games, or other coin-operated devices to steal from them.

A person commits this offense if, without the owner's effective consent, they break or enter into any coin-operated machine, coin collection machine, or other coin-operated or coin collection device used for amusement, sales, services, or telecommunications — with intent to obtain property or services.

Every kind of entry counts except entry with the owner's effective consent.

Punishment

  • Class A Misdemeanor
§30.04 BURGLARY OF VEHICLES ▾

§30.04 – BURGLARY OF VEHICLES

Breaking into or entering a vehicle without permission, with intent to commit theft or a felony inside.

A person commits this offense if, without the owner's effective consent, they break into or enter a vehicle (or any part of a vehicle) with intent to commit any felony or theft. "Enter" includes any part of the body or any object connected to the body.

Punishment

  • Class A Misdemeanor — base offense.
  • Class A Misdemeanor with minimum 6 months confinement — one prior conviction under this section.
  • State Jail Felony — two or more prior convictions; OR the vehicle is a rail car.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — the vehicle belongs to a wholesale drug distributor and the intent was to steal controlled substances; OR committed during human smuggling (§20.05(a)(2)).

Defense

Railroad employees or union representatives exercising rights under the Railway Labor Act are exempt from the "entering a rail car" provision.

§30.05 CRIMINAL TRESPASS ▾

§30.05 – CRIMINAL TRESPASS

Entering or staying on someone's property after being told not to — or when signs, fences, or other notice clearly indicate entry is forbidden.

A person commits criminal trespass if they enter or remain on property without effective consent, and either:

  • Had notice that entry was forbidden, OR
  • Received notice to depart but refused.

"Entry" means the intrusion of the entire body (unlike burglary, where any body part counts).

"Notice" can be: oral or written communication; fencing; posted signs; purple paint marks on trees or posts (specific size/placement requirements); or a visible growing crop.

Punishment

  • Class C Misdemeanor (max $200 fine) — entering with a firearm where the only prohibition is "no firearms," and the person did not receive personal oral notice and fail to leave.
  • Class C Misdemeanor — on agricultural land within 100 feet of the boundary, or on residential land within 100 feet of a protected freshwater area.
  • Class B Misdemeanor — standard/base offense.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — if committed in a habitation or shelter center; on a Superfund site; on or in a critical infrastructure facility; while carrying a deadly weapon; on a residential treatment center property; or repeat offense on a college campus.
  • Felony of the 3rd Degree — if committed during human smuggling (§20.05(a)(2)).

Notable Defenses

  • Firefighters and EMS personnel acting in discharge of duty under exigent circumstances.
  • Utility workers, pipeline employees, or telecom workers performing duties within scope of employment.
  • License holders (LTC) carrying a concealed or holstered handgun when the only prohibition is "no handguns" (separate defenses exist for condo owners, tenants, and hotel guests).
  • Peace officers are completely exempt from prosecution if the sole basis for the prohibition is weapons — whether on or off duty.
  • For critical infrastructure facilities: defendant can raise the issue of peaceful/lawful assembly or labor rights.
§30.06 TRESPASS BY LICENSE HOLDER WITH A CONCEALED HANDGUN ▾

§30.06 – TRESPASS BY LICENSE HOLDER WITH A CONCEALED HANDGUN

A license-to-carry holder entering private property with a concealed handgun after being properly notified that concealed carry is prohibited there.

A license holder (someone with a Texas LTC) commits this offense if they carry a concealed handgun on someone else's property without consent, and received notice that concealed carry by a license holder is forbidden. Notice must be by:

  • Oral or written communication from the owner or authorized person, OR
  • The specific §30.06 sign (posted in both English and Spanish with block letters at least 1 inch high, conspicuously visible).

Punishment

  • Class C Misdemeanor (max $200 fine) — base offense.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — if, after entering, the license holder was personally given oral notice to leave and then failed to depart.

Notable Defenses

  • Property owned or leased by a governmental entity that doesn't otherwise prohibit carry under §46.03.
  • Condominium owners/tenants, apartment tenants, manufactured home lot tenants, and hotel guests carrying in their own unit, room, or vehicle on the property.
  • Volunteer emergency services personnel.
  • Certain first responders and tactical medical professionals with proper certification.
  • License holder who was given oral notice and promptly departed.
§30.07 TRESPASS BY LICENSE HOLDER WITH AN OPENLY CARRIED HANDGUN ▾

§30.07 – TRESPASS BY LICENSE HOLDER WITH AN OPENLY CARRIED HANDGUN

A license-to-carry holder entering private property with a handgun carried openly (in a holster) after being properly notified that open carry is prohibited there.

A license holder commits this offense if they openly carry a handgun on another's property without consent, and received notice that open carry by a license holder is forbidden. Notice must be by:

  • Oral or written communication from the owner or authorized person, OR
  • The specific §30.07 sign (posted in both English and Spanish with block letters at least 1 inch high, conspicuously visible at each entrance).

Punishment

  • Class C Misdemeanor (max $200 fine) — base offense.
  • Class A Misdemeanor — if, after entering, the license holder was personally given oral notice to leave and then failed to depart.

Notable Points

  • It is NOT a defense that the handgun was carried in a holster (unlike the concealed-carry provision).
  • Same defenses as §30.06 apply: condo/apartment/hotel residents carrying in their own space, volunteer emergency services personnel, certain first responders, and a license holder who promptly departed after oral notice.
  • Property owned/leased by a government entity (not prohibited under §46.03) is exempt.

§48.01 SMOKING TOBACCO ▾

§48.01 – SMOKING TOBACCO

Smoking or using an e-cigarette in certain protected public facilities — schools and enclosed public spaces.

A person commits this offense if they possess a burning tobacco product, smoke tobacco, or operate an e-cigarette in any of the following locations:

  • A public primary or secondary school facility
  • An elevator
  • An enclosed theater or movie house
  • A library or museum
  • A hospital
  • A transit system bus, intrastate bus, plane, or train that qualifies as a public place

Grade: Class C Misdemeanor

Defenses

  • The location did not have a prominently displayed notice stating that smoking is prohibited by state law (with the fine amount)
  • The location was not equipped with facilities for extinguishing smoking materials
  • The person was in a designated smoking or e-cigarette area, or was participating as an actor in an authorized theatrical performance

Note: Any designated smoking area on a transit bus or intrastate plane or train must also include the area occupied by the operator of that vehicle.

§48.015 PROHIBITIONS RELATING TO CERTAIN CIGARETTES ▾

§48.015 – PROHIBITIONS RELATING TO CERTAIN CIGARETTES

Trafficking non-compliant or improperly stamped cigarettes into Texas for sale or distribution.

A person may not acquire, hold, own, possess, or transport cigarettes for sale or distribution in Texas — or import them into Texas for sale or distribution — if those cigarettes:

  • Do not comply with all applicable federal requirements and regulations, OR
  • Are not eligible to have Texas tax stamps affixed to them under the Tax Code — unless they are lawfully imported for personal use or are lawfully sold duty-free by a duty-free enterprise

A person who commits this act, knowing or having reason to know they are doing so, is guilty of:

Grade: Class A Misdemeanor

§48.02 PROHIBITION OF THE PURCHASE AND SALE OF HUMAN ORGANS ▾

§48.02 – PROHIBITION OF THE PURCHASE AND SALE OF HUMAN ORGANS

Buying or selling human organs for money — treating a body part as a commercial commodity.

A person commits this offense if they knowingly or intentionally offer to buy, offer to sell, acquire, receive, sell, or otherwise transfer any human organ for valuable consideration (i.e., payment or other compensation).

"Human organ" includes: kidney, liver, heart, lung, pancreas, eye, bone, skin, or any other human organ or tissue. It does not include hair, blood, blood components, plasma, blood derivatives, blood reagents, or human fetal tissue (covered separately in §48.03).

Grade: State Jail Felony

Exceptions (Not a Crime)

  • A fee paid to a physician or other medical personnel for services rendered in the usual course of medical practice, or fees for hospital or clinical services
  • Reimbursement of legal or medical expenses incurred for the benefit of the organ recipient
  • Reimbursement of the organ donor's expenses for travel, housing, and lost wages directly connected to the donation
§48.03 PROHIBITION ON PURCHASE AND SALE OF HUMAN FETAL TISSUE ▾

§48.03 – PROHIBITION ON PURCHASE AND SALE OF HUMAN FETAL TISSUE

Buying or selling human fetal tissue for economic gain.

A person commits this offense if they knowingly offer to buy, offer to sell, acquire, receive, sell, or otherwise transfer any human fetal tissue for economic benefit.

Grade: State Jail Felony

Defense

It is a defense if the person is an employee of or under contract with an accredited public or private university or college AND acquired or transferred the fetal tissue solely to fulfill a donation specifically authorized under Health and Safety Code §173.005.

Exceptions (Not a Crime)

  • Fetal tissue acquired, received, or transferred solely for diagnostic or pathological testing
  • Fetal tissue acquired, received, or transferred solely for purposes of a criminal investigation
  • Fetal tissue acquired solely for lawful disposal in accordance with state law
  • Fetal tissue or human tissue acquired during pregnancy or at delivery by an accredited college or university for use in research approved by an institutional review board (IRB)
  • Cell lines derived from fetal tissue existing before September 1, 2017, used by an accredited institution in IRB-approved research

Note: The Attorney General has concurrent jurisdiction with local prosecutors to pursue this offense (with the local prosecutor's consent).

§48.04 PROHIBITION ON PURCHASE AND SALE OF ADULT STEM CELLS FOR CERTAIN INVESTIGATIONAL TREATMENTS ▾

§48.04 – PROHIBITION ON PURCHASE AND SALE OF ADULT STEM CELLS FOR CERTAIN INVESTIGATIONAL TREATMENTS

Selling or buying adult stem cells for use in unapproved, experimental treatments.

A person commits this offense if they knowingly offer to buy, offer to sell, acquire, receive, sell, or otherwise transfer any adult stem cells for valuable consideration (payment) for use in an investigational stem cell treatment — meaning a stem cell treatment that is still in clinical trials and has not yet been approved by the FDA for general use.

An "adult stem cell" is an undifferentiated cell found in specialized body tissue that can renew itself and produce the cell types of the tissue it came from.

Grade: Class A Misdemeanor

Exceptions (Not a Crime)

  • A fee paid to a physician or other medical personnel for services in the usual course of medical practice, or hospital/clinical fees
  • Reimbursement of legal or medical expenses incurred for the benefit of the treatment recipient
  • Reimbursement of the donor's travel, housing, and lost wages related to the stem cell donation
  • Conduct authorized under Health and Safety Code Chapter 162 (relating to blood banks and tissue banks)
§48.05 PROHIBITED CAMPING ▾

§48.05 – PROHIBITED CAMPING

Setting up an unauthorized camp in a public place.

A person commits this offense if they intentionally or knowingly camp in a public place without the effective consent of the officer or agency legally responsible for managing that public place.

"Camp" means to reside temporarily in a place with shelter. "Shelter" includes tents, tarps, lean-tos, sleeping bags, bedrolls, blankets, or any temporary or semipermanent structure designed to protect against weather — but not clothing or hand-held devices.

A suspect's intent or knowledge can be established through evidence of activities such as:

  • Cooking or making a fire
  • Storing personal belongings for an extended period
  • Digging
  • Sleeping

Grade: Class C Misdemeanor

When Government Consent Is Valid

Consent from a government agency or officer is only effective if the camping is authorized for:

  • Recreational purposes
  • Sheltering homeless individuals under a state-approved housing plan
  • Purposes permitted by an approved beach access plan
  • Emergency shelter during a state or local declared disaster

Officer Duties Before Issuing a Citation

Before or at the time of issuing a citation, the peace officer must make a reasonable effort to:

  • Advise the person of an alternative lawful place to camp
  • Contact an appropriate government official or nonprofit organization and request that they provide the person with services — including human trafficking prevention information — that might reduce the likelihood of continued camping

This requirement is waived if there is an imminent threat to anyone's health or safety.

Property Protection: If a person is arrested or detained solely for this offense, the officer must ensure all personal property (not designated as contraband) is preserved — either by allowing the person to take it, or by taking it into custody for later retrieval. No storage or release fee may be charged.

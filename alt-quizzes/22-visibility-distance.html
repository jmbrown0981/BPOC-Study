Vehicle Lighting & Reflectors — Required Visibility Distance

Every distance Transportation Code Chapter 547 (and Chapter 551 for bicycles) requires a lamp or reflector to be seen from, grouped by vehicle type: motor vehicles, bicycles, and trailers.

How to read this: Transportation Code §547.301(a) says that unless a section states otherwise, a visibility-distance requirement is measured with the vehicle unloaded, on a straight, level, unlighted highway, under normal atmospheric conditions. A brass bar with an arrow is an open-ended minimum — the device must be visible from at least that many feet, and more is always fine. A navy bar is a true range — the device (almost always a reflector) must be visible at every distance between the two ends, no more and no less, when directly in front of headlamp beams of the stated type. Unlike the companion Height & Year Exceptions chart, there is no federal comparison series here: FMVSS 108 (49 C.F.R. §571.108) measures lamp and reflector performance photometrically (candela at specified test angles), not as a plain-language visibility distance, so no federal "feet" figure exists to place side-by-side with the Texas figure. For offense classifications and fines, see the Offense & Penalty Reference.

Exemption to note: §547.304 exempts a farm trailer or fertilizer trailer registered under §502.146, and a boat trailer with a gross weight of 3,000 lb or less, from the fixed-electric-light requirements of this chapter entirely — unless it's being operated at a time or under a condition described by §547.302(a) (darkness, dusk/dawn, or a person/vehicle not clearly discernible at 1,000 ft). No visibility-distance figure applies to these trailers outside those conditions.

Child (Family Code 51.02)	A person 10 or older and under 17, or 17 and under 18 if alleged/found to have engaged in delinquent conduct or CINS before turning 17
Custodian	The adult with whom the child resides
Guardian	The person who, under court order, is the guardian of the person of the child, or the public/private agency the child has been placed with by a court
Judge / Juvenile Court Judge	The judge of a juvenile court
Juvenile Court	A court designated under FC 51.04 to exercise jurisdiction over Title 3 proceedings
Nonoffender	A child subject to a court's jurisdiction under abuse/dependency/neglect statutes for reasons other than the child's own conduct, or held solely for deportation
Parent (Family Code 51.02)	The mother or father of a child, not including a parent whose parental rights have been terminated
Referral to Juvenile Court	Referral of a child or the child's case to the office or official (intake or probation officer) designated by the juvenile board to process children in the juvenile justice system
Res Gestae	Latin for "things done"; excited utterances made under the stress of excitement, admissible even without Miranda-style warnings because they weren't the product of interrogation
Secure Correctional Facility	A facility with movement-restricting construction fixtures used for the placement of a juvenile already ADJUDICATED as having committed an offense (post-adjudication), a nonoffender, or a convicted individual
Secure Detention Facility	A facility with movement-restricting construction fixtures used for the TEMPORARY placement of a juvenile only ACCUSED of an offense (pre-adjudication), a nonoffender, or an accused individual
Status Offender	A child accused, adjudicated, or convicted for conduct that would not be a crime if committed by an adult (e.g., running away, violating student conduct standards, minors-only alcohol violations)
Traffic Offense (Family Code 51.02)	A penal statute violation under Transportation Code Ch. 729 (except conduct punishable by imprisonment/jail) or a municipal traffic ordinance violation
Valid Court Order	An FC 54.04 order concerning a child adjudicated to have engaged in conduct indicating a need for supervision as a status offender
Delinquent Conduct	Conduct (other than a traffic offense) that violates a penal law punishable by imprisonment/jail confinement, certain contempt-of-court conduct, PC 49.04-49.08 violations, or a 3rd+ DUI-minor offense under AB Code 106.041
Conduct Indicating a Need for Supervision (CINS)	Fine-only misdemeanor/ordinance violations, voluntary unauthorized absence from home, certain inhalant offenses, student-conduct expulsion conduct, and specified PC violations not otherwise delinquent conduct
Directive to Apprehend	A juvenile court order, issued on request of a law enforcement/probation officer upon a probable cause finding, directing that a child be taken into custody — the juvenile-system analog to an adult arrest warrant
Adjudication	A juvenile court's finding that a child engaged in delinquent conduct or CINS — the juvenile-system analog to an adult conviction
Disposition Hearing	The juvenile-system analog to an adult sentencing hearing
Detention Hearing (analog)	The juvenile-system analog to an adult arraignment
Petition (juvenile)	The juvenile-system analog to an adult indictment or information
Respondent (juvenile)	The juvenile-system analog to an adult defendant
Plea of True / Not True	The juvenile-system analog to an adult guilty/not guilty plea
Juvenile Processing Office	A designated office/room (not a cell) for the temporary detention of a child, limited to specific purposes and capped at 6 hours (FC 52.025)
Place of Nonsecure Custody	An unlocked, multipurpose area (lobby, office, interview room, or unlocked juvenile processing office) where a child may not be secured to a stationary object; capped at 6 hours (CCP 45A.453)
First Offender Program	A juvenile-board-established program (FC 52.031) for referring/disposing of lower-level offenders without court referral, requiring child and parent consent and juvenile-board guidelines
Youth Diversion Coordinator	A person a court may designate (CCP Art. 45A.507) to determine diversion eligibility, employ diversion strategies, and monitor diversion agreements
Abuse (Family Code 261.001)	Mental/emotional injury causing observable impairment; substantial-harm physical injury; harmful sexual conduct; compelling sexual conduct; forcing marriage; and related acts/omissions by a person responsible for or around a child
Neglect (Family Code 261.001)	An act or failure to act by a person responsible for a child's care, custody, or welfare evidencing blatant disregard for the consequences, resulting in harm or immediate danger to the child's physical health/safety
Person Responsible for a Child's Care, Custody, or Welfare	A parent, guardian, conservator, foster parent, household member, parent's cohabitant, school personnel/volunteer, or child-care/residential facility staff or volunteer
Severe Emotional Disturbance (FC 261.001)	A mental, behavioral, or emotional disorder of sufficient duration to result in functional impairment substantially interfering with family, school, or community functioning
Child Advocacy Center (CAC)	A center that coordinates a multidisciplinary team response to child abuse/neglect allegations under an interagency MOU with CPS, law enforcement, and prosecuting attorneys (FC 264.403)
Emergency Possession Without a Court Order (FC 262.104)	Taking possession of a child without a court order based on personal knowledge or corroborated information of immediate danger, sexual abuse, trafficking, or a controlled-substance/meth-manufacture danger — never on a non-examining medical professional's opinion alone
Nonsecure Custody Procedures	A child in nonsecure custody may not be cuffed to a stationary object, held more than 6 hours, housed residentially, or left without continuous visual supervision
Place of Detention (CCP 2B.0201)	A police station or similar law-enforcement-operated building used to detain individuals suspected of a penal-law violation; excludes a courthouse
Electronic Recording of Custodial Interrogation (CCP 2B.0202)	Required for custodial interrogations, occurring in a place of detention, of persons suspected of one of 12 listed serious offenses (murder, capital murder, kidnapping, trafficking, sexual offenses, etc.), absent good cause
Abducted Child (Government Code 411.351)	A child 17 or younger whose whereabouts are unknown and whose disappearance poses a credible threat to safety/health, as determined by local law enforcement — the AMBER Alert age threshold
AMBER Alert System	America's Missing: Broadcast Emergency Response — the statewide alert system (administered by DPS) for abducted children and missing persons with an intellectual disability
Clearinghouse (CCP 63.001)	The state's missing children and missing persons information clearinghouse — the centralized statewide hub for missing-person reporting
Interference with Child Custody (PC 25.03)	Taking or retaining a child under 18 knowing it violates a court custody order, or during a pending custody suit without court permission, or internationally without permission
Agreement to Abduct from Custody (PC 25.031)	Agreeing, for remuneration, to abduct a child known to be under someone else's court-ordered care by force, threat, misrepresentation, stealth, or unlawful entry
Enticing a Child (PC 25.04)	Knowingly enticing, persuading, or taking a child under 18 from the custody of a parent/guardian with intent to interfere with lawful custody
Harboring Runaway Child (PC 25.06)	Knowingly harboring a child, with criminal negligence as to whether the child is under 18 and has escaped custody or is an unauthorized runaway
Hindering Apprehension or Prosecution (PC 38.05)	Harboring/concealing a person, aiding their escape, or warning them of impending discovery, with intent to hinder arrest, prosecution, or (for a child) detention/adjudication/disposition
Interference with Public Duties (PC 38.15)	Criminally negligent interruption, disruption, impediment, or interference with a peace officer or other listed public-duty personnel performing their duties
Failure to Stop or Report Sexual/Assaultive Offense Against a Child (PC 38.17)	Failing to assist a child or report an observed sexual/assaultive offense against a child when able to do so safely
Notification to Schools Required (CCP Art. 15.27)	Requires a law enforcement agency to notify a student's school superintendent within 24 hours (or before the next school day) of an arrest or juvenile referral for felony or listed misdemeanor offenses
Gang Involvement — Five Risk Domains	Individual, Family, School, Peers, and Community — the five domains BPOC identifies as risk factors for youth gang involvement (Peers is described as one of the strongest)
Consent to Treatment by Child (FC 32.003)	A child may consent to their own treatment if 16+ and living independently while managing their own finances, on active military duty, for certain infectious diseases, unmarried pregnancy care, drug-dependency treatment, as an unmarried parent consenting for their own child, or while TDCJ-confined
Examination Without Consent of Abuse or Neglect (FC 32.005)	A physician, dentist, or psychologist with reasonable grounds to believe abuse/neglect may examine a child without consent (including X-rays, blood tests, photographs), except a refusing child 16+ or a court-ordered prohibition

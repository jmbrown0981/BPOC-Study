TC 502–504 Standard & Specialty License Plates — Untouched by HB 718 ▾

These plate categories existed before HB 718 and were not part of its 2023/2025 overhaul — that bill only touched temporary tags, permits, and the point-of-sale process. They're included here because "valid Texas license plates" has to start with the plates that make up the vast majority of traffic.

PlateValidityStatusCitationSample legal-use scenario
General Issue (Standard) PlateAny privately owned passenger vehicle, truck, or trailer 12 months — a staggered registration year TxDMV assigns to spread renewal workload Valid TC §502.044 A trooper runs a plate on a routine stop and TLETS shows registration current through next April — the plate itself doesn't expire; only the annual registration behind it does, per its own designated 12-month cycle.
Specialty PlateCause, collegiate, or organization design purchased through the state's specialty-plate program Same annual cycle as the vehicle's registration, plus a specialty fee Valid TC §504.851 A vehicle displays a university-alumni design; the officer treats it exactly like a standard plate for registration-status purposes — the artwork carries no special renewal rule of its own.
Personalized ("Vanity") PlateCustom alphanumeric pattern, subject to DPS/DMV approval Same annual cycle as registration Valid TC §504.601; display: 43 TAC §217.27 A driver argues their approved pattern has an innocent personal meaning after a citizen complaint; 43 TAC §217.27 makes clear DPS/DMV needn't consider the owner's subjective intent when deciding whether a pattern is objectionable — the plate's registration status is unaffected either way.
Disabled Person PlateVehicle regularly used to transport a person with a qualifying permanent disability Same annual registration cycle — the statute sets no separate term Valid TC §504.201 A vehicle displays the international access symbol on a Disabled Person plate; the officer confirms current registration through TLETS exactly as with any standard plate.
Veterans with Disabilities Plate50%+ (or 40%+ with lower-extremity amputation) service-connected disabled veteran; one vehicle ≤18,000 lb or a motor home 1 year (the statute states this explicitly) Valid TC §504.202 A veteran renews Veterans with Disabilities plates annually; the disability designation doesn't extend the registration term beyond the one-year cycle the statute itself sets. (2021's SB 792 later let this plate also carry the access symbol and unlock a placard without extra paperwork — see the chapter's 2026 Update — New House & Senate Bills topic.)
Antique PlateVehicle 25+ years old; exhibition, club, and parade use only — not for regular transportation 5-year fixed term — expires on the 5th anniversary of issuance Valid TC §504.502 A 1978 pickup with a current Antique plate is stopped on a Tuesday commute to the grocery store. The plate's own 5-year registration may still be running, but the vehicle's actual use already violates the same section's "exhibition/club use only, not regular transportation" restriction — a separate problem from whether the plate itself is current (offense: TC §504.941).
Classic / Custom Vehicle / Street Rod PlateClassic & Custom Vehicle: 25+ years old; Street Rod: pre-1949 or built to resemble one Same annual registration cycle — §504.501 sets no separate multi-year term Valid TC §504.501 A modified pre-1949-style roadster displays a Street Rod plate; the officer confirms annual registration the same way as any standard plate, since Chapter 504 doesn't give this category the multi-year term it gives Antique plates.
Dealer's (GDN) License PlateNon-temporary metal plate assigned to a GDN-holding dealership — not the HB 718 Dealer Temporary Plate below Tied to the dealership's General Distinguishing Number (GDN) license expiration Valid TC §503.061; use limits §503.068 A dealership employee is stopped driving a dealer-plated vehicle to a personal appointment unrelated to the business. §503.068 limits dealer plates to inventory-related business use, so this is an improper-use violation even though the plate's own registration is fully current. HB 718's guidance confirms these standard metal dealer plates were unaffected by the 2023/2025 changes — only the paper temporary tags were repealed.
TC 681 Disabled Parking Placards ▾

Not a license plate, but the other credential officers routinely check alongside a Disabled Person or Veterans with Disabilities plate — and one SB 792 (2021) directly modified.

PlacardValidityStatusCitationSample legal-use scenario
Permanent (Blue) PlacardPermanent mobility or visual disability 4 years, renewable Valid TC §681.004(a) A blue placard's printed expiration is three years out — no issue; it stays valid until that printed date, up to the 4-year maximum.
Temporary (Red) PlacardTemporary disability, per the certifying physician Up to 6 months Interim TC §681.004(a) A red placard was issued eight months ago with no renewal on file — past its 6-month statutory ceiling, it should be treated as expired regardless of what date is printed on it.
SB 792 (87th Legislature, 2021) let a Veterans with Disabilities plate under §504.202(b-1) also carry the access symbol, which then qualifies the holder for a §681.004 placard for each set of plates — without the extra medical documentation a stand-alone Disabled Person plate application would otherwise require. HB 718 HB 718 (2023) — What Changed, Effective July 1, 2025 ▾

House Bill 718, enacted by the 88th Legislature (2023), targeted the fraudulent use of paper temporary tags and permits by eliminating six paper documents in favor of new metal temporary license plates and a faster point-of-sale process. Its provisions and the associated DPS/DMV administrative rules took effect July 1, 2025.

Because tags issued before the effective date remain valid until their own printed expiration, officers can still legitimately encounter a repealed paper document for a short window after 7/1/25 — the table below gives each document's own grace period.

7/1/2025Dealers stop issuing all six paper documents; the new plates and process take effect.
7/15/2025Paper One-Trip Permits should not be seen as valid beyond this date.
7/30/2025Paper 30-Day Permits should not be seen as valid beyond this date.
~8/31/2025Last date a legitimate Buyer's Tag (60-day expiration) could still be running, if issued right at the 7/1/25 cutoff.

Source: TxDPS Transportation Code Training Program, HB 718 Guidance.

REPEALED The Six Repealed Paper Documents ▾

None of these are issued anymore. A genuine one can only be a holdover issued before 7/1/25 that hasn't yet reached its own printed expiration — anything past that date, or any of these issued fresh today, is fraudulent.

DocumentOld validityStatusReplaced bySample scenario
30-Day PermitPassenger vehicles, private buses, trailers/semitrailers ≤10,000 lb GVW — unladen only if heavier 30 days; sunset ~7/30/25 Repealed Temporary Registration Plate A 30-Day Permit dated June 20, 2025 hasn't reached its printed expiration when stopped in early July — still valid on its own terms. The same permit displayed on a laden 12,000-lb commercial vehicle was never a valid use to begin with, permit date aside.
One-Trip PermitSingle point-to-point trip that must begin or end in Texas, ≤15 days 15 days max; sunset ~7/15/25 Repealed Temporary Registration Plate A stop reveals a purely intrastate trip (neither begins nor ends in Texas) already 20 days old — unregistered on two independent grounds: the origin/destination rule and the 15-day cap, permit-repeal timing aside.
Buyer's TagPaper tag a dealer issued directly to a buyer at time of sale 60 days; last legitimate ones ran through ~8/31/25 Repealed Two standard metal plates at sale (TC §503.063) A dealer stopped issuing Buyer's Tags on 7/1/25, but one issued 6/29/25 is still technically within its 60-day window into late August — an officer checks the printed issue date rather than assuming every Buyer's Tag is automatically fraudulent.
Internet Down TagPaper fallback dealers used when TxDMV's online Inventory Management System (IMS) was unreachable No longer issued in any form Repealed Two standard metal plates, issued anyway IMS access is down at the exact moment of a sale. The dealer doesn't wait or fall back to paper — two standard plates go on the vehicle immediately, the sale is entered once IMS access returns, and the buyer gets a form explaining the temporary IMS/TLETS gap.
Dealer/Converter Temp Tags & Authorized Agent TagsThree named paper variants: Dealer Vehicle Specific, Authorized Agent, and Converter tags Varied by variant; none valid as fresh issuance after 7/1/25 Repealed Dealer Temporary Plate A vehicle in dealer inventory previously would have worn one of these three paper tags for test drives or transit; today it wears a single metal Dealer Temporary Plate instead, whichever of the three former uses applies.

HB 718 Guidance counts these as "six paper documents" (30-Day, One-Trip, Buyer's Tag, Internet Down, and the two dealer/converter/authorized-agent variants) — the last row above bundles three named tag types under one replacement since they all became the same new plate.

NEW New HB 718 Metal Plates & the Point-of-Sale Replacement ▾
PlateValidityStatusCitationSample legal-use scenario
Two General-Issue Plates (at sale)What a dealer now attaches to every sold vehicle instead of a Buyer's Tag Standard 12-month registration begins at the sale Valid TC §503.063; receipt §503.0631(d-2); insignia §502.059 TLETS shows registration already processed on a newly purchased vehicle, but no windshield insignia is displayed yet — that's reasonable suspicion for a stop. If TLETS instead shows registration not yet processed, no enforcement action is warranted for the missing insignia; the buyer's receipt (carried per §503.0631(d-2)) covers that gap.
Temporary Registration PlateReplaces the One-Trip & 30-Day Permits; one plate, rear only; county tax office or TxDMV regional service center One-trip variant: ≤15 days, must begin or end in TX. 30-day-style variant: ≤30 days, unladen only if a CMV >10,000 lb GVW Interim TC §502.095; 43 TAC §217.40 A cross-country move uses the one-trip variant, point to point, beginning in Texas, completed well inside 15 days. A separate local hauler uses the 30-day variant on a light truck under 10,000 lb GVW — the moment that truck carries a load over that weight, the plate's valid-use condition is broken even though the plate itself hasn't expired.
Buyer Provisional PlateBridges the gap when a dealer doesn't yet have the buyer's ordered specialty/personalized plate in stock Valid until the permanent specialty plate is assigned, plus 7 more days, then returned to the dealer Interim HB 718 Guidance A buyer orders Veterans with Disabilities plates at purchase, but the dealer doesn't have them in stock yet; the dealer issues a Buyer Provisional Plate to cover the interim, then swaps it for the permanent plates and windshield insignia once they arrive — the provisional plate goes back to the dealer, not to the buyer as a keepsake.
Out-of-State Buyer PlateBuyer lives out of state and won't register the vehicle in Texas; one plate, rear only 60 days Interim HB 718 Guidance A Texas dealer sells to an Oklahoma resident who will title and register the vehicle at home; the dealer issues a single Out-of-State Buyer Plate for the drive home. If the buyer is still on it past 60 days with no other registration obtained, the vehicle should be considered unregistered.
Dealer Temporary PlateDealer's own business-use plate — test drives, loaners, transit, road testing, charitable use Synced to the dealership's GDN license expiration (shown by stickers) Valid TC §503.062, §503.0671 A dealership loans a customer a vehicle with a Dealer Temporary Plate while the customer's own car is being repaired — an approved use. A dealer or employee using the same plate for a personal errand, or on a vehicle already sold, a rental/leased vehicle, or a laden commercial vehicle, is a prohibited use even though the plate's own registration is current.
SURVIVED Paper Documents Still Valid Today — Not Repealed by HB 718 ▾

HB 718 eliminated six paper documents but left these four in place — all still issued on paper, all still fully legal to encounter today.

DocumentValidityStatusCitationSample legal-use scenario
72-Hour PermitUS/Canada/Mexico resident; commercial motor vehicle, trailer, semitrailer, or bus with no TX registration or reciprocity 72 hours from the date/time on the receipt ($25 fee) Interim TC §502.094 A Canadian-registered semitrailer without U.S. reciprocity authority crosses into Texas; the driver buys a 72-Hour Permit to legally reposition it before the window runs out.
144-Hour PermitSame eligible parties/vehicles as the 72-Hour Permit, longer window 144 hours from the date/time on the receipt ($50 fee) Interim TC §502.094 The same kind of unregistered commercial vehicle needs a longer repositioning trip than 72 hours allows; the 144-Hour Permit covers it instead, at the higher $50 fee.
Texas Temporary InsigniaIssued when a vehicle is assigned a personalized or specialty plate (e.g., Veterans with Disabilities) not yet physically received Valid until the owner receives the actual plates Valid HB 718 Guidance (functions within the general registration-insignia framework of TC §502.059) A veteran orders Veterans with Disabilities plates; until they physically arrive, the vehicle displays the Texas Temporary Insignia instead of blank plate slots — an officer treats this as the vehicle's valid interim registration proof, just like the standard windshield insignia.
Vehicle Transit Permit ReceiptPrivate-party sale where the seller keeps their own plates; ≤6,000 lb passenger vehicle or ≤10,000 lb GVW light truck 5 days, single trip, point of origin to destination; purchased and printed online Interim 43 TAC §217.53(c) A buyer purchases a car from a private seller who keeps their own plates on sale. The buyer prints a Vehicle Transit Permit Receipt online and drives the vehicle home within the 5-day, single-trip window — the same document that lets a private sale close without either party needing a dealer's paperwork.
FLIER Spotting a Fake or Invalid Plate ▾

From MyPlates.com's "How to Spot a Fake Plate in Texas" flier — the visual tells that separate a genuine plate from a counterfeit, independent of whatever plate number it displays.

  • Security thread: since August 2012, every genuine Texas plate has had two vertical threads joining the existing round holograms.
  • Correct size: passenger, truck, and trailer plates are 6×12 in.; motorcycle plates are 4×7 in. Any other size is either fake or a legitimate vintage plate issued before 1956 — Texas has never issued European-sized plates.
  • Reflective white characters: genuine designs use reflective white alphanumeric characters that contrast against the plate's background color; a color-on-color pattern (e.g., red characters on a black background) is a common fake.
  • Lone Star series character limit: the large-star Lone Star series (black, blue, pink, silver, white, red) holds a maximum of 6 characters — a 7-character pattern on that design is fake.
  • A correct plate number doesn't make a fake plate legal: a novelty/fake plate design printed with the vehicle's own correct, registered plate number is still illegal.
  • The offenses: TC §504.946, Deceptively Similar License Plate (felony 3rd degree to manufacture/sell, Class C to possess), and TC §502.4755, Deceptively Similar Insignia, are the parallel offenses for a fake plate and a fake registration sticker respectively.

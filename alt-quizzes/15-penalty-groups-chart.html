How to read this: manufacture, delivery, and possession-with-intent-to-deliver a controlled substance are all graded on the same weight ladder within a penalty group — the law doesn't grade "making" it differently from "selling" it or "holding it to sell." Simple possession (no intent to deliver) uses a separate, generally lower ladder. Every ladder below is keyed to aggregate weight, including adulterants and dilutants (cutting agents count), except Penalty Group 1-A (measured in individual dosage/"abuse units") and Penalty Group 2-A possession (measured in ounces/pounds, not grams — the one outlier). This is a study aid, not legal advice — always verify against current statute before relying on a specific number. Several Chapter 481 offenses go past the standard Felony 1st Degree range at the top of their ladder — a Ch.481-specific enhanced minimum (10, 15, or 20 years instead of 5) and a raised maximum fine ($100,000–$500,000 instead of $10,000). Those "enhanced 1st degree" tiers are marked F1 ENHANCED in the ladders below.

PG 1-A Penalty Group 1-A — HSC 481.1021 ▾

What's in it: LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide) and the "NBOMe" family of structurally related synthetic hallucinogens (25I-NBOMe and similar compounds), by name and by a defined chemical-substitution test. The whole group is measured in individual dosage ("abuse") units, not weight — the one penalty group that works this way.

Notice possession has five tiers where manufacture/delivery only has four — the possession ladder splits the top of the range into a plain Felony 1st Degree band (4,000–8,000 units) before the enhanced tier kicks in at 8,000+, while manufacture/delivery jumps straight from Felony 1st Degree to the enhanced tier at 4,000 units.

PG 1-B Penalty Group 1-B — HSC 481.1022 ▾

What's in it: Fentanyl and fentanyl analogs — carfentanil, sufentanil, alfentanil, remifentanil, acetylfentanyl, and others. Added in 2021 given the outsized overdose risk these synthetic opioids carry even in tiny quantities; possession uses the same ladder as Penalty Group 1 (481.115), but manufacture/delivery gets its own, harsher ladder that starts a full grade higher than PG1's.

Manufacture / Delivery / Possession-with-Intent — HSC 481.1123 (aggregate weight)

Compare the starting point: PG1 manufacture/delivery under 1 gram is only a state jail felony (481.112(b)), but the same weight of fentanyl under 481.1123(b) is already a felony of the third degree — two full grades higher at the bottom of the ladder. Simple possession of a PG1-B substance, though, is charged under 481.115 and uses the ordinary Penalty Group 1 possession ladder shown in the PG1 section above.

The PG2-A possession outlier: synthetic cannabinoids are bulky, low-potency-by-weight plant material, so the legislature measures PG2-A possession in ounces and pounds instead of grams — the only possession ladder on this whole chart that isn't gram-based. Manufacture/delivery of PG2-A, by contrast, is folded into the ordinary gram-based PG2/2-A ladder above.

PG3 and PG4 possession share the exact same weight breakpoints (28g / 200g / 400g) — the only difference is where each starts on the ladder: PG3 starts at a Class A misdemeanor, PG4 (the least severe group) starts one notch lower at Class B.

MJ Marijuana — Its Own Ladder (Not a Penalty Group) ▾

Marihuana isn't in Penalty Groups 1–4 at all — it gets its own dedicated offense sections, 481.120 (delivery) and 481.121 (possession), with their own weight ladder measured in ounces and pounds.

Delivery's quarter-ounce quirk: a quarter-ounce-or-less delivery is only a Class B misdemeanor if no payment changed hands, but jumps to a Class A misdemeanor the moment any remuneration is received — the amount doesn't change, only whether it was a sale. Don't confuse marijuana with hemp (Ag. Code 121.001): hemp is Cannabis sativa L. at 0.3% delta-9 THC or less by dry weight, and isn't a controlled substance at all.

DFZ Drug-Free Zone Enhancements — HSC 481.134 ▾

What it does: committing a qualifying manufacture/delivery/possession offense in a drug-free zone can (a) bump the offense grade up one step at the lower tiers, and/or (b) add 5 years to the minimum sentence and double the maximum fine at the higher tiers. The enhanced sentence may not run concurrently with any other criminal statute's punishment (481.134(h)).

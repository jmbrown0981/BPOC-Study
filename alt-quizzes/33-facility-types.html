Secure Correctional vs. Secure Detention — and Every Other Facility Type

BPOC specifically flags this distinction as one to reinforce with questions. Here's the head-to-head comparison, plus a walk through every custody/detention location a child can pass through and when each one applies.

Head-to-Head Custody Journey All Facility Types
The one-line rule: a Secure Detention Facility holds a child who is only accused — pending proceedings, pre-adjudication. A Secure Correctional Facility holds a child who has already been adjudicated — post-disposition. Both share nearly identical physical-security language ("construction fixtures designed to physically restrict... movements and activities") — that part does not distinguish them. The adjudication status of the child is what distinguishes them. FC 51.02(13)-(14)

A Child's Custody Journey — Where They Can Legally Be Held, In Order

Either pre- or post-adjudication use · Pre pre-adjudication only · Post post-adjudication only

Pre
1. Taken into custody
FC 52.01 · CCP 45A.453
Under a court order, the laws of arrest, an officer's probable cause, a probation officer's probable cause, or a directive to apprehend. This is not legally an "arrest," except for search/custody-validity purposes.
Pre
2. Juvenile Processing Office or Place of Nonsecure Custody
FC 52.025 · CCP 45A.453 · max 6 hours each
An unlocked, multipurpose area (lobby, office, interview room) — never a cell. Limited to identification, paperwork, authorized photographing/fingerprinting, required warnings, and taking certain statements. The child may not be cuffed to a stationary object, held residentially, or left without continuous visual supervision.
Pre
3a. Released to parent/guardian/custodian
FC 52.02(a)(1) · FC 53.02
The default outcome absent one of the statutory detention-warranting conditions (flight risk, no suitable supervision, danger to self/public, prior delinquency history, or the FC 53.02(f) firearm trigger).
Pre
3b. Secure Detention Facility (if not released)
FC 51.12(j) · FC 51.02(14)
Holds the child while still only accused, pending a detention hearing (2nd working day generally; 24 hours for status offenders/nonoffenders) and, if detained, pending adjudication. Must be sight-and-sound separated from adults. Detention orders cap at 10 working days each (15 in counties without a certified facility), renewable only through fresh hearings.
Either
3c. County jail / other facility (narrow exception)
FC 51.12(l) · FC 53.02(f)
Used only when no certified juvenile detention facility or 51.12(j) secure detention facility is available in-county or in an adjacent county, with strict sight-and-sound separation, no shared staff contact, and only in a non-metro county with annual judicial certification.
4. Adjudication Hearing
FC 54.03
The juvenile court determines whether the child engaged in the alleged delinquent conduct/CINS. This is the dividing line between "accused" and "adjudicated" — and therefore between Secure Detention and Secure Correctional facility eligibility.
Post
5. Secure Correctional Facility (if adjudicated and committed)
FC 51.12(c) · FC 51.02(13)
Now holds the child because they have been adjudicated as having committed an offense — e.g., commitment to the Texas Juvenile Justice Department under FC 54.04. This is a fundamentally different legal basis than the pre-adjudication detention facility, even though the physical security features may look similar.
Either
Nonsecure Correctional Facility (alternate track, pre-adjudication)
FC 51.12(j-1)
Despite the name "correctional," this is a pre-adjudication option under 51.12(j-1) — used when no certified secure detention facility is available, the facility is registered/certified and meets short-term detention standards, and the county juvenile board has designated it. Don't assume "correctional" always means post-adjudication — this is the one statutory exception to that pattern.

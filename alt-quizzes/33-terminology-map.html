Adult ↔ Juvenile Terms Court Process Analogs Juvenile-Term Definitions
Why this matters: the juvenile system deliberately avoids adult-court vocabulary — a child isn't "convicted," they're "adjudicated"; there's no "arrest warrant," there's a "directive to apprehend." Mixing up the two vocabularies in a report or in court testimony is a quick way to look like you don't know the system you're working in. Every juvenile-side term below traces back to a specific Family Code or CCP section — this isn't just relabeling, the underlying legal process is genuinely different (see the glossary below for each one).

Careful — adjudication is NOT legally a "conviction" in most cases. Under FC 51.13(a), an order of adjudication or disposition is generally not a conviction of crime and doesn't carry the civil disabilities a conviction would. There are narrow exceptions: FC 51.13(d) treats certain felony adjudications resulting in TJJD commitment as a final felony conviction only for repeat-offender punishment enhancement purposes (PC 12.42(a)-(c)(1), 12.425), and FC 51.13(b)(3) allows use in civil commitment proceedings under Health & Safety Code Ch. 841. Don't casually call a juvenile disposition a "conviction" in a report — it isn't one, with those narrow statutory exceptions.
GLOSSARY What Each Juvenile-Side Term Actually Means 14 terms ▾
Delinquent Conduct
Conduct (other than a traffic offense) that violates a penal law punishable by imprisonment/jail, certain contempt-of-court conduct, PC 49.04-49.08 (DWI-family offenses), or a 3rd+ DUI-minor offense under AB Code 106.041. FC 51.03(a)
Conduct Indicating a Need for Supervision (CINS)
Fine-only misdemeanor/ordinance violations, unauthorized absence from home ("running away"), certain inhalant/vape offenses, expellable student-conduct violations, and a short list of specific Penal Code sections. FC 51.03(b)
Directive to Apprehend
A juvenile court order, requested by a law enforcement or probation officer, directing that a child be taken into custody upon a probable cause finding. Not subject to appeal. FC 52.015
Juvenile Petition
The formal pleading that opens a delinquency/CINS proceeding in juvenile court — functionally the juvenile system's charging instrument.
Respondent
The child (or, in a discretionary-transfer proceeding against an adult for conduct committed as a juvenile, the person) against whom the juvenile petition is brought.
Adjudication
The juvenile court's finding, at the adjudication hearing, that the child engaged in delinquent conduct or CINS — not legally a "conviction" except in the narrow circumstances described in the callout above. FC 51.13
Disposition Hearing
The hearing (following adjudication) where the juvenile court decides the outcome/consequence — probation, commitment to TJJD, etc. FC 54.04
Plea of True / Not True
The juvenile-system equivalent of an adult guilty/not guilty plea, entered at the adjudication hearing in response to the petition's allegations.
Juvenile Processing Office
A designated office or room (not a cell) where a child may be temporarily held — capped at 6 hours, limited to specific purposes (return to parent, paperwork, authorized photographing/fingerprinting, required warnings, certain statements). FC 52.025
Detention Hearing
A hearing (without a jury) held within strict statutory deadlines to decide whether a detained child should be released or remain in detention pending further proceedings. FC 54.01
Adjudication Hearing
The hearing where the juvenile court determines whether the child engaged in the alleged delinquent conduct/CINS — the juvenile analog to trial or a guilty plea proceeding. FC 54.03
Petition to Modify Disposition
A filing seeking to change a prior disposition order (e.g., because the child violated probation conditions) — the juvenile analog to a motion to revoke community supervision.
Status Offender
A child accused, adjudicated, or convicted for conduct that would not be a crime if committed by an adult — running away, violating school conduct standards, minors-only alcohol violations, etc. FC 51.02(15)
Nonoffender
A child in the juvenile system for reasons unrelated to their own prohibited conduct (e.g., an abuse/neglect/dependency case), or held solely for deportation. FC 51.02(8)

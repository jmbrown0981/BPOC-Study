# Chapter 19: Child Safety Check Alert List — Question Bank

*Covers LOs 19.1-19.7 (Purpose of the Child Safety Check Alert List; Encounters with Persons Listed on
the CSCAL; Reporting). Definitions and step/checklist items are drawn from the BPOC Chapter 19 material;
code-section questions are checked against the current statutory text of Family Code §§261.3022, 261.301,
262.102, 262.104, 262.105, Code of Criminal Procedure Arts. 2A.056-2A.058, and Penal Code §§20A.02-20A.03.*

## LO 19.1 — Purpose of the Child Safety Check Alert List (Family Code §261.3022)

### Q1 — Definition
**LO:** 19.1 · **Source:** BPOC 19.1 / Family Code §261.3022

What does the acronym **CSCAL** stand for?

- [ ] Child Safety Custody Alert List
- [x] Child Safety Check Alert List
- [ ] Child Services Case Alert Log
- [ ] Child Supervision Compliance Alert List

**Explanation:** CSCAL = Child Safety Check Alert List, established under Family Code §261.3022 as part of the Texas Crime Information Center (TCIC).

---

### Q2 — Definition
**LO:** 19.1 · **Source:** BPOC 19.1

What does the acronym **CPS** stand for?

- [ ] Child Placement Services
- [ ] Child Protection Statute
- [x] Child Protective Services
- [ ] Community Protective Services

**Explanation:** CPS = Child Protective Services, the division of the Department of Family and Protective Services (DFPS) that reports CSCAL cases to TCIC.

---

### Q3 — Definition
**LO:** 19.1 · **Source:** BPOC 19.1 / Family Code §261.3022

What does the acronym **DFPS** stand for, and which division of it submits CSCAL reports to TCIC?

- [ ] Department of Family Protection Services; the Investigations division
- [x] Department of Family and Protective Services; the Child Protective Services (CPS) division
- [ ] Division of Family Placement Services; the Conservatorship division
- [ ] Department of Family Preservation Services; the Intake division

**Explanation:** DFPS is the Department of Family and Protective Services. Its CPS division submits the names of children and legal guardians it is attempting to locate to TCIC as the CSCAL.

---

### Q4 — Definition
**LO:** 19.1 · **Source:** BPOC 19.1

What does the acronym **TCIC** stand for?

- [x] Texas Crime Information Center
- [ ] Texas Central Investigations Clearinghouse
- [ ] Texas Child Interdiction Center
- [ ] Texas Criminal Intelligence Coordination

**Explanation:** TCIC is the Texas Crime Information Center — the statewide database (Texas only) that hosts the CSCAL.

---

### Q5 — Concept
**LO:** 19.1 · **Source:** Family Code §261.3022(a)

Which agency is responsible for **maintaining** the Child Safety Check Alert List as part of TCIC?

- [ ] The Department of Family and Protective Services (DFPS)
- [ ] The Office of Court Administration
- [x] The Department of Public Safety (DPS)
- [ ] The county juvenile probation department

**Explanation:** §261.3022(a) — DPS maintains the CSCAL as part of TCIC. DFPS's CPS division is the one that submits/requests the listing, but DPS is the agency that actually maintains the list.

---

### Q6 — Concept
**LO:** 19.1 · **Source:** BPOC 19.1 / Family Code §261.3022(a)

Which of the following is **NOT** one of the three purposes for which CPS uses the CSCAL to locate a child and legal guardian?

- [ ] Investigating a report of child abuse or neglect
- [ ] Providing protective services to a family receiving family-based safety services
- [ ] Providing protective services to the family of a child in the managing conservatorship of the department
- [x] Serving an arrest warrant on the legal guardian

**Explanation:** The three statutory purposes under §261.3022(a) are investigating a report of abuse/neglect, providing services to a family receiving family-based safety services, and providing services to the family of a child in DFPS's managing conservatorship — CSCAL is a location tool for CPS casework, not a warrant-service mechanism.

---

### Q7 — Recall
**LO:** 19.1 · **Source:** Family Code §261.3022(b)

- [ ] 30 days

**Explanation:** §261.3022(b) — if DFPS is unable to locate the child or family after attempting to do so for not more than 20 days, the department must notify DPS that it is unable to locate them.

### Q9 — Recall

## LO 19.2 — CSCAL vs. a Missing Person Report

### Q10 — Concept
**LO:** 19.2 · **Source:** BPOC 19.2

In a Child Safety Check Alert List case, where is the child?

- [ ] The child's location is completely unknown to anyone
- [x] The child is with a legal guardian
- [ ] The child has run away voluntarily
- [ ] The child is in the physical custody of DFPS already

**Explanation:** BPOC 19.2 — the defining feature of a CSCAL case is that the child IS with a legal guardian; CPS just can't locate that guardian and child to investigate or provide services. This is what separates it from a true missing person case.

---

### Q11 — Concept
**LO:** 19.2 · **Source:** BPOC 19.2

In a Missing Person case, who does **not** know the location of the child?

- [x] The legal guardian
- [ ] Child Protective Services
- [ ] The Department of Public Safety
- [ ] The reporting party

**Explanation:** BPOC 19.2 — in a Missing Person case, it's the legal guardian who doesn't know where the child is. (In a CSCAL case, by contrast, the guardian knows exactly where the child is — CPS just can't locate them.)

---

### Q12 — Concept
**LO:** 19.2 · **Source:** BPOC 19.2

Which of the following can itself be the "legal guardian" in a Missing Person report, in a way that is not true of a CSCAL case?

- [x] The Department of Family and Protective Services, including as managing conservator
- [ ] A biological parent
- [ ] A court-appointed attorney ad litem
- [ ] A school administrator

**Explanation:** BPOC 19.2 — in a Missing Person case, the legal guardian can be DFPS itself (e.g., as managing conservator of a child already in the department's custody who then goes missing). A CSCAL case, by definition, involves a child who is still with a private legal guardian.

---

### Q15 — Concept
**LO:** 19.2 · **Source:** BPOC 19.2

Which system(s) receive a Missing Person report, as opposed to a CSCAL entry?

- [ ] TCIC only, same as CSCAL
- [x] NCIC (which is nationwide, unlike CSCAL's TCIC-only listing)

- [ ] Interpol only

**Explanation:** BPOC 19.2 — a Missing Person report goes into NCIC (national); CSCAL is TCIC-only (Texas). Both are Texas Crime Information Center-adjacent concepts, but only CSCAL is limited to TCIC.

## LO 19.3 — The Family Code §261.301 Update (Investigation of Report)

### Q17 — Recall

- [x] Notify DPS that the location of the child and the child's family is unknown
- [ ] Immediately file a suit affecting the parent-child relationship
- [ ] Request a temporary restraining order under §262.102

**Explanation:** §261.301(i) — this is the CSCAL trigger provision: when DFPS can't locate the child/family during a highest-priority investigation, it must notify DPS, which is how a case ends up on the CSCAL.

---

### Q18 — Recall
**LO:** 19.3 · **Source:** Family Code §261.301(i)

Once DPS locates the child and family after a §261.301(i) notification, what is DPS required to do?

- [ ] Take the child into protective custody directly
- [ ] Refer the case to the local district attorney
- [ ] Nothing further — DPS's role ends at locating them

---

### Q19 — Recall

## LO 19.5 — Authority to Temporarily Detain and Take Possession of a Child (CCP Art. 2A.056(c)-(d); Family Code §262.104)

### Q30 — Recall
**LO:** 19.5 · **Source:** CCP Art. 2A.056(c)

Under Art. 2A.056(c), what authority does a peace officer have regarding a CSCAL-listed child or other person, independent of whether the §262.104 conditions for taking possession are met?

- [x] The officer may temporarily detain the child or other person to ensure the safety and well-being of the child
- [ ] The officer may immediately arrest the legal guardian
- [ ] The officer may only observe and report, with no detention authority
- [ ] The officer may enter and search the guardian's residence without a warrant

**Explanation:** Art. 2A.056(c) grants a standalone detention authority — to assess the child's safety and well-being — that doesn't depend on whether the stricter §262.104 possession conditions are also satisfied.

---

### Q31 — True/False
**LO:** 19.5 · **Source:** CCP Art. 2A.056(c)-(d)

True or false: an officer may only temporarily detain a CSCAL-listed child if the officer can already point to one of the §262.104 conditions for taking possession without a court order.

- [ ] True
- [x] False

**Explanation:** Temporary detention to assess safety and well-being under Art. 2A.056(c) is a lower, independent bar. Taking full possession without a court order is the separate, higher bar that requires one of the §262.104 conditions, invoked through Art. 2A.056(d).

---

### Q32 — Recall
**LO:** 19.5 · **Source:** Family Code §262.104(a)

The opening clause of §262.104(a) allows taking possession of a child without a court order only when there is no time to obtain what, consistent with the child's health and safety?

- [ ] A search warrant
- [ ] Written consent from both parents
- [ ] Authorization from the county attorney

**Explanation:** §262.104(a) exists specifically for emergencies where there's no time to get a §262.102(a) court order first — if there IS time, the officer should be seeking that order instead of relying on §262.104.

### Q33 — Recall

Which three categories of people are authorized under §262.104(a) to take possession of a child without a court order?

- [ ] Any adult relative, a peace officer, and a licensed counselor

---

### Q34 — Recall
**LO:** 19.5 · **Source:** Family Code §262.104(a)

How many distinct numbered conditions does §262.104(a) list for taking possession of a child without a court order?

- [ ] 3
- [ ] 4
- [x] 5
- [ ] 7

**Explanation:** §262.104(a)(1)-(5) lists five conditions, each built from one of two evidentiary bases (personal knowledge, or corroborated information furnished by another) paired with one of three danger categories (immediate danger to health/safety, sexual abuse or trafficking, or a parent's controlled-substance use).

---

### Q35 — Concept
**LO:** 19.5 · **Source:** Family Code §262.104(a)(1)-(2)

What is the key difference between the evidentiary basis in §262.104(a)(1) and §262.104(a)(2)?

- [ ] (a)(1) applies only to sexual abuse cases; (a)(2) applies only to drug cases
- [x] (a)(1) requires personal knowledge alone; (a)(2) allows information furnished by another, but only if corroborated by personal knowledge
- [ ] (a)(1) requires a warrant; (a)(2) does not
- [ ] There is no meaningful difference — they are duplicate provisions

**Explanation:** Both (a)(1) and (a)(2) address "immediate danger to the physical health or safety of the child" — the difference is purely evidentiary: (a)(1) is personal knowledge only, (a)(2) is information furnished by someone else, but it must be corroborated by the officer's own personal knowledge of facts.

---

### Q36 — True/False
**LO:** 19.5 · **Source:** Family Code §262.104(a)(2)

True or false: under §262.104(a)(2), information furnished by another person is sufficient on its own, without any corroborating personal knowledge, to support taking possession of a child.

- [ ] True
- [x] False

**Explanation:** Every "information furnished" condition in §262.104(a) — (2), (4), and (5) — requires that information to be corroborated by personal knowledge of facts. Uncorroborated secondhand information alone is not enough under any of them.

---

### Q37 — Recall

- [ ] Truancy or educational neglect
- [ ] Cyberbullying

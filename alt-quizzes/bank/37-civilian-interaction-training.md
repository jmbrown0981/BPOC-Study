## LO 37.3 - Review of the Seven Step Violator Contact method.

### Q19 — Recall

- [ ] Statement of violation committed
- [ ] Identification of driver and check of conditions

**Explanation:** Step One is the greeting and identification of the police agency — officers should identify themselves and name their agency, using a natural, courteous greeting to help put the subject at ease.

---

### Q20 — Concept

- [x] To employ business courtesy, help make the subject feel at ease, and establish common ground free of unnatural actions, superiority, or deference
- [ ] To immediately establish the officer's legal authority to search the vehicle
- [ ] To determine whether the driver has any outstanding warrants before speaking further
- [ ] To discourage the driver from asking any questions during the stop

**Explanation:** The greeting's objectives are to employ business courtesy, help make the subject feel at ease, and establish common ground free of unnatural actions, superiority, or deference — smiling and speaking in a normal tone.

---

### Q21 — Recall
**LO:** 37.3 · **Source:** BPOC 37.3

What is Step Two of the Seven Step Violator Contact method?

- [x] Statement of violation committed
- [ ] Greeting and identification of the police agency
- [ ] Take that action
- [ ] Explain what the violator must do

**Explanation:** Step Two is the statement of violation committed — officers owe the driver the courtesy of telling them at once the reason they were stopped.

---

### Q22 — Concept
**LO:** 37.3 · **Source:** BPOC 37.3

According to this chapter's guidance on Step Two, what form should an officer's remarks generally take when discussing the violation with the driver?

- [x] A statement rather than a question — with one specific exception for asking if there's a reason for excessive speed
- [ ] A series of yes-or-no questions about the driver's knowledge of the law
- [ ] An open-ended interrogation about the driver's recent activities
- [ ] A recitation of the exact statutory text of the violation

**Explanation:** With the narrow exception of asking whether there's a reason for an excessive rate of speed (which lets the subject justify their actions or effectively admit the violation), officers should refrain from asking questions about the subject's knowledge of the violation — remarks should be statements, not questions.

---

### Q23 — Recall
**LO:** 37.3 · **Source:** BPOC 37.3

What is Step Three of the Seven Step Violator Contact method?

- [x] Identification of driver and check of conditions of violator and vehicle
- [ ] Statement of violation committed
- [ ] Statement of action to be taken
- [ ] Leave

**Explanation:** Step Three is identification of the driver and a check of the conditions of the violator and vehicle, beginning with requesting the driver's license.

---

### Q24 — Concept

- [ ] Because it is required by the Fourth Amendment
- [ ] Because officers are legally prohibited from touching a citizen's belongings
- [ ] Because it speeds up the process of writing the citation

---

### Q25 — Recall
**LO:** 37.3 · **Source:** BPOC 37.3

If a violator has no driver's license, what does this chapter instruct the officer to do in Step Three?

- [ ] Immediately place the violator under arrest
- [ ] Release the violator without any identification check
- [ ] Call for a supervisor before proceeding further

**Explanation:** If the subject has no license, the officer should ask for other identification (preferably one carrying a description), and if the subject has none of that either, write down a brief description including age, height, weight, eyes, hair, identifying marks, and address.

---

### Q26 — Recall
**LO:** 37.3 · **Source:** BPOC 37.3

What is Step Four of the Seven Step Violator Contact method?

- [x] Statement of action to be taken
- [ ] Identification of driver and check of conditions
- [ ] Take that action
- [ ] Explain what the violator must do

**Explanation:** Step Four is the statement of action to be taken — a clear statement, in a firm but calm manner, leaving no doubt as to the planned action (citation, warning, or arrest).

---

### Q27 — Concept
**LO:** 37.3 · **Source:** BPOC 37.3

Why does this chapter recommend that officers avoid using the word "I" during the violator interview in Step Four, favoring "you" instead?

- [x] Using "I" shifts the emphasis from the violation committed to the officer's own action, giving the violator an opening to shift blame onto the officer rather than the offense — "you" keeps the focus on the violation
- [ ] Because department policy legally prohibits officers from using first-person pronouns
- [ ] Because using "I" is considered a Miranda violation
- [ ] Because "you" is grammatically required in a citation's legal language

**Explanation:** When an officer says "I am going to...," the action shifts from the violation committed to the officer's own action, giving the violator a chance to shift blame from their offense to the officer's action — using "you" keeps the emphasis on the violator and the violation, avoiding unpleasantness.

---

### Q28 — Recall
**LO:** 37.3 · **Source:** BPOC 37.3

What is Step Five of the Seven Step Violator Contact method?

- [x] Take that action
- [ ] Statement of action to be taken
- [ ] Explain what the violator must do
- [ ] Leave

**Explanation:** Step Five is taking the action decided upon in Step Four — writing the citation, taking the violator into custody, or issuing a warning.

---

### Q29 — Recall
**LO:** 37.3 · **Source:** BPOC 37.3

What is Step Six of the Seven Step Violator Contact method, and why does this chapter say it matters?

- [x] Explain what the violator must do — a short, clear explanation dispels uncertainty, since the violator may not be as familiar with the courts and procedures as the officer is
- [ ] Explain what the violator must do — required solely to satisfy department paperwork
- [ ] Take that action — performing the citation or arrest itself
- [ ] Leave — closing out the contact and returning to the patrol car

**Explanation:** Step Six is explaining exactly what the violator must do next (e.g., appear at a certain court by a certain time). A short, clear explanation dispels uncertainty — the chapter notes the violator may not know the courts and locations as well as the officer does, and a little extra time here can mean more court appearances and fewer warrants.

---

### Q30 — Concept
**LO:** 37.3 · **Source:** BPOC 37.3

What guidance does this chapter give for Step Seven, "Leave"?

- [ ] The officer should linger at the vehicle until the violator drives away
- [ ] The officer should apologize for having stopped the violator
- [ ] The officer should ask the violator to sign a satisfaction survey before leaving

**Explanation:** Step Seven's guidance is that the leave-taking should be as firm and impersonal as the approach, avoiding a gloating attitude — a sincere, business-like parting comment is sufficient, and this is an appropriate time to notify the driver they're free to leave.

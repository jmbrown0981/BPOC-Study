# Chapter 33: Juvenile Offenders — Instructor Knowledge Check Bank

Sourced from the department's instructor slide deck ("33 Juvenile Offenders 02.2020 Judd final v1.pptx", filed under `resources/BPOC_markdown/33_supplemental/`). Every question below was independently re-verified against current Texas statute text before being added here — several of the original slides' answer-key letters did not match the statute (the deck's Notes fields appear to reference the original slide's visual grid position rather than reading order) and a handful of prompts had been subtly altered from the actual statutory language (dropped "not"s, swapped standards like "immediate danger" vs. "substantial risk," etc.) — those are preserved as True/False "spot the altered statute" questions with an explanation showing the correction, exactly as the original deck intended them to be caught. Several "select all that apply" slides were split into individual True/False items, or converted to a single "which of the following is entirely accurate" multiple-choice question, because this quiz engine only supports one correct answer per question.

### Q1 — Recall
**LO:** 33.2 · **Source:** FC 51.02(2)

"Child" means a person who is:

- [x] 10 years of age or older and under 17 years of age
- [ ] 10 years of age or older and under 16 years of age
- [ ] 5 years of age to 18 years of age
- [ ] 11 years of age or older and under 17 years of age

**Explanation:** FC 51.02(2)(A): a child is 10 or older and under 17 (or, under (B), 17 or older and under 18 if the conduct occurred before turning 17).

### Q2 — Definition
**LO:** 33.2 · **Source:** FC 51.02(15)

"Status offender" means:

- [ ] a child who is accused, adjudicated, or convicted for conduct that would, under state law, be a crime if committed by an adult.
- [x] a child who is accused, adjudicated, or convicted for conduct that would not, under state law, be a crime if committed by an adult.
- [ ] a child who is accused, adjudicated, or convicted for delinquent conduct
- [ ] a child who is convicted of a felony

**Explanation:** FC 51.02(15): a status offender is accused/adjudicated/convicted for conduct that would NOT be a crime if committed by an adult (e.g., running away, truancy, a minor-in-possession alcohol violation).

### Q3 — Definition
**LO:** 33.3 · **Source:** FC 51.03(a)(1)

Delinquent conduct is:

- [x] conduct, other than a traffic offense, that violates a penal law of this state or of the United States punishable by imprisonment or by confinement in jail
- [ ] a traffic offense that violates a penal law of this state or of the United States punishable by imprisonment or by confinement in jail
- [ ] conduct, including traffic offenses, that violates a penal law of this state or of the United States punishable by imprisonment or by confinement in jail
- [ ] conduct, other than a traffic offense, that violates a penal law of this state or of the United States punishable by a fine only

**Explanation:** FC 51.03(a)(1). Note this deck's original answer key marked a different option correct — that was checked against the statute text and corrected. "Other than a traffic offense" and "punishable by imprisonment or confinement in jail" are both required; a fine-only offense is conduct indicating a need for supervision (CINS), not delinquent conduct.

### Q4 — Definition
**LO:** 33.3 · **Source:** FC 51.03(b)(1)(A)

Conduct Indicating a Need for Supervision (CINS) means:

- [x] conduct, other than a traffic offense, that violates the penal laws of this state of the grade of misdemeanor that are punishable by fine only
- [ ] a traffic offense that violates a penal law of this state or of the United States punishable by imprisonment or by confinement in jail
- [ ] conduct that violates a penal law of this state or of the United States punishable by imprisonment or by confinement in jail
- [ ] conduct, other than a traffic offense, that violates a penal law of this state or of the United States punishable by imprisonment or by confinement in jail

**Explanation:** FC 51.03(b)(1)(A). Fine-only misdemeanors (other than traffic offenses) are CINS; imprisonment/jail-punishable offenses are delinquent conduct (51.03(a)(1)), not CINS.

### Q6 — Recall
**LO:** 33.4 · **Source:** FC 51.095(a)(1)(A)

The statement of a child is admissible in evidence in any future proceeding concerning the matter about which the statement was given if:

- [ ] the statement shows that the child has at some time before the making of the statement received from a magistrate a Miranda warning in the presence of a parent or guardian.
- [ ] the statement shows that the child has at some time before the making of the statement received from a peace officer a Miranda warning.
- [ ] the statement shows that the child has at some time before the making of the statement received a phone call and the opportunity to contact an attorney.
- [x] the statement shows that the child has at some time before the making of the statement received from a magistrate a Miranda warning.

**Explanation:** FC 51.095(a)(1)(A) requires the warning to come from a magistrate — not a peace officer — and does not itself require a parent/guardian to be present for this particular condition (that is a separate signing requirement under (a)(1)(B)).

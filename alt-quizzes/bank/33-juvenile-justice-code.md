## LO 33.1 — Purpose of Title 3, Juvenile Justice Code

### Q1 — Recall

- [x] To remove, where appropriate, the taint of criminality from children committing certain unlawful acts
- [ ] To eliminate all confidentiality protections for juvenile records

**Explanation:** FC 51.01(2)(B) lists removing the taint of criminality from children, where appropriate, as one of the title's purposes — alongside promoting punishment for criminal acts and providing treatment/rehabilitation that holds both parent and child accountable.

**Explanation:** FC 51.01(5) — the purposes should be achieved in a family environment whenever possible, separating the child from the parents only when necessary for the child's welfare or public safety.

#### Secure Correctional Facility vs. Secure Detention Facility

### Q8 — Concept
**LO:** 33.2 · **Source:** FC 51.02(13)-(14)

What is the key distinction between a "Secure Correctional Facility" and a "Secure Detention Facility" under FC 51.02?

- [x] A correctional facility houses juveniles already adjudicated (post-disposition); a detention facility houses juveniles only accused, pending proceedings (pre-adjudication)
- [ ] A correctional facility is always operated by TJJD; a detention facility is always operated by a county
- [ ] A correctional facility is unlocked; a detention facility has construction fixtures restricting movement
- [ ] There is no legal distinction — the terms are interchangeable under the Family Code

**Explanation:** FC 51.02(13): a Secure Correctional Facility is used for the placement of a juvenile "who has been adjudicated" as having committed an offense (post-adjudication). FC 51.02(14): a Secure Detention Facility is used for the "temporary placement" of a juvenile "accused" of an offense (pre-adjudication/awaiting proceedings). Both share the same "construction fixtures... to physically restrict movement" language — that part is NOT the distinguishing feature.

---

### Q9 — Scenario
**LO:** 33.2 · **Source:** FC 51.02(13)-(14)

A 15-year-old has just been taken into custody and is awaiting her detention hearing; no adjudication has occurred yet. Which type of facility is legally appropriate to hold her?

- [x] Secure Detention Facility
- [ ] Secure Correctional Facility
- [ ] Either one — the statute treats them as functionally identical for this purpose
- [ ] Neither — she must be held only in the county jail until adjudication

**Explanation:** Because she is only "accused" and has not been adjudicated, FC 51.02(14)'s Secure Detention Facility definition applies. A Secure Correctional Facility (51.02(13)) is for juveniles already adjudicated as having committed an offense.

"Res Gestae" is a Latin phrase meaning "things done" — it refers to statements made under the stress of excitement. Why does this concept matter for juvenile interrogation law?

## LO 33.3 — Delinquent Conduct; Conduct Indicating a Need for Supervision (CINS)

### Q15 — Definition
**LO:** 33.3 · **Source:** FC 51.03(a)

Which of the following is delinquent conduct under FC 51.03(a), rather than CINS?

- [x] Conduct, other than a traffic offense, that violates a penal law punishable by imprisonment or jail confinement
- [ ] Voluntary absence from home without parental consent for a substantial time
- [ ] A violation of a school district's written standards of student conduct
- [ ] A fine-only misdemeanor under state penal law

**Explanation:** FC 51.03(a)(1) — conduct (other than a traffic offense) that violates a penal law punishable by imprisonment or jail confinement is delinquent conduct. Fine-only misdemeanors, running away, and most student-conduct violations fall under CINS (51.03(b)) instead.

---

### Q16 — Fill-in-Blank
**LO:** 33.3 · **Source:** FC 51.03(a)(4); AL 106.041

Conduct that violates Section ______, Alcoholic Beverage Code — driving under the influence of alcohol by a minor — is delinquent conduct only when it is the minor's ______ or subsequent offense.

- [x] 106.041; third
- [ ] 106.04; second
- [ ] 106.02; first
- [ ] 106.05; fourth

**Explanation:** FC 51.03(a)(4) specifically elevates a third-or-subsequent DUI-minor offense under AB Code 106.041 to delinquent conduct; a first or second offense under 106.041 is otherwise a Class C misdemeanor and would fall under CINS instead.

---

### Q17 — Scenario
**LO:** 33.3 · **Source:** FC 51.03(a)(3); PC 49.04-49.08

A 16-year-old is found to have been driving a motor vehicle in a public place while intoxicated (not merely "any detectable amount" — legally intoxicated). Under FC 51.03(a)(3), how is this conduct classified?

- [x] Delinquent conduct, because it violates PC 49.04 (DWI)
- [ ] CINS, because DWI by a minor is always fine-only
- [ ] Neither — minors cannot be charged with DWI under the Penal Code
- [ ] A status offense, since it involves alcohol

**Explanation:** FC 51.03(a)(3) makes conduct that violates PC 49.04-49.08 (DWI, DWI with child passenger, BWI, intoxication assault, intoxication manslaughter) delinquent conduct — distinct from the "detectable amount" DUI-minor offense under AB Code 106.041, which is a separate, lesser CINS/Class-C scheme unless it's a third offense.

### Q18 — Definition
**LO:** 33.3 · **Source:** FC 51.03(b)

Which of the following IS conduct indicating a need for supervision (CINS) under FC 51.03(b)?

- [x] The voluntary absence of a child from home without parental consent, for a substantial time or without intent to return
- [ ] Conduct that violates PC 49.04 (DWI)
- [ ] A felony violation of the Penal Code
- [ ] Conduct that violates PC 19.02 (Murder)

**Explanation:** FC 51.03(b)(2) explicitly lists a child's voluntary, unauthorized absence from home for a substantial time (or without intent to return) as CINS — this is the statutory basis for "runaway" status offenses.

## LO 33.4 — Waiver of Rights & Admissibility of a Child's Statement

### Q22 — Recall
**LO:** 33.4 · **Source:** FC 51.09

Under FC 51.09, a right granted to a child under Title 3 may be waived only if all of several conditions are met. Which of the following is NOT one of those conditions?

- [ ] The waiver is made by the child AND the child's attorney
- [ ] The waiver is voluntary
- [x] A parent or guardian must also independently co-sign the waiver
- [ ] The waiver is made in writing or on the record in recorded court proceedings

**Explanation:** FC 51.09 requires all four of: (1) waiver by the child AND the child's attorney, (2) informed understanding, (3) voluntariness, and (4) writing or recorded proceedings — there's no independent parental co-signature requirement in 51.09 itself (note this differs from FC 51.095's magistrate-warning statement procedure, which does not require parental involvement either).

---

### Q23 — Concept
**LO:** 33.4 · **Source:** FC 51.095(a)(1)

For a child's WRITTEN statement to be admissible under FC 51.095(a)(1), who must administer the warnings and witness the signing — separate from the officer taking the statement?

- [x] A magistrate
- [ ] The child's school principal
- [ ] Any two peace officers acting as witnesses for each other
- [ ] The elected county judge only

**Explanation:** FC 51.095(a)(1) requires a magistrate to give the warnings, and the statement must generally be signed in the presence of the magistrate with no law enforcement officer or prosecuting attorney present (subject to a narrow safety exception).

---

### Q24 — Scenario
**LO:** 33.4 · **Source:** FC 51.095(a)(5)

An officer wants a juvenile's ORAL statement, made during custodial interrogation, to be admissible. Under FC 51.095(a)(5), what combination of steps makes this possible?

- [x] The statement is recorded by an electronic recording device, the child received a magistrate warning that is part of the recording, and the child knowingly/voluntarily waived the rights on the recording
- [ ] Any peace officer can give the warning as long as a parent is present
- [ ] The statement only needs to be corroborated by a second officer's testimony
- [ ] Oral statements from juveniles are never admissible under any circumstances

**Explanation:** FC 51.095(a)(5) permits oral statements when they are electronically recorded (audio or audiovisual), the magistrate's warning is part of that same recording, the child's waiver is captured on the recording, the equipment/operator meet accuracy requirements, and defense counsel gets a copy at least 20 days before the proceeding.

---

### Q25 — True/False
**LO:** 33.4 · **Source:** FC 51.095(a)(2)-(3)

True or false: A child's oral statement that leads to finding secreted or stolen property, or the instrument used to commit the offense, can be admissible even without a prior magistrate warning.

- [x] True
- [ ] False

**Explanation:** FC 51.095(a)(2) — a statement of facts/circumstances found true that tend to establish guilt (like locating hidden stolen property or the instrument of the offense) is admissible on that basis alone, similar to the adult "fruit" exception under CCP 38.22 Sec. 3(c).

### Q26 — Concept

- [ ] Juvenile procedures are always faster to complete
- [ ] Adult procedures are illegal to use on anyone under 21
- [ ] There is no practical difference between the two sets of procedures

**Explanation:** Per the BPOC instructor note, compliance with juvenile procedures (FC 51.095) satisfies the adult admissibility statute (CCP 38.22) as well, but not vice versa — so when age is uncertain, the safer path is to use juvenile procedures.

## LO 33.6 — Fingerprinting, Photographing, and Polygraph Restrictions

### Q37 — Recall
**LO:** 33.6 · **Source:** FC 58.001(c)

Under FC 58.001(c), when must a law enforcement agency destroy fingerprints, photographs, and records relating to a child taken into custody?

- [x] If the child is not referred to juvenile court within 10 calendar days after being taken into custody
- [ ] If the child is not referred to juvenile court within 30 calendar days
- [ ] Never — juvenile records are retained permanently regardless of referral
- [ ] Only if a judge specifically orders destruction

**Explanation:** FC 58.001(c) — records must be forwarded to DPS only if referral to juvenile court happens within 10 calendar days (including weekends/holidays) of custody; otherwise, the agency must destroy all such information, with narrow exceptions for first-offender or informal-disposition programs.

---

### Q38 — Concept
**LO:** 33.6 · **Source:** FC 58.002(a)

Under FC 58.002(a), when may a child be photographed or fingerprinted without consent of the juvenile court?

- [x] If the child is taken into custody, or referred to juvenile court for conduct that is a felony or a jailable misdemeanor
- [ ] Anytime a school resource officer believes it is warranted
- [ ] Only with written parental consent, regardless of the offense
- [ ] Never — juvenile court consent is always required

**Explanation:** FC 58.002(a) permits fingerprinting/photographing without the juvenile court's consent specifically when the child is taken into custody, or referred for conduct constituting a felony or a jailable misdemeanor — otherwise, court consent (or parental consent under 58.002(c)) is required.

---

### Q39 — Scenario
**LO:** 33.6 · **Source:** FC 58.0021

An officer investigating a burglary finds latent fingerprints at the scene and has probable cause to believe a particular child's prints will match. The child has not been taken into custody. Under FC 58.0021, what may the officer do?

- [x] Take temporary custody of the child solely to obtain fingerprints for comparison — this is not a "taking into custody" under FC 52.01 and may not be reported to the juvenile justice information system
- [ ] Nothing — fingerprints can only be taken after a formal arrest
- [ ] Detain the child indefinitely until the comparison is complete
- [ ] Obtain the fingerprints only with a search warrant, no exceptions

**Explanation:** FC 58.0021 creates a narrow, separate custody category just for obtaining comparison fingerprints or a photograph when there's probable cause the child engaged in delinquent conduct and that the prints/photo will materially assist the investigation. It is explicitly NOT a FC 52.01 custody and isn't reported to the state juvenile justice information system; prints/photos that don't match must be destroyed immediately.

### Q40 — Recall
**LO:** 33.6 · **Source:** FC 58.0022

FC 58.0022 allows fingerprinting or photographing a child to establish identity in which specific circumstance?

- [x] The officer has probable cause the child engaged in CINS conduct described by FC 51.03(b)(2) (running away) and, after reasonable effort, cannot determine the child's identity
- [ ] Any time a child refuses to give their name during a traffic stop
- [ ] Whenever a parent requests it for a missing-child report
- [ ] Only during a formal juvenile court adjudication hearing

**Explanation:** FC 58.0022 is specifically aimed at identifying runaways: an officer may fingerprint/photograph a child taken into custody for suspected running-away CINS conduct when identity can't otherwise be reasonably determined, and must destroy the records once identity is established (or determined unascertainable by that method).

### Q41 — Recall
**LO:** 33.6 · **Source:** FC 51.151

Under FC 51.151, who must consent before a polygraph examination may be administered to a child taken into custody under FC 52.01?

- [ ] The child alone, if 14 years of age or older

- [ ] No consent is required if the child agrees orally

# Chapter 22: Traffic Code — Offenses & Classifications Question Bank

*This bank covers scenario-based application of the Transportation Code (and related Penal Code) offenses cataloged on the Ch.22 Offense &amp; Penalty Reference page (`alt-quizzes/22-offense-penalties.html`). Each question presents a fact pattern and asks for two independent answers: which offense (if any) applies, and its punishment classification. Each half is scored independently (half credit each). Several offenses appear across 2–3 scenarios with different facts, priors, or injury outcomes in play, mirroring how the same statute grades up or down depending on circumstances — especially the general-penalty catchalls (a section with no penalty of its own defaults to its subtitle's flat fine) and the Illegal Dumping value ladder (graded by weight/volume, not dollars). Companion banks: `22-day1-4.md` and `22-day5-8.md` (the broader Ch.22 definitions/rules coverage), and the standalone reference page itself for the full offense catalog with statute links.*

## General-Penalty Catchalls — When a Section Doesn't State Its Own Punishment

### Q1 — Scenario
**LO:** 22.15 · **Source:** TC 545.4145, 542.401

An officer stops a truck towing a boat trailer and finds a teenager riding inside the boat itself while it's being towed down a farm-to-market road at 55 mph. No injury results, and the driver has no other violations.

**Offense:**
- [x] Riding in or on Boat or Personal Watercraft Drawn by Vehicle
- [ ] Riding in Open Beds
- [ ] Unsafe Vehicle Equipment
- [ ] No offense — towing a boat with a passenger inside is legal on a farm-to-market road

**Classification:**
- [x] Misdemeanor (flat fine) — $1–$200, no jail (General Penalty, TC 542.401)
- [ ] Class C Misdemeanor
- [ ] Class B Misdemeanor
- [ ] Class A Misdemeanor

**Explanation:** TC 545.4145 makes it an offense to ride in or on a boat or personal watercraft drawn by a vehicle, but the section states no penalty of its own. Because it falls within Subtitle C's rules-of-the-road framework, it defaults to the Ch.545 catchall at TC 542.401 — a flat fine of $1–$200 with no jail time, not a standard "Class" misdemeanor.

## Illegal Dumping Value Ladder — Graded by Weight/Volume, Not Dollars (HSC 365.012)

### Q6 — Scenario
**LO:** 22.5 · **Source:** HSC 365.011(5), 365.012

A homeowner is caught tossing a single kitchen trash bag — about 4 pounds — off the tailgate onto the shoulder of a county road rather than paying for a dump run.

**Offense:**
- [x] Illegal Dumping
- [ ] Littering (civil violation only)
- [ ] Obeying Warning Signs and Barricades
- [ ] No offense — a single trash bag is too small to qualify as "dumping"

**Classification:**
- [x] Class C Misdemeanor
- [ ] Class B Misdemeanor
- [ ] Class A Misdemeanor
- [ ] State Jail Felony

**Explanation:** Disposing of waste at a place that isn't an approved solid waste site is Illegal Dumping under HSC 365.012, regardless of size — the weight/volume only sets the grade. At 5 lb or less (or 5 gallons or less), the bottom tier applies: Class C misdemeanor.

### Q69 — Scenario

### Q2 — Term ID
**LO:** 38.1 · **Source:** BPOC 38.1; Human Resources Code §81.001

**Answer:** Hard of Hearing

"______" means a hearing status that results in a loss of hearing function to an individual and in which the individual relies on residual hearing and may depend on visual methods to communicate.

**Explanation:** This is the BPOC 38.1 definition of "Hard of hearing," taken from Human Resources Code §81.001. The key difference from "Deaf" is that a hard of hearing person relies on residual hearing.

### Q3 — Term ID
**LO:** 38.1 · **Source:** BPOC 38 Unit Goal

**Answer:** Americans with Disabilities Act | Americans with Disability Act

In the context of BPOC Chapter 38, "ADA" stands for ______.

**Explanation:** The Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) requires that people who are deaf or hard of hearing be given the same services provided to everyone else, and that they not be excluded or segregated from services.

### Q24 — Scenario

### Q26 — Scenario
**LO:** 38.5 · **Source:** BPOC 38.5 Example #3

An officer responds to the scene of a domestic disturbance. One person says the other has been beating their children and they have been trying to restrain them. One of them is deaf. The officer begins questioning them by writing notes, but their response indicates a lack of comprehension. They request a sign language interpreter.

Is an interpreter needed?

- [x] Needed
- [ ] Not Needed

**Explanation:** Needed. Written notes aren't working, and the person has requested a sign language interpreter, so one should be called. If the person's behavior is threatening, the officer can make an arrest and have an interpreter available later at the booking station. A family member or companion should not interpret here, because emotional ties may interfere with impartiality.

### Q27 — Scenario
**LO:** 38.5 · **Source:** BPOC 38.5 Example #4

An officer responds to the scene of a car accident where a person has been seriously injured. The person is conscious but is unable to comprehend the officer's questions because they are deaf. A family member who is present begins interpreting what the officer is saying.

Is an agency interpreter needed at this point?

- [ ] Needed
- [x] Not Needed

**Explanation:** Not needed. A family member or companion may interpret when the parties are willing, the need for information is urgent (such as an accident with serious injuries), and the questions are basic and uncomplicated. In general, though, the agency must provide the interpreter; don't expect or demand that a deaf person provide their own.

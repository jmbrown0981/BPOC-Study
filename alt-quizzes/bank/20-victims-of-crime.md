## LO 20.1 — Elements of a Crisis Reaction

### Q1 — Definition
**LO:** 20.1 · **Source:** BPOC 20.1

BPOC cites Merriam-Webster's definition of **trauma** as a disordered psychic or behavioral state resulting from what?

- [ ] A pre-existing personality disorder
- [x] Severe mental or emotional stress or physical injury
- [ ] A chemical imbalance unrelated to any external event
- [ ] Prolonged exposure to a stressful work environment only

**Explanation:** BPOC 20.1 — trauma is a disordered psychic or behavioral state resulting from severe mental or emotional stress or physical injury; the "crisis reaction" is the normal, universal human response pattern that follows.

---

### Q2 — Concept
**LO:** 20.1 · **Source:** BPOC 20.1

- [ ] "Fight-or-flight fatigue"
- [x] "Frozen fright"
- [ ] "Adrenal collapse"
- [ ] "Somatic dissociation"

**Explanation:** BPOC 20.1 describes the physical response to trauma, based on animal instinct, as including physical shock, disorientation, and numbness — collectively "frozen fright."

---

### Q3 — Recall
**LO:** 20.1 · **Source:** BPOC 20.1

- [ ] Adrenaline pumping through the body

- [ ] Hyperventilation and sweating

**Explanation:** BPOC 20.1's fight-or-flight list: adrenaline release, the body relieving itself of excess materials (e.g., ingested food), physical senses becoming acute while others shut down, increased heart rate, and hyperventilation/sweating — not memory loss, which isn't part of the physical fight-or-flight sequence.

- [x] Exhaustion

**Explanation:** BPOC 20.2's three phases, in order: Impact, Recoil, and Reorganization.

### Q22 — Concept

- [x] Impact
- [ ] Recoil

---

### Q23 — Concept
**LO:** 20.2 · **Source:** BPOC 20.2

- [ ] Euphoria and relief

### Q24 — Recall
**LO:** 20.2 · **Source:** BPOC 20.2

- [x] They diminish in intensity as the victim becomes more normalized
- [ ] They intensify permanently
- [ ] They are entirely replaced by feelings of shock
- [ ] They convert into physical symptoms only

### Q41 — Recall
**LO:** 20.4 · **Source:** BPOC 20.4

- [x] Categorical
- [ ] Sequential
- [ ] Longitudinal
- [ ] Retrospective

### Q42 — Concept
**LO:** 20.4 · **Source:** BPOC 20.4

Why might a chronological interview approach be difficult for a trauma victim, according to BPOC?

- [x] Trauma affects how memories are encoded, so fragmented memories may not be recalled in chronological order

- [ ] Chronological interviewing takes longer than categorical interviewing in every case

**Explanation:** BPOC 20.4 — because trauma affects memory encoding, fragmented memories may be randomly stored and not recalled chronologically, which is why categorical interviewing (gathering information as the victim remembers it) can work better for some victims. BPOC notes which approach is used depends on agency policy.

### Q43 — Recall
**LO:** 20.4 · **Source:** BPOC 20.4

### Q44 — Concept
**LO:** 20.4 · **Source:** BPOC 20.4

Why does BPOC caution against asking victims direct "why" questions (e.g., "why didn't you leave")?

**Explanation:** BPOC 20.4 — the concern with "why" questions is that they can come across as challenging the victim's credibility; instead, officers should explain why they're asking a question and phrase it non-judgmentally (e.g., asking about evidentiary details like clothing worn, rather than "why were you wearing that").

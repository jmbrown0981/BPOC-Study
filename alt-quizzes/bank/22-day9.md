# Chapter 22: Traffic Code and Crash Investigations — Day 9 (Crash Investigation Procedures, CR-3, and Traffic Control)

*This bank covers LO 22.30–22.38: the procedural/practical half of Chapter 22 — crash-investigation legal authority and equipment, scene response and management, interviewing, measuring the scene and collecting evidence, the CR-3 report, and directing traffic. It complements (and avoids re-testing) the statute-heavy crash-duty and offense material already covered in `22-day5-8.md` and the scenario-based dual-answer questions in `22-offense-penalties.md`.*

## Day 9 — LO 22.30: Crash Investigation — Legal Authority & Equipment

### Q1 — Fill-in-Blank

Under TC 550.001, Chapter 550's duties-following-collision requirements apply to a highway or other public place, to certain roads owned by a water control and improvement district, and to a private access way or parking area provided for a client or patron by a ______ (other than a private residential property).

### Q2 — Multiple Choice
**LO:** 22.30 · **Source:** TC 550.041(a)

A collision occurs in a privately owned shopping-center parking lot (not a residential lot and no parking fee is charged), resulting in property damage of $1,500. Under TC 550.041, may a peace officer investigate and file justifiable charges?

- [x] Yes — 550.041 lets an officer investigate a collision causing injury, death, or at least $1,000 in apparent property damage regardless of whether Chapter 550 applies to that property
- [ ] No — the officer may only investigate collisions on a public highway
- [ ] No — private property collisions require the property owner's written consent first

**Explanation:** 550.041(a) authorizes a peace officer to investigate and file charges for a collision meeting the injury/death or $1,000+ property-damage threshold "without regard to whether the collision occurred on property to which this chapter applies" — the shopping-center lot qualifies since it isn't a private residential lot or a fee parking lot (the two carve-outs in 550.041(b)).

## Day 9 — LO 22.38: Directing and Controlling Traffic

### Q27 — Multiple Choice
**LO:** 22.38 · **Source:** BPOC 22.38

Per BPOC guidance on vehicle lighting during traffic control, officers should limit the number of flashing lights displayed because:

- [x] Drivers who are fatigued, impaired, or aged have more difficulty with glare, and people tend to drive toward where they look
- [ ] Flashing lights drain the patrol vehicle's battery too quickly
- [ ] State law caps the number of emergency lights a vehicle may display
- [ ] Flashing lights interfere with the officer's in-car camera system

**Explanation:** BPOC's rationale is driver behavior, not equipment limits: excess flashing lights make gaze more difficult for fatigued, impaired, or aged drivers, and since people tend to steer toward what they're looking at, too many lights can actually draw traffic toward the scene instead of away from it.

- [ ] Attracting drivers' attention to start moving
- [ ] A general warning signal

### Q31 — Multiple Choice
**LO:** 22.38 · **Source:** BPOC 22.38

Per BPOC guidance on pedestrian control while directing traffic at an incident, an officer should:

- [x] Hold pedestrians back behind the curb, off the roadway, until traffic movement has stopped, and start/stop their flow the same way as vehicle traffic

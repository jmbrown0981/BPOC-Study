# Chapter 22: Traffic Code and Crash Investigations — Definitions Question Bank

*This bank covers every term BPOC Chapter 22 designates for definition: vehicle types, persons and governmental authorities, traffic/traffic-area/traffic-control terms, the Chapter 541 miscellaneous terms, Health and Safety Code litter/dumping definitions, driver's license and CDL terms, Chapter 545 and automated-vehicle definitions, equipment/inspection definitions, bicycle/scooter/golf-cart/off-highway-vehicle definitions, motorcycle and criminal-street-gang definitions, and vehicle registration/size-and-weight definitions. Crash investigation procedure, licensing procedure, and equipment-standard procedural sections (non-definitional) are covered elsewhere; this bank is defs-only, matching the Chapter 8 Definitions bank's scope convention.*

## LO 22.1 — Vehicle Types (TC 541.201)

### Q1 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(1)

TC 541.201(1) defines "Authorized emergency vehicle" to include a fire department or police vehicle, and also:

- [x] A public or private ambulance licensed by the Department of State Health Services, and a private vehicle of a volunteer firefighter or certified EMS employee/volunteer responding to a fire alarm or medical emergency
- [ ] Any vehicle displaying a red or blue flashing light, regardless of ownership
- [ ] Only vehicles owned directly by a municipality's fire or police department
- [ ] Any tow truck responding to a highway collision

**Explanation:** The definition is a long enumerated list (fire/police vehicles, licensed ambulances, certain EMS vehicles, designated municipal/county emergency vehicles, volunteer firefighter/EMS private vehicles while responding, industrial emergency response vehicles, blood/tissue bank vehicles on emergency runs, federal law enforcement vehicles, and certain county emergency management vehicles) — merely having emergency-style lights doesn't qualify a vehicle on its own.

### Q2 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(2)

TC 541.201(2) defines "Bicycle" as a device, excluding a moped, capable of being ridden solely using human power that has:

- [x] Two tandem wheels (at least one over 14 inches in diameter), or three wheels with at least one over 14 inches in diameter, or any number of wheels with adaptive technology for a rider with a disability
- [ ] Exactly two wheels of any diameter and a motor under 750 watts
- [ ] No more than one wheel over 20 inches in diameter
- [ ] Pedals and a seat, regardless of wheel count or size

**Explanation:** The "over 14 inches in diameter" wheel-size threshold is what separates a "bicycle" from a child's tricycle or similar small-wheeled toy under this definition.

### Q3 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(3)

TC 541.201(3) defines "Bus" as a motor vehicle designed to accommodate more than 10 passengers (including the operator), or:

- [x] A motor vehicle, other than a taxicab, designed and used to transport persons for compensation
- [ ] Any vehicle owned by a school district
- [ ] A motor vehicle designed to accommodate more than 15 passengers only
- [ ] Any vehicle used to transport cargo for compensation

**Explanation:** "Bus" has two independent prongs — capacity (more than 10 passengers) OR for-compensation passenger transport (excluding taxicabs) — either one is enough to qualify.

### Q4 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(4)

TC 541.201(4) defines "Farm tractor" as:

- [x] A motor vehicle designed and used primarily as a farm implement to draw an implement of husbandry, including a plow or mowing machine
- [ ] Any motor vehicle registered to a farm or ranch
- [ ] A vehicle designed and adapted to deliver feed to livestock
- [ ] A towed vehicle that spreads fertilizer or agricultural chemicals

**Explanation:** The last two distractors describe "implement of husbandry" under 541.201(6), not "farm tractor" — the two terms are related but distinct.

### Q5 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(5)

TC 541.201(5) defines "House trailer" as a trailer or semitrailer, other than a towable recreational vehicle, that is transportable on a highway, built on a permanent chassis, includes plumbing/heating/electrical systems, and is:

- [x] Less than 45 feet in length (excluding tow bar) while in traveling mode, and designed for dwelling or commercial use if connected to required utilities
- [ ] Less than 20 feet in length and designed only for recreational camping
- [ ] Exactly 45 feet or more in length while in traveling mode
- [ ] Designed exclusively for permanent, non-mobile residential use

**Explanation:** The 45-foot traveling-mode length ceiling and the utility-connection requirement are what distinguish a "house trailer" from both a "towable recreational vehicle" and a permanent manufactured home.

---

### Q6 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(6)

TC 541.201(6) defines "Implement of husbandry" to include a vehicle designed and adapted for tilling the soil (other than a passenger car or truck), and also:

- [x] A towed vehicle that transports to the field and spreads fertilizer or agricultural chemicals, or a motor vehicle designed and adapted to deliver feed to livestock
- [ ] Any vehicle registered under a farm exemption plate
- [ ] A farm tractor used to draw a plow
- [ ] A pickup truck used on a farm

**Explanation:** "Implement of husbandry" and "farm tractor" (541.201(4)) are separate, cross-referencing definitions — a farm tractor is the vehicle that draws an implement of husbandry, not the implement itself.

---

### Q7 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(7)

TC 541.201(7) defines "Light truck" as a truck (including pickup, panel delivery, or carryall trucks) with a manufacturer's rated carrying capacity of:

- [x] 2,000 pounds or less
- [ ] 6,000 pounds or less
- [ ] 26,000 pounds or less
- [ ] 10,000 pounds or less

**Explanation:** 2,000 pounds is the exact statutory ceiling for "light truck" under 541.201(7).

---

### Q8 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(8)

TC 541.201(8) defines "Moped" as a motor vehicle with a rider's saddle, no more than three wheels when propelled, unable to exceed 30 mph, whose engine:

- [x] Cannot produce more than five-brake horsepower, and if internal combustion, has a piston displacement of 50 cubic centimeters or less with no required gear shifting
- [ ] Cannot produce more than 15-brake horsepower and requires manual gear shifting
- [ ] Has a piston displacement of exactly 50 to 150 cubic centimeters
- [ ] Cannot exceed 20 mph regardless of horsepower

**Explanation:** Two thresholds define a moped's engine: five-brake horsepower max, and (if internal combustion) 50cc or less displacement with an automatic power-drive system — this is what separates a moped from a motorcycle for licensing/equipment purposes.

---

### Q9 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(9)

TC 541.201(9) defines "Motorcycle" (general Transportation Code sense) as a motor vehicle, other than a tractor or moped, equipped with a rider's saddle and designed to have when propelled:

- [x] Not more than three wheels on the ground
- [ ] Exactly two wheels on the ground
- [ ] Not more than four wheels on the ground
- [ ] Any number of wheels, so long as it has a saddle

**Explanation:** This general definition (up to three wheels) is broader than the everyday image of a two-wheeled motorcycle — three-wheeled saddle vehicles can qualify here, though TC Chapter 661's motorcycle-specific definition (Sec. 661.001) uses very similar but not identical language (see Q124).

---

### Q10 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(11)

TC 541.201(11) defines "Motor vehicle" as a self-propelled vehicle, or a vehicle propelled by electric power from overhead trolley wires. The term specifically excludes:

- [x] An electric bicycle or an electric personal assistive mobility device (as defined by Sec. 551.201)
- [ ] Any vehicle with fewer than four wheels
- [ ] A moped or a golf cart
- [ ] Any vehicle that cannot exceed 25 mph

**Explanation:** The exclusion of electric bicycles and EPAMDs (Segway-type devices) from "motor vehicle" is deliberate — it keeps those lower-powered devices out of motor-vehicle-specific licensing and registration requirements.

---

### Q11 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(11-a)

TC 541.201(11-a) defines "Multifunction school activity bus" as a bus-safety-standard-compliant vehicle used to transport students on school-related activity trips (not routes to/from school) that specifically lacks which federal school-bus feature?

- [x] Alternately flashing red lights and movable stop arms
- [ ] Seat belts
- [ ] A federally compliant fuel system
- [ ] Emergency exit doors

**Explanation:** A multifunction school activity bus meets school-bus manufacturing standards except for the stop-arm/flashing-red-light requirements — that's the specific feature the statute carves out, and it's what distinguishes this term from an ordinary "school bus."

---

### Q12 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(12)

TC 541.201(12) defines "Passenger car" as a motor vehicle, other than a motorcycle, designed to accommodate how many passengers (including the operator)?

- [x] 10 or fewer
- [ ] 15 or fewer
- [ ] More than 10
- [ ] Exactly 5

**Explanation:** "Passenger car" (10 or fewer) is the mirror image of "bus" (more than 10) under 541.201(3) — the same 10-passenger line separates the two categories.

---

### Q13 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(13)

TC 541.201(13) defines "Pole trailer" as a vehicle without motive power, designed to be drawn by another vehicle and secured by a pole, reach, boom, or similar device, ordinarily used to transport:

- [x] A long or irregularly shaped load (poles, pipes, or structural members) capable of sustaining themselves as beams between supporting connections
- [ ] Livestock or agricultural feed
- [ ] Passengers in an enclosed compartment
- [ ] Loose aggregate material like sand or gravel

**Explanation:** The "beam between supporting connections" language is specific to pole trailers — it's what a semitrailer or ordinary trailer (541.201(17), (20)) is not designed to carry.

---

### Q14 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(13-a)

TC 541.201(13-a) defines "Police vehicle" as a vehicle used by a peace officer (as defined by CCP Art. 2A.001) for law enforcement purposes that is owned/leased by a governmental entity, or:

- [x] Owned/leased by a private institution of higher education's police department that commissions officers under Education Code Sec. 51.212, or a private vehicle owned/leased by the officer and approved for law enforcement use by the officer's agency head or designee
- [ ] Any vehicle with a law enforcement decal affixed to it
- [ ] Any personally owned vehicle used by an off-duty officer for any purpose
- [ ] Only vehicles marked with agency insignia and equipped with emergency lighting

**Explanation:** A private vehicle can qualify as a "police vehicle" if the agency head (or designee) has approved it for law enforcement use — but note the definition's caveat that an unmarked private vehicle may not count as an "authorized emergency vehicle" for certain statutory exemption purposes unless marked.

---

### Q15 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(14)

TC 541.201(14) defines "Road tractor" as a motor vehicle designed and used to draw another vehicle, but not constructed to carry:

- [x] A load independently, or a part of the weight of the other vehicle or its load
- [ ] More than 26,000 pounds gross vehicle weight
- [ ] Passengers of any kind
- [ ] A trailer longer than 45 feet

**Explanation:** Compare to "truck tractor" (541.201(22)), which IS constructed to carry part of the weight of the towed vehicle and its load — a road tractor carries none of that weight, which is the key distinction between the two terms.

---

### Q16 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(15)

TC 541.201(15) defines "School activity bus" as a bus designed to accommodate more than 15 passengers (including the operator), owned/operated/leased by a school entity, used to transport students on school-related activity trips. The term specifically excludes:

- [x] A chartered bus, a mass-transit-authority bus, a school bus, or a multifunction school activity bus
- [ ] Only buses owned outright by the school district (leased buses are included as "school activity buses")
- [ ] Any bus manufactured before the current federal school-bus safety standards took effect
- [ ] Buses used exclusively for routes to and from school

**Explanation:** "School activity bus" is specifically for activity trips (not the daily to/from-school route), accommodates more than 15 passengers, and is a distinct category from "school bus" and "multifunction school activity bus" even though all three transport students.

---

### Q17 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(16)

TC 541.201(16) defines "School bus" as a motor vehicle manufactured in compliance with federal school-bus safety standards, used to transport pre-primary/primary/secondary students on:

- [x] A route to or from school, or a school-related activity trip other than routes to and from school
- [ ] Only routes to and from school, never activity trips
- [ ] Only activity trips, never routes to and from school
- [ ] Any trip, including non-school-related personal use by district employees

**Explanation:** Unlike "school activity bus" (activity trips only) or "multifunction school activity bus" (activity trips only, missing stop-arm features), a "school bus" under 541.201(16) covers both the daily to/from-school route AND activity trips.

---

### Q18 — Concept
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(11-a), (15), (16); TC 545.001(3)

Which statement correctly distinguishes "School Bus," "School Activity Bus," and "Multifunction School Activity Bus"?

- [x] "School bus" covers both to/from-school routes and activity trips and has full federal stop-arm/flashing-light equipment; "school activity bus" (over 15 passengers) and "multifunction school activity bus" are both limited to activity trips only, with the multifunction version specifically lacking the stop-arm/flashing-red-light features; TC 545.001(3) further provides that "school bus" includes a multifunction school activity bus for Chapter 545 purposes
- [ ] All three terms are legally interchangeable with no substantive differences
- [ ] "School activity bus" is the only one of the three required to comply with any federal safety standards
- [ ] "Multifunction school activity bus" is simply a school bus older than 10 years

**Explanation:** These three closely related, easily confused terms are a classic BPOC/TCOLE trap — the key discriminators are (1) route type covered and (2) whether the stop-arm/flashing-light equipment is present, and Sec. 545.001(3) creates a special rule folding "multifunction school activity bus" into "school bus" specifically for Chapter 545's rules-of-the-road purposes (see Q92).

---

### Q19 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(17)

TC 541.201(17) defines "Semitrailer" as a vehicle with or without motive power, other than a pole trailer, designed to be drawn by a motor vehicle, constructed so that:

- [x] Part of the vehicle's weight and load rests on or is carried by another vehicle
- [ ] None of the vehicle's weight and load rests on the motor vehicle
- [ ] All of the vehicle's weight rests on its own structure exclusively
- [ ] It cannot be legally drawn by any motor vehicle

**Explanation:** This "part of the weight rests on another vehicle" feature is exactly what separates "semitrailer" from "trailer" (541.201(20)), where NO part of the weight rests on the towing vehicle.

---

### Q20 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(18)

TC 541.201(18) defines "Special mobile equipment" as a vehicle not designed or used primarily to transport persons or property that is only incidentally operated on a highway, including road construction/maintenance machinery. The definition specifically excludes:

- [x] A vehicle designed to transport persons or property with machinery attached, including a house trailer, dump truck, truck-mounted transit mixer, crane, or shovel
- [ ] Any vehicle used by TxDOT for any purpose
- [ ] Ditchdigging apparatus and well boring apparatus
- [ ] Motor graders and road rollers

**Explanation:** The exclusion clause is the trap here — a dump truck sounds like construction equipment, but because it's primarily designed to transport property (dirt/debris), it's excluded from "special mobile equipment" even though ditchdiggers, graders, and rollers are included.

---

### Q21 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(19)

TC 541.201(19) defines "Towable recreational vehicle" as a nonmotorized vehicle designed to be towable and used for temporary human habitation (camping/seasonal use), permanently built on a single chassis, that may:

- [x] Contain one or more life-support systems, and be used temporarily or permanently for advertising/selling/displaying merchandise, but not for transporting property for hire or private-carrier distribution
- [ ] Never be used for any commercial purpose whatsoever
- [ ] Only be used for permanent, year-round residential habitation
- [ ] Be self-propelled if the motor is under 750 watts

**Explanation:** The "not used for transporting property for hire" limitation distinguishes a towable RV from a commercial cargo trailer that happens to also have living quarters.

---

### Q22 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(20)

TC 541.201(20) defines "Trailer" as a vehicle, other than a pole trailer, with or without motive power, designed to be drawn by a motor vehicle, constructed so that:

- [x] No part of the vehicle's weight and load rests on the motor vehicle
- [ ] Part of the vehicle's weight and load rests on the motor vehicle
- [ ] It must be registered separately from the towing vehicle in all cases
- [ ] It cannot exceed 26 feet in length

**Explanation:** Compare directly to "semitrailer" (541.201(17)) — the presence or absence of shared weight-bearing on the towing vehicle is the single distinguishing fact between the two terms.

---

### Q23 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(21)

TC 541.201(21) defines "Truck" as a motor vehicle designed, used, or maintained primarily to:

- [x] Transport property
- [ ] Transport more than 10 passengers
- [ ] Draw another vehicle without carrying any load itself
- [ ] Operate exclusively off-highway

**Explanation:** This is the broadest, simplest definition in the vehicle-type list — "truck" turns entirely on the primary purpose of transporting property, without a weight or size threshold (those distinctions come in via "light truck," 541.201(7)).

---

### Q24 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(22)

TC 541.201(22) defines "Truck tractor" as a motor vehicle designed and used primarily to draw another vehicle, but not constructed to carry a load other than:

- [x] A part of the weight of the other vehicle and its load
- [ ] Any load whatsoever, including any part of the towed vehicle's weight
- [ ] Passengers, but never cargo
- [ ] Its own fuel and equipment only

**Explanation:** Compare to "road tractor" (541.201(14)), which carries NONE of the towed vehicle's weight — "truck tractor" is the one that does carry part of that weight (this is the classic semi-truck cab/semitrailer combination).

---

### Q25 — Definition
**LO:** 22.1 · **Source:** TC 541.201(23)

TC 541.201(23) defines "Vehicle" (general Transportation Code sense) as a device that can be used to transport or draw persons or property on a highway. The term does not include:

- [x] A device exclusively used on stationary rails or tracks, or manufactured housing as defined by Occupations Code Chapter 1201
- [ ] Any device with fewer than four wheels
- [ ] Bicycles or electric bicycles
- [ ] Any device that is not self-propelled

**Explanation:** "Vehicle" is intentionally the broadest catch-all term in this section (it doesn't even require self-propulsion — that's what makes something a "motor vehicle" specifically) — the only carve-outs are stationary-rail devices (like trains on tracks) and manufactured housing.

## LO 22.2 — Persons, Governmental Authorities, Metropolitan/Restricted Districts, and Related Penal Code Cross-References (TC 541.001, 541.002, 541.101, 541.102; PC 1.07)

### Q26 — Definition
**LO:** 22.2 · **Source:** TC 541.001(1-a)

TC 541.001(1-a) defines "Operator," as used in reference to a vehicle, as a person who:

- [x] Drives or has physical control of a vehicle
- [ ] Holds the vehicle's certificate of title
- [ ] Is listed as the primary driver on the vehicle's insurance policy
- [ ] Has a valid driver's license, regardless of whether they are currently driving

**Explanation:** "Operator" turns on driving/physical control at the relevant time, not on ownership, licensure, or insurance status — a person can be an "operator" even without a license.

---

### Q27 — Definition
**LO:** 22.2 · **Source:** TC 541.001(2)

TC 541.001(2) defines "Owner," as used in reference to a vehicle, as a person with a property interest in or title to the vehicle. This term specifically includes a person entitled to use/possess a vehicle subject to a security interest, but excludes:

- [x] A lienholder and a lessee whose lease is not intended as security
- [ ] Any co-signer on the vehicle's loan
- [ ] A person who has fully paid off the vehicle's loan
- [ ] Any family member who regularly drives the vehicle

**Explanation:** This mirrors the general property-law distinction between holding a security interest (lienholder) versus holding actual ownership/title — a lienholder's interest in the vehicle as collateral doesn't make them the "owner" under this section.

---

### Q28 — Definition
**LO:** 22.2 · **Source:** TC 541.001(3)

TC 541.001(3) defines "Pedestrian" as:

- [x] A person on foot
- [ ] A person on foot or riding a bicycle
- [ ] Any person not currently operating a motor vehicle
- [ ] A person on foot within a marked crosswalk only

**Explanation:** This is a short, literal definition — being "on foot" is the entire test, regardless of location (crosswalk or not) or whether the person is also a "sidewalk user" for other purposes under 541.001(6).

### Q29 — Definition
**LO:** 22.2 · **Source:** TC 541.001(4)

TC 541.001(4) defines "Person" as:

- [x] An individual, firm, partnership, association, or corporation
- [ ] Only a natural human being
- [ ] Any entity registered to do business in Texas
- [ ] An individual or corporation, but not a partnership or association

**Explanation:** Like the Penal Code's broader "person" definition (PC 1.07(a)(38)), this Transportation Code version extends beyond individuals to business entities — firms, partnerships, associations, and corporations all qualify.

### Q30 — Definition
**LO:** 22.2 · **Source:** TC 541.001(5)

TC 541.001(5) defines "School crossing guard" as a responsible person designated by a local authority to direct traffic in a school crossing zone, who must be at least:

- [x] 18 years of age
- [ ] 16 years of age
- [ ] 21 years of age
- [ ] There is no minimum age requirement

**Explanation:** 18 is the statutory floor — a school crossing guard must be an adult, designated by a local authority, for the protection of children going to or leaving school.

TC 541.001(6) defines "Sidewalk user" as an individual lawfully operating one of several listed devices on a sidewalk. Which of the following is included in that list?

- [ ] Only bicycles and electric personal assistive mobility devices
- [ ] Any motorized vehicle capable of operating on a sidewalk
- [ ] Golf carts and neighborhood electric vehicles

### Q34 — Definition
**LO:** 22.2 · **Source:** TC 541.002(3)

TC 541.002(3) defines "Local authority" as a county, municipality, or other local entity authorized to enact traffic laws, or:

- [x] A school district created under Texas law, but only when designating school crossing guards for schools it operates
- [ ] Any homeowners' association within an incorporated city
- [ ] A private university's campus police department
- [ ] Any county regardless of whether it has traffic-law authority

**Explanation:** The school-district prong is narrow and purpose-limited — a school district only counts as a "local authority" for the specific act of designating school crossing guards, not for traffic regulation generally.

### Q35 — Definition
**LO:** 22.2 · **Source:** TC 541.002(4)

TC 541.002(4) defines "Police officer" (Subtitle C general sense) as:

- [x] An officer authorized to direct traffic or arrest persons who violate traffic regulations
- [ ] Any person holding a TCOLE peace officer license
- [ ] An officer employed exclusively by a municipal police department
- [ ] A peace officer as defined by CCP Art. 2A.001, and no one else

**Explanation:** This definition is functional (authorized to direct traffic or arrest traffic violators) rather than tied to a specific licensing status — it's broader in some ways and narrower in others than the CCP's general "peace officer" definition used elsewhere (e.g., in TC 541.201(13-a)'s "police vehicle" definition).

### Q36 — Definition
**LO:** 22.2 · **Source:** TC 541.002(5)

TC 541.002(5) provides that "State" (Subtitle C general sense) has the meaning assigned by Government Code Sec. 311.005, and additionally includes:

- [x] A province of Canada
- [ ] A territory of Mexico
- [ ] Any U.S. territory or possession
- [ ] The District of Columbia only

**Explanation:** This Canada-inclusive definition matters for out-of-state/out-of-country license and traffic-violation reciprocity questions — compare it to TC 522.003(26)'s CDL-specific "state" definition, which is narrower (a U.S. state or D.C. only, no Canadian provinces).

### Q37 — Definition
**LO:** 22.2 · **Source:** TC 541.101

TC 541.101 defines "Metropolitan area" as an area containing at least one municipality with a population of at least 100,000, and that also:

- [x] Includes the adjacent municipalities and unincorporated urban districts
- [ ] Has a total combined population of at least 500,000
- [ ] Is located entirely within a single county
- [ ] Has been formally designated as such by TxDOT

**Explanation:** The 100,000-population anchor municipality, plus its adjacent municipalities and unincorporated urban districts, is the complete two-part test — no separate combined-population or single-county requirement exists.

### Q38 — Definition
**LO:** 22.2 · **Source:** TC 541.102(1)

TC 541.102(1) defines "Business district" as territory adjacent to and including a highway where qualifying buildings (hotel, bank, office, public building, railroad station, etc.) are located within a 600-foot segment along the highway, and within that segment the buildings occupy at least:

- [x] 300 feet of frontage, on one side of the highway or collectively on both sides
- [ ] 600 feet of frontage on one side of the highway only
- [ ] 100 feet of frontage on each side of the highway separately
- [ ] Half of the total 600-foot segment's length on each side

**Explanation:** The 600-foot segment / 300-foot frontage test is specific and numeric — this matters because "business district" affects statutory speed limits and other traffic rules elsewhere in the Transportation Code.

### Q39 — Definition
**LO:** 22.2 · **Source:** TC 541.102(2)

TC 541.102(2) defines "Residence district" as territory (other than a business district) adjacent to and including a highway, where at least 300 feet of highway frontage is primarily improved with:

- [x] Residences, or buildings used for business purposes and residences
- [ ] Residences only, with no business use whatsoever
- [ ] At least 10 separate residential structures
- [ ] Any structure used for dwelling purposes, regardless of frontage footage

**Explanation:** "Residence district" allows for some mixed business/residential frontage, as long as it's not already a "business district" and meets the same 300-foot frontage threshold used for business districts.

### Q40 — Definition
**LO:** 22.2 · **Source:** TC 541.102(3)

TC 541.102(3) defines "Urban district" as territory adjacent to and including a highway that is not in a municipality, and is improved with structures used for business/industry/dwelling located at intervals of less than 100 feet for a distance of at least:

- [x] One-quarter mile on either side of the highway
- [ ] One-half mile on either side of the highway
- [ ] One mile on either side of the highway
- [ ] 600 feet on either side of the highway

**Explanation:** Unlike "business district" and "residence district" (which can be inside a municipality), "urban district" specifically applies to unincorporated territory that has taken on a dense, urbanized character.

### Q41 — Definition
**LO:** 22.2 · **Source:** PC 1.07(a)(8)

PC 1.07(a)(8), cross-referenced by BPOC Chapter 22, defines "Bodily injury" as:

- [x] Physical pain, illness, or any impairment of physical condition
- [ ] An injury that creates a substantial risk of death
- [ ] Any injury requiring hospitalization
- [ ] Physical pain lasting more than 24 hours

**Explanation:** This is the low-bar injury standard — even brief physical pain qualifies — distinct from the higher "serious bodily injury" standard tested in Q42.

### Q42 — Definition
**LO:** 22.2 · **Source:** PC 1.07(a)(46)

- [ ] Is always privately owned
- [ ] Is limited to pedestrian use only
- [ ] Must be less than 20 feet wide

**Explanation:** The "not for through traffic, provides rear access" functional test is what distinguishes an "alley" from an ordinary "highway or street" (541.302(5)).

**Explanation:** This is a two-prong definition — a marked crossing anywhere on a roadway, OR an unmarked but implied crossing at any intersection defined by the extension of the sidewalk lines — meaning crosswalks legally exist at most intersections even without painted lines.

- [ ] A highway with at least three lanes in each direction
- [ ] Any toll-free highway maintained by TxDOT

**Explanation:** The two required features are "divided" and "controlled-access" — a toll-free, high-speed, or multi-lane highway that lacks controlled access is not a "freeway" under this definition.

TC 541.302(4) defines "Freeway main lane" as:

- [ ] The leftmost lane of a freeway only
- [ ] Any lane on a freeway, without distinction

**Explanation:** "Freeway main lane" specifically excludes ramps and other interrupted-flow lanes — it's the through-traffic lane, which matters for statutes that impose special rules for stopping or crossing main lanes.

### Q51 — Definition
**LO:** 22.3 · **Source:** TC 541.302(5)

TC 541.302(5) defines "Highway or street" as:

- [x] The width between the boundary lines of a publicly maintained way, any part of which is open to the public for vehicular travel

- [ ] Any privately maintained road open to public use
- [ ] A way maintained exclusively by the state, not counties or municipalities

**Explanation:** "Publicly maintained" and "open to the public for vehicular travel" (even in part) are the two requirements — private roads and driveways are covered by a separate definition (541.302(9)).

TC 541.302(6) defines "Improved shoulder" as:

- [x] A paved shoulder
- [ ] Any shoulder wider than eight feet
- [ ] A shoulder marked with rumble strips
- [ ] A shoulder designed for emergency vehicle use only

**Explanation:** This is a one-clause definition — "improved" simply means paved, as opposed to a gravel or dirt shoulder.

### Q53 — Definition

- [x] Divided into at least two clearly marked lanes for vehicular travel
- [ ] Divided into at least four clearly marked lanes
- [ ] Marked with a center double-yellow line only
- [ ] Restricted to one direction of travel

**Explanation:** Two clearly marked lanes is the minimum threshold — this matters for statutes governing lane changes and driving within a single marked lane.

---

### Q54 — Definition
**LO:** 22.3 · **Source:** TC 541.302(8)

TC 541.302(8) defines "Limited-access or controlled-access highway" as a highway to which persons (including abutting property owners) have no right of access, and access is:

- [ ] Completely prohibited to all vehicles except emergency vehicles
- [ ] Open only during daylight hours
- [ ] Restricted solely to vehicles with a valid TxDOT permit

**Explanation:** The key feature is that access is legally restricted to designated points (like interchanges/ramps) rather than allowed anywhere along the highway's length — this is what a "freeway" (541.302(3)) typically is.

---

### Q55 — Definition
**LO:** 22.3 · **Source:** TC 541.302(9)

TC 541.302(9) defines "Private road or driveway" as a privately owned way used for vehicular travel and used only by:

- [x] The owner and persons who have the owner's express or implied permission
- [ ] The owner exclusively, with no exceptions
- [ ] Any member of the public, provided no gate blocks entry
- [ ] Emergency services personnel and the owner only

**Explanation:** "Express or implied permission" is a fairly broad standard — a visitor, delivery driver, or guest with the owner's implied consent to be there is still using the road lawfully under this definition.

---

### Q56 — Definition
**LO:** 22.3 · **Source:** TC 541.302(10)

TC 541.302(10) defines "Ramp" as an interconnecting roadway of a traffic interchange, or a connecting roadway between highways at different levels or between parallel highways, that allows a vehicle to:

- [x] Enter or exit a roadway
- [ ] Change lanes within the same roadway only
- [ ] Cross a median without a designated crossover
- [ ] Park temporarily while waiting to merge

**Explanation:** A "ramp" is specifically the connector roadway used to enter or exit — a "freeway main lane" (541.302(4)) is explicitly NOT a ramp, since main lanes are for uninterrupted through traffic.

### Q57 — Definition
**LO:** 22.3 · **Source:** TC 541.302(11)

TC 541.302(11) defines "Roadway" as the portion of a highway, other than the berm or shoulder, that is:

- [ ] Any paved surface within the highway's boundary lines, including the shoulder
- [ ] Only the portion marked with painted lane lines
- [ ] The entire width between property lines, including sidewalks

**Explanation:** "Roadway" explicitly excludes the berm/shoulder — and on a divided highway with two separate roadways (e.g., separated by a median), each roadway is treated as its own distinct "roadway" for legal purposes.

---

### Q58 — Definition
**LO:** 22.3 · **Source:** TC 541.302(12)

TC 541.302(12) defines "Safety zone" as the area in a roadway officially designated for exclusive:

- [x] Pedestrian use, protected or marked by adequate signs so as to be plainly visible at all times while so designated
- [ ] Emergency vehicle staging during a crash response
- [ ] School bus loading and unloading
- [ ] Bicycle and motor-assisted scooter use

**Explanation:** A "safety zone" is a pedestrian-refuge area within the roadway itself (commonly seen at streetcar/transit stops) — it's a distinct concept from a "school crossing zone" (541.302(13)).

---

### Q59 — Definition
**LO:** 22.3 · **Source:** TC 541.302(13)

TC 541.302(13) defines "School crossing zone" as a reduced-speed zone designated on a street by a local authority to facilitate safe crossing of the street by children going to or leaving:

- [ ] Any school, including colleges and universities, at all times
- [ ] A public elementary school only
- [ ] Any school, but only during the first and last hour of the school day

**Explanation:** "School crossing zone" is the reduced-speed area itself (a distance along the street), while "school crosswalk" (541.302(14)) is the specific marked crossing point — both are limited to elementary/secondary schools, public or private.

---

### Q60 — Definition
**LO:** 22.3 · **Source:** TC 541.302(14)

TC 541.302(14) defines "School crosswalk" as a crosswalk designated on a street by a local authority to facilitate safe crossing by children going to or leaving:

- [x] A public or private elementary or secondary school
- [ ] Any location within a designated school crossing zone
- [ ] A public school only, not a private school
- [ ] Any crosswalk within 1,000 feet of a school

**Explanation:** "School crosswalk" is the marked crossing point; "school crossing zone" (541.302(13)) is the surrounding reduced-speed area — a school crossing zone can contain one or more school crosswalks.

---

### Q61 — Definition
**LO:** 22.3 · **Source:** TC 541.302(15)

TC 541.302(15) defines "Shoulder" as the portion of a highway adjacent to the roadway that is designed or ordinarily used for parking, distinguished from the roadway by different design/construction/marking, and:

- [x] Not intended for normal vehicular travel
- [ ] Always paved and clearly marked with a white line
- [ ] Reserved exclusively for law enforcement and emergency vehicle use
- [ ] At least 10 feet wide

**Explanation:** "Shoulder" is defined by its function (not intended for normal travel, used for parking) rather than by construction material — that distinction belongs to "improved shoulder" (541.302(6)), which specifically means a paved one.

---

### Q62 — Definition
**LO:** 22.3 · **Source:** TC 541.302(16)

TC 541.302(16) defines "Sidewalk" as the portion of a street that is between a curb or lateral line of a roadway and the adjacent property line, and:

- [x] Intended for pedestrian use
- [ ] Paved with concrete specifically (asphalt sidewalks don't qualify)
- [ ] At least four feet wide
- [ ] Located only within a municipality's incorporated limits

**Explanation:** This simple, functional definition ("intended for pedestrian use," located between the roadway and the property line) is what makes bicycles, motor-assisted scooters, and EPAMDs "sidewalk users" under Sec. 541.001(6) when lawfully operated there.

**LO:** 22.3 · **Source:** TC 542.202(b)(2)

TC 542.202(b)(2) defines "Through highway" as a highway or portion of a highway on which vehicular traffic is given preferential right-of-way, and traffic entering from an intersecting highway is required by law to:

- [ ] Come to a complete stop, regardless of any posted sign or signal
- [ ] Always yield to pedestrians only, not to vehicular traffic
- [ ] Merge only at designated on-ramps

**Explanation:** A local authority has the power under TC 542.202(a)(9) to designate a highway as a "through highway" — once designated, entering traffic must yield per the controlling sign or signal, whether that's a stop sign, yield sign, or signal.

- [ ] Two alleys
- [ ] A private driveway and a highway
- [ ] Two one-way streets

**Explanation:** Subsection (c) adds an important wrinkle: where a highway has two roadways at least 30 feet apart, each roadway's junction with an intersecting highway is treated as a SEPARATE intersection — relevant for divided highways with wide medians.

- [ ] From 6:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m.
- [ ] Beginning one hour before sunrise and ending one hour after sunset

**Explanation:** The half-hour buffer on each end (not a full hour, and not exact sunrise/sunset) is the precise statutory window — it directly determines when equipment like headlamps become legally required.

- [ ] The empty (unladen) weight of the vehicle only
- [ ] The manufacturer's maximum rated weight capacity
- [ ] The weight of the vehicle, its load, and its trailer combined

**Explanation:** "Gross vehicle weight" is actual weight-plus-load, distinct from "gross vehicle weight rating" (TC 522.003(18), Q82), which is the manufacturer's specified maximum loaded weight rating rather than an actual measured weight.

---

### Q70 — Definition
**LO:** 22.4 · **Source:** TC 541.401(5)

TC 541.401(5) defines "Nighttime" as the period:

- [x] Beginning one-half hour after sunset and ending one-half hour before sunrise
- [ ] Beginning at sunset and ending at sunrise exactly
- [ ] From 6:00 p.m. to 6:00 a.m.
- [ ] Beginning one hour after sunset and ending one hour before sunrise

**Explanation:** "Nighttime" and "Daytime" (Q68) are complementary half-hour-buffer definitions that, together, cover the full 24-hour day with no gap.

---

### Q71 — Definition
**LO:** 22.4 · **Source:** TC 541.401(6)

TC 541.401(6) defines "Park" or "parking" as to stand an occupied or unoccupied vehicle, other than temporarily while:

- [x] Loading or unloading merchandise or passengers
- [ ] Waiting for a traffic signal to change
- [ ] Receiving or discharging passengers only (not merchandise)
- [ ] Stopped due to traffic congestion

**Explanation:** The "loading or unloading" exception is what separates "park/parking" from a brief, legitimate stop — compare this to "stand/standing" (541.401(9)), which has a different, narrower exception (receiving/discharging passengers only).

---

### Q72 — Definition
**LO:** 22.4 · **Source:** TC 541.401(7)

TC 541.401(7) defines "Personal injury" as:

- [x] An injury to any part of the human body that requires treatment
- [ ] Any injury, regardless of whether treatment is required
- [ ] An injury that creates a substantial risk of death
- [ ] Any injury resulting in property damage exceeding $1,000

**Explanation:** "Requires treatment" is the operative qualifier — this Transportation Code definition is distinct from (and lower-bar than) the Penal Code's "bodily injury" (Q41) and "serious bodily injury" (Q42) standards, and it's specifically what triggers TC 550.021's duty-following-collision reporting requirements.

---

### Q73 — Definition
**LO:** 22.4 · **Source:** TC 541.401(8)

TC 541.401(8) defines "Right-of-way" as the right of one vehicle or pedestrian to proceed in a lawful manner in preference to another that is approaching from a direction, speed, and proximity that could cause a collision unless:

- [x] One grants precedence to the other
- [ ] Both parties come to a complete stop first
- [ ] A traffic-control signal explicitly assigns priority
- [ ] The approaching vehicle is traveling faster than the posted speed limit

**Explanation:** "Right-of-way" is a relative concept — it only becomes legally relevant when a collision risk exists between two parties approaching each other, and it requires one to yield precedence to the other.

---

### Q74 — Definition
**LO:** 22.4 · **Source:** TC 541.401(9)

TC 541.401(9) defines "Stand" or "standing" as to halt an occupied or unoccupied vehicle, other than temporarily while:

- [x] Receiving or discharging passengers
- [ ] Loading or unloading merchandise or passengers
- [ ] Waiting at a stop sign or red light
- [ ] Parked in a designated parking space

**Explanation:** Compare directly to "park/parking" (541.401(6)) — "standing" has a narrower exception (passengers only, not merchandise), which is why a delivery truck loading boxes at the curb is "parking," not "standing."

---

### Q75 — Definition
**LO:** 22.4 · **Source:** TC 541.401(10)

TC 541.401(10) defines "Stop" or "stopping." When stopping is required, it means to completely cease movement; when stopping is prohibited, it means to halt (including momentarily) an occupied or unoccupied vehicle, unless necessary to:

- [x] Avoid conflict with other traffic, or to comply with the directions of a police officer or a traffic-control sign or signal
- [ ] Allow a passenger to exit the vehicle
- [ ] Check a mobile device or GPS navigation
- [ ] Adjust the vehicle's mirrors or seat position

**Explanation:** This two-part definition matters because "stop" means something different depending on context — a full cessation of movement where stopping is legally required (like at a stop sign), versus even a brief halt where stopping is prohibited (like a no-stopping zone), with a narrow safety/compliance exception either way.

- [ ] Two or more persons who commit a single criminal act together

**Explanation:** The BPOC material is explicit that a motorcycle club's group-riding activity, distinctive clothing, and "flying their colors" do NOT by themselves make it a criminal street gang — the statutory definition turns on continuous/regular association specifically in the commission of criminal activities, not on appearance or affiliation alone. This distinction matters directly for avoiding profiling-based stops of motorcyclists.

## LO 22.25 — Vehicle Registration Definitions (TC 502.001)

### Q126 — Definition
**LO:** 22.25 · **Source:** TC 502.001(7)

TC 502.001(7) defines "Commercial motor vehicle" (registration context) as a motor vehicle, other than a motorcycle or moped, designed or used primarily to transport property, which specifically includes a reconstructed passenger car used primarily for delivery, but excludes:

- [x] A passenger car used to deliver the United States mail
- [ ] Any vehicle with a gross vehicle weight rating under 10,000 pounds
- [ ] Pickup trucks used for personal, non-commercial purposes
- [ ] Any vehicle registered to an individual rather than a business entity

**Explanation:** The mail-delivery passenger car exclusion is specific and easy to overlook — compare this registration-context definition to the different thresholds used in Chapter 548 (inspection, Q108) and Chapter 522 (CDL, Q90).

### Q127 — Definition
**LO:** 22.25 · **Source:** TC 502.001(15)

TC 502.001(15) defines "Farm semitrailer" or "farm trailer" as a vehicle designed and used primarily as:

- [x] A farm vehicle
- [ ] An implement of husbandry, as defined by TC 541.201(6)
- [ ] Any trailer registered under a farm exemption plate, regardless of actual use
- [ ] A vehicle used exclusively to transport livestock

**Explanation:** This is a short, purpose-based definition — "used primarily as a farm vehicle" is the entire test, without the more elaborate multi-part structure used for "implement of husbandry."

### Q128 — Definition
**LO:** 22.25 · **Source:** TC 502.001(30)

TC 502.001(30) defines "Operate temporarily on the highways" as to travel between different farms, between a place of supply/storage and a farm, or:

- [x] Between an owner's farm and the place at which the owner's farm produce is prepared for market or is marketed
- [ ] Between any two points within the same county
- [ ] Between a farm and any retail location, regardless of distance
- [ ] For a cumulative total of no more than 30 days per calendar year

**Explanation:** This definition supports certain limited registration exemptions for farm vehicles that only occasionally use public highways for these specific farm-related trip purposes.

### Q129 — Definition
**LO:** 22.25 · **Source:** TC 502.001(33)

TC 502.001(33) defines "Power sweeper" as an implement (with or without motive power) designed for the removal of debris/dirt/gravel/litter/sand from paved surfaces using:

- [x] A broom, vacuum, or regenerative air system
- [ ] A rotating brush only, with no vacuum or air-based option

- [ ] Manual labor assisted by mechanical conveyor belts

**Explanation:** The definition also clarifies that a vehicle with the sweeping implement permanently mounted still counts as a "power sweeper," provided the vehicle is used only as a power sweeper.

### Q130 — Definition
**LO:** 22.25 · **Source:** TC 502.001(36)

TC 502.001(36) defines "Public property" as property:

- [x] Owned or leased by this state or a political subdivision of this state
- [ ] Open to public access, regardless of ownership
- [ ] Owned by the federal government only
- [ ] Owned by any governmental entity, including out-of-state or foreign governments

**Explanation:** "Public property" turns on ownership/leasehold by Texas or one of its political subdivisions (counties, municipalities, etc.) — mere public accessibility of privately owned property (like a shopping center) doesn't make it "public property" under this definition.

## LO 22.26 — Vehicle Size and Weight Definitions (TC 621.001)

### Q131 — Definition
**LO:** 22.26 · **Source:** TC 621.001(1)

TC 621.001(1) defines "Commercial motor vehicle" (size-and-weight context) as a motor vehicle, other than a motorcycle, designed or used for:

- [x] The transportation of property, or delivery purposes
- [ ] The transportation of 15 or more passengers
- [ ] Any purpose, provided its GVWR exceeds 26,000 pounds
- [ ] Hauling hazardous materials specifically

**Explanation:** This is yet another distinct "commercial motor vehicle" definition specific to Chapter 621's size-and-weight rules — simpler than the registration (Q126), inspection (Q108), or CDL (Q90) versions, since it doesn't specify weight or passenger thresholds at all.

---

### Q132 — Definition
**LO:** 22.26 · **Source:** TC 621.001(6)

TC 621.001(6) defines "Semitrailer" (size-and-weight context) as a vehicle without motive power designed or used with a motor vehicle so that:

- [x] Some of its weight and the weight of its load rests on or is carried by the motor vehicle
- [ ] None of its weight rests on the towing motor vehicle
- [ ] All of its weight rests on the towing motor vehicle
- [ ] It is towed exclusively by another semitrailer, never a motor vehicle directly

**Explanation:** This tracks the same "partial weight transfer" concept used in the general 541.201(17) semitrailer definition (Q19), applied specifically within the vehicle size-and-weight chapter.

---

### Q133 — Definition
**LO:** 22.26 · **Source:** TC 621.001(7)

TC 621.001(7) defines "Trailer" (size-and-weight context) as a vehicle without motive power designed or used to carry property or passengers on its own structure exclusively, and:

- [x] Drawn by a motor vehicle
- [ ] Self-propelled but not exceeding 25 mph
- [ ] Carried entirely on another vehicle's structure
- [ ] Designed to be drawn only by another trailer

**Explanation:** "Its own structure exclusively" is the key phrase distinguishing "trailer" from "semitrailer" (Q132) in this chapter — a trailer bears none of its weight on the towing vehicle, consistent with the general definition at 541.201(20).

### Q134 — Definition
**LO:** 22.26 · **Source:** TC 621.001(9)

TC 621.001(9) defines "Vehicle" (size-and-weight context) as a mechanical device, other than one moved by human power or used exclusively on stationary rails/tracks, in which a person or property can be transported on a public highway. The term specifically includes a motor vehicle, commercial motor vehicle, truck-tractor, trailer, or semitrailer, but excludes:

- [x] Manufactured housing as defined by Occupations Code Chapter 1201
- [ ] Any vehicle registered outside the state of Texas
- [ ] Bicycles and electric bicycles
- [ ] Farm tractors and implements of husbandry

**Explanation:** This size-and-weight "vehicle" definition adds an explicit "mechanical device...other than one moved by human power" clause (ruling out bicycles by definition, rather than needing a separate express exclusion), while still excluding manufactured housing just like the general 541.201(23) definition does.

## LO 22.33 / 22.37 / 22.38 — Crash Investigation and Traffic Control Terms (Embedded BPOC Definitions)

### Q135 — Definition
**LO:** 22.33 · **Source:** BPOC 22.33

BPOC Chapter 22.33 defines a "Nomograph" as:

- [x] A graph on which three or more scales are arranged so that a straight line drawn through values on any two will cross the third at a corresponding value
- [ ] A digital app used to calculate vehicle speed from skid-mark length
- [ ] A written narrative description of a crash scene
- [ ] A scaled diagram of a crash scene drawn to a specific ratio

**Explanation:** A nomograph is a graphical calculation tool (not a piece of software or a scene diagram) — it's used in crash investigation to relate multiple physical measurements (like speed, distance, and drag factor) to each other visually.

---

### Q136 — Definition
**LO:** 22.37 · **Source:** BPOC 22.37; Traffic Incident Management Handbook (FHWA Office of Travel Management)

BPOC Chapter 22.37, citing the Traffic Incident Management Handbook, defines an "Incident" as:

- [x] Any non-recurring event that causes a reduction of roadway capacity or an abnormal increase in demand and requires a law enforcement response
- [ ] Any event resulting in a fatality or serious bodily injury
- [ ] Any traffic crash reported using a CR-3 form
- [ ] Any planned road closure for scheduled maintenance

**Explanation:** "Incident" is deliberately broad and includes non-crash events (spilled cargo, disabled vehicles, special events like parades or concerts) as long as they reduce roadway capacity or spike demand and need a law enforcement response — a scheduled, planned closure is explicitly the opposite of "non-recurring."

### Q137 — Concept
**LO:** 22.38 · **Source:** BPOC 22.38; FHWA Safe and Effective Use of Law Enforcement Personnel in Work Zones

BPOC Chapter 22.38 identifies five areas of a work zone that equipment placement should cover. What are they, in order from the point where traffic first encounters the zone?

- [x] Advanced warning area, transition area, buffer area, work area, termination area
- [ ] Approach area, merge area, activity area, clearance area, resumption area
- [ ] Warning zone, taper zone, work zone, clearance zone, exit zone
- [ ] Detection area, response area, containment area, recovery area, clearance area

**Explanation:** In order: the advanced warning area alerts traffic to what's ahead; the transition area moves traffic out of the incident's path; the buffer area protects victims/responders; the work area is where responders actually work; and the termination area lets traffic resume normal flow. Note the BPOC material also flags "activity area" as an informal term sometimes used to describe the combined buffer + work space, not a sixth distinct area.

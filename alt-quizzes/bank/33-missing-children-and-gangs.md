# Chapter 33: Juvenile Offenders — Missing Children, AMBER Alert & Gang Involvement — Question Bank

*Covers LOs 33.18-33.25 (Unit Goals 2-3: Conducting investigations involving missing children and
missing persons; Juvenile issues related to youth involvement in gangs). LOs 33.18-33.23 are built
primarily on Code of Criminal Procedure Chapter 63 and Government Code Chapter 411, Subchapter L
(AMBER Alert) — this material overlaps heavily with the existing Chapter 18 banks
(`18-missing-exploited-persons.md`, `18-offense-penalties.md`), so this file focuses on the AMBER
Alert statute in full detail and the CCP 63 provisions specific to Chapter 33's LO list, rather than
re-covering Chapter 18's broader missing/exploited-persons material. LOs 33.24-33.25 (gang involvement)
are conceptual/lecture-based rather than statute-based, per the BPOC material — Source is marked
"BPOC 33.24/33.25" accordingly.*

## LO 33.18 — Legal Definitions for a Missing Person or Missing Child

### Q1 — Definition
**LO:** 33.18 · **Source:** CCP 63.001

For purposes of Chapter 33's missing-child provisions, a "child" under CCP 63.001 is a person:

- [x] Under 18 years of age
- [ ] Under 17 years of age
- [ ] Under 21 years of age
- [ ] Under 16 years of age

**Explanation:** CCP 63.001 defines "child" as a person under 18 for purposes of the Texas Missing Persons and Missing Children's Act — a broader age band than the Family Code's "child" definition used elsewhere in Chapter 33 (10-16, or 17 for pre-17 conduct).

---

### Q2 — Recall
**LO:** 33.18 · **Source:** CCP 63.001

Under CCP 63.001, who qualifies as a "legal custodian" of a missing child for reporting purposes?

- [x] A parent, guardian, or other person or agency entitled to legal custody of the child
- [ ] Only a person named in a will
- [ ] Only the Texas Department of Family and Protective Services
- [ ] Only a court-appointed guardian ad litem

**Explanation:** CCP 63.001's "legal custodian" definition tracks whoever holds legal custody of the child — a parent, guardian, or other custodial person/agency — not a narrower category.

What is the "clearinghouse" referenced throughout CCP Chapter 63?

- [x] The missing children and missing persons information clearinghouse operated as part of the statewide reporting system

## LO 33.20 — Law Enforcement Requirements Once a Child Is Reported Missing

### Q5 — Recall
**LO:** 33.20 · **Source:** CCP 63.009

After receiving a report of a missing child, within how long must the agency enter the child into the clearinghouse and NCIC with all available identifying information?

- [x] Not later than two hours after receiving the report
- [ ] Not later than 24 hours after receiving the report
- [ ] Not later than 48 hours after receiving the report
- [ ] Immediately, with no defined outer limit

**Explanation:** CCP 63.009 sets a strict two-hour deadline for both the clearinghouse/NCIC entry and the TLETS entry — there is no waiting period for taking a missing-child report in the first place.

---

### Q6 — Recall
**LO:** 33.20 · **Source:** CCP 63.009

Not later than how many hours after receiving a missing-child report must an agency electronically submit the report to each municipal or county law enforcement agency within 200 miles?

- [x] 48 hours
- [ ] 2 hours
- [ ] 24 hours
- [ ] 72 hours

**Explanation:** CCP 63.009 sets this wider-radius-notification deadline at 48 hours, distinct from the 2-hour clearinghouse/NCIC/TLETS entry deadline and the 30-day deadline for NamUS entry.

---

### Q7 — Recall
**LO:** 33.20 · **Source:** CCP 63.009

Not later than what deadline must an agency enter the missing child's name into the National Missing & Unidentified Persons System (NamUS)?

- [x] The 30th day after receiving the report
- [ ] The 2nd hour after receiving the report
- [ ] The 48th hour after receiving the report
- [ ] The 7th day after receiving the report

**Explanation:** CCP 63.009 sets the NamUS entry deadline at 30 days — the longest of the four staged deadlines (2 hours for clearinghouse/NCIC, 2 hours for TLETS, 48 hours for the 200-mile agency notification, and 30 days for NamUS).

---

### Q8 — Scenario
**LO:** 33.20 · **Source:** CCP 63.0091

A missing child report involves a child who disappeared while in a dangerous environment and was last known to be communicating with an adult unknown to the child's family. Under CCP 63.0091, what must the agency do?

- [x] Reasonably escalate the response and, not later than two hours, notify all law enforcement agencies within 100 miles (including other states) of the high-risk designation
- [ ] Wait 24 hours to confirm the risk factors before escalating
- [ ] Notify only the clearinghouse, with no expanded-radius notification
- [ ] Treat the case identically to a standard missing-child report with no additional steps

**Explanation:** CCP 63.0091 creates a heightened response for specifically enumerated high-risk factors (trafficking/sexual assault/exploitation/neglect risk, dangerous environment, mental/behavioral needs, intellectual/developmental disability, or contact with an unknown adult) — including a 100-mile, 2-hour, multi-state notification requirement.

---

### Q9 — Concept
**LO:** 33.20 · **Source:** BPOC 33.20

If a law enforcement agency, upon locating a child taken or retained without the custodian's permission, has reason to believe the child is a victim of abuse or neglect, what must the agency do?

- [x] Notify DFPS, which may then take possession of the child and initiate its own investigation
- [ ] Release the child back to the taking party without further action
- [ ] Close the case immediately since the child was located
- [ ] Refer the matter only to the school district

**Explanation:** Per BPOC 33.20 (and the CCP 63.009 framework), locating the child doesn't end the agency's obligations — a reasonable belief of abuse/neglect triggers a DFPS notification, and DFPS may then investigate and take possession.

## LO 33.21 — Steps in a Missing Child Investigation

### Q10 — Recall
**LO:** 33.21 · **Source:** BPOC 33.21; CCP 63.011

When taking an initial missing-child report, what waiting period applies before an officer may begin the investigation?

- [x] None — there is no waiting period
- [ ] 24 hours
- [ ] 48 hours

- [ ] 60 days; 120 days if born outside the U.S.
- [ ] 7 days; 14 days if born outside the U.S.

### Q12 — Recall

- [ ] Only the county clerk's office

**Explanation:** CCP 63.020 ties the school/day-care/vital-records flagging duty specifically to missing children under 11 years old — entities that receive the notice must then flag the child's records.

### Q13 — Recall

## LO 33.23 — Activating the Statewide AMBER Alert System

### Q16 — Definition
**LO:** 33.23 · **Source:** GV 411.351(1)

Under GV 411.351, an "abducted child" is a child of what age or younger whose whereabouts are unknown and whose disappearance poses a credible threat to safety/health as determined by local law enforcement?

- [x] 17 years of age or younger
- [ ] 14 years of age or younger
- [ ] 18 years of age or younger
- [ ] 12 years of age or younger

**Explanation:** GV 411.351(1) sets the "abducted child" age ceiling at 17 for AMBER Alert purposes — note this is a different age threshold than CCP 63.001's "child" (under 18) or the Family Code's "child" (10-16, generally).

---

### Q17 — Recall
**LO:** 33.23 · **Source:** GV 411.351(2), (2-a)

What does the statewide "alert system" under GV 411.351 cover, beyond abducted children?

- [x] Missing persons with an intellectual disability
- [ ] Missing pets and livestock
- [ ] Runaway juveniles who left voluntarily with no danger indicators
- [ ] Missing adults with no reported disability of any kind

**Explanation:** GV 411.351(2) defines the "alert system" as covering both abducted children AND missing persons with an intellectual disability (itself defined in 411.351(2-a) by reference to significantly subaverage intellectual functioning with adaptive-behavior deficits originating in the developmental period).

---

### Q18 — Recall
**LO:** 33.23 · **Source:** GV 411.352

Under GV 411.352, which department is responsible for developing and implementing the statewide AMBER Alert system, in cooperation with TxDOT, the governor's office, and other agencies?

- [x] The Department of Public Safety
- [ ] The Texas Juvenile Justice Department
- [ ] The Department of Family and Protective Services
- [ ] The Texas Education Agency

**Explanation:** GV 411.352 assigns the statewide alert-system development/implementation role to "the department" — DPS, per the chapter's defined terms — working with TxDOT, the governor's office, and other law enforcement agencies.

---

### Q19 — Recall
**LO:** 33.23 · **Source:** GV 411.355(a)

Under GV 411.355(a), for AMBER Alert activation regarding an abducted child, the taking person must be more than how many years older than the child, and unrelated to the child by any degree of consanguinity or affinity?

- [x] More than three years older
- [ ] More than one year older
- [ ] More than five years older
- [ ] Any age difference qualifies, as long as they are unrelated

**Explanation:** GV 411.355(a)(1)(B) requires the abductor to be more than three years older than the child AND unrelated by any degree of consanguinity/affinity, as one of the activation criteria for a child under 14 taken without parental/guardian permission.

---

### Q20 — Recall
**LO:** 33.23 · **Source:** GV 411.355(a)

Beyond the age/relationship criteria, GV 411.355(a) requires the local law enforcement agency to believe what about the abducted child's situation before activation?

- [x] The child is in immediate danger of serious bodily injury, death, or becoming a victim of sexual assault
- [ ] The child has been missing for at least 72 hours
- [ ] The child was last seen more than 50 miles from home
- [ ] The child has a documented history of running away

**Explanation:** GV 411.355(a)(2) requires belief of immediate danger of serious bodily injury, death, or sexual assault, in addition to (a)(1)'s abduction-circumstance criteria, (a)(3)'s preliminary-investigation verification requirement, and (a)(4)'s sufficient-information-to-disseminate requirement.

---

### Q21 — Scenario
**LO:** 33.23 · **Source:** GV 411.355(b)

For AMBER Alert activation regarding a missing person with an intellectual disability, GV 411.355(b) requires local law enforcement to verify what, at the time the person is reported missing?

- [x] The person has an intellectual disability (per the HSC 593.005 procedure) and the person's location is unknown, plus a credible threat to health/safety and sufficient dissemination information
- [ ] The person is under 18 years of age
- [ ] The person has a criminal record
- [ ] The person was last seen with a family member

**Explanation:** GV 411.355(b) sets a parallel four-part activation test for missing persons with an intellectual disability: verified disability status (per HSC 593.005), unknown location, a credible threat to health/safety, and sufficient information to disseminate publicly.

### Q22 — Recall
**LO:** 33.23 · **Source:** GV 411.356

Before requesting activation of the AMBER Alert system, what must a local law enforcement agency do under GV 411.356?

### Q24 — Recall
**LO:** 33.23 · **Source:** GV 411.358

Under GV 411.358, the director shall terminate an AMBER Alert activation for a particular case under which circumstance?

- [x] The abducted child/missing person is recovered or the situation is otherwise resolved, or the alert is no longer an effective tool for locating/recovering them

## LO 33.25 — Important Points on Youth Gang Involvement

### Q31 — True/False
**LO:** 33.25 · **Source:** BPOC 33.25

True or false: Per BPOC, the presence of multiple risk-factor domains in a youth's life increases the likelihood of gang involvement.

- [x] True
- [ ] False

**Explanation:** BPOC 33.25 lists this as one of the three key points — risk is cumulative across the five domains, not confined to any single factor.

---

### Q32 — True/False
**LO:** 33.25 · **Source:** BPOC 33.25

True or false: Per BPOC, most youth are forced to join gangs against their will.

- [ ] True
- [x] False

**Explanation:** BPOC 33.25 states the opposite — most youth are NOT forced to join; gang involvement is generally understood as filling needs the youth perceives the gang as meeting (status, belonging, protection, etc.), not coercion.

---

### Q33 — Concept
**LO:** 33.25 · **Source:** BPOC 33.25

Per BPOC 33.25, is joining a gang treated as a permanent, irreversible decision?

- [x] No — joining a gang is not a permanent decision
- [ ] Yes — BPOC treats gang membership as permanent for all legal and practical purposes
- [ ] Yes, but only for youth over the age of 15
- [ ] The material takes no position on this point

**Explanation:** BPOC 33.25 explicitly lists this as one of its three key points, which matters for how officers and diversion programs approach at-risk or currently-involved youth — exit is possible, and treating involvement as irreversible undercuts intervention efforts.

---

### Q34 — Recall
**LO:** 33.25 · **Source:** BPOC 33.25

Which of the following is listed among the needs BPOC identifies that gangs meet for juveniles?

- [x] Status, self-worth/esteem, acceptance, and protection
- [ ] Formal education and vocational certification
- [ ] Legal emancipation from parents
- [ ] Health insurance coverage

**Explanation:** BPOC's list of needs gangs can meet for juveniles includes status, self-worth/esteem, acceptance, fun/excitement/risk, credibility/notoriety, protection, recognition of identity, bonding, substitution for boredom, obtaining wealth/materialism, and survival.

---

### Q35 — Recall
**LO:** 33.25 · **Source:** BPOC 33.25

Which of the following is listed among the "underlying societal strains" BPOC identifies as giving rise to gangs?

- [x] High unemployment and an atmosphere of discrimination/abuse/criticism
- [ ] Excessive access to higher education
- [ ] Overly lenient school discipline policies alone
- [ ] Abundant recreational youth programming

**Explanation:** BPOC's list of underlying societal strains includes high unemployment, lack of primary bonding groups, an atmosphere of discrimination/abuse/criticism, a community perceived as uncaring, a youth's underdeveloped self-control/respect for self and others, an environment using intimidation/aggression to solve problems, and the profitability of illegal activity.

### Q2 — Recall
**LO:** 9.1 · **Source:** CCP Art. 1.03

CCP Art. 1.03 ("Objects of This Code") lists six things the Code seeks to do. Which of the following is NOT one of them?

- [ ] To adopt measures for preventing the commission of crime
- [ ] To insure a fair and impartial trial
- [x] To maximize the number of convictions obtained by the state
- [ ] To insure a trial with as little delay as is consistent with the ends of justice

**Explanation:** The six objects: (1) prevent the commission of crime, (2) exclude the offender from all hope of escape, (3) insure a trial with as little delay as is consistent with the ends of justice, (4) bring all evidence tending toward conviction or acquittal to trial, (5) insure a fair and impartial trial, and (6) the certain execution of the sentence when declared. Notably, "maximizing convictions" isn't one of them — the Code is explicitly balanced toward a fair process, not a prosecutorial outcome.

## LO 9.3 — Duties of peace officers

### Q13 — Recall
**LO:** 9.3 · **Source:** CCP Art. 2A.051

Art. 2A.051 lists the general powers and duties of every peace officer. Which of the following is NOT one of the seven listed duties?

- [ ] Preserve the peace within the officer's jurisdiction using all lawful means
- [ ] Execute all lawful process issued to the officer by a magistrate or court
- [x] Personally prosecute all misdemeanor cases arising within the officer's jurisdiction
- [ ] When authorized by law, arrest an offender without a warrant so the offender may be taken before the proper magistrate or court

**Explanation:** The seven duties are: preserve the peace, interfere without a warrant to prevent/suppress crime when authorized, execute lawful process, notify a magistrate of offenses, make warrantless arrests when authorized, take possession of a child under Art. 63.00905(g), and execute certain civil commitment emergency detention orders on request. Prosecuting cases is the job of the attorney representing the state (Art. 2A.101–2A.107), not the peace officer.

## LO 9.7 — Duties relating to protective orders for victims of sexual assault

### Q48 — Recall
**LO:** 9.7 · **Source:** CCP Art. 7B.001

Per Art. 7B.001, who may file an application for a protective order under this subchapter?

- [x] A victim of specified offenses (including sexual assault, indecent assault, stalking, and certain burglary offenses), without regard to the relationship between the applicant and the alleged offender
- [ ] Only a spouse or former spouse of the alleged offender
- [ ] Only the attorney representing the state, never the victim personally
- [ ] Only a victim who has already obtained a criminal conviction against the offender

**Explanation:** Unlike a family-violence protective order, this subchapter's protective order is explicitly available "without regard to the relationship between the applicant and the alleged offender" — it covers strangers as well as family/household members. A parent/guardian may also apply on behalf of a victim under 18 or an adult ward, as may a prosecuting attorney.

### Q49 — Concept
**LO:** 9.7 · **Source:** CCP Art. 7B.002

Per Art. 7B.002, under what standard may a court issue a temporary ex parte protective order, and what procedural step does this skip?

- [x] If the court finds a clear and present danger of the described conduct or other harm to the applicant — the order may issue without further notice to the alleged offender and without a hearing
- [ ] Only after a full adversarial hearing with both parties present
- [ ] Only with the unanimous agreement of a three-judge panel
- [ ] Only after the alleged offender has been formally charged with a crime

**Explanation:** "If the court finds from the information contained in an application for a protective order that there is a clear and present danger of conduct... or other harm to the applicant, the court, without further notice to the alleged offender and without a hearing, may issue a temporary ex parte order."

---

### Q50 — Definition
**LO:** 9.7 · **Source:** CCP Art. 7B.003

Per Art. 7B.003, what finding must a court make at the close of a hearing before issuing a (non-temporary) protective order under this subchapter?

- [x] Whether there are reasonable grounds to believe the applicant is the victim of an offense listed in Art. 7B.001(a)(1) or (2)
- [ ] Proof beyond a reasonable doubt that the offense occurred
- [ ] A unanimous jury verdict on the underlying offense
- [ ] Only that the applicant has filed the correct paperwork, with no substantive finding required

**Explanation:** The standard is "reasonable grounds to believe" — notably lower than the criminal "beyond a reasonable doubt" standard, since this is a civil protective order proceeding. An offender's actual conviction or deferred adjudication for the listed offense automatically satisfies this standard.

### Q51 — Concept
**LO:** 9.7 · **Source:** CCP Art. 7B.004

- [ ] Younger than 10 years of age
- [ ] Younger than 12 years of age

**Explanation:** "A statement that is made by a child younger than 14 years of age who is the victim of an offense under Section 21.02, 21.11, 22.011, 22.012, or 22.021, Penal Code, and that describes the offense committed against the child is admissible as evidence" in the same manner as a child's abuse statement is admissible in a Family Code suit affecting the parent-child relationship.

### Q53 — Recall

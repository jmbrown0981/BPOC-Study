# Chapter 18: Missing and Exploited Persons — Offenses & Classifications Question Bank

*Companion to `18-missing-exploited-persons.md`. Each question presents a fact pattern from the Chapter 18
material (missing-persons and exploitation/trafficking offenses cited in `18_codes.md`) and asks for two
independent answers: which offense (if any) applies, and its punishment classification. Each half is
scored independently (half credit each), same convention as the Ch 8 and Ch 22 offense/classification
banks. One scenario is deliberately a "no offense" fact pattern testing whether an affirmative defense
defeats the case.*


### Q1 — Scenario
**LO:** 18.2 / 18.6 · **Source:** PC 25.03(a)(1), (d)

A father, who lost primary custody in a divorce decree, picks his son up from school on a non-visitation day and refuses to return him, knowing this violates the express terms of the custody order.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Aggravated Kidnapping
- [ ] Enticing a Child
- [x] Interference with Child Custody
- [ ] No offense — parents cannot be charged for taking their own child

**Classification:**
- [ ] Class B Misdemeanor
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [x] State Jail Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony

**Explanation:** Knowingly taking or retaining a child in violation of the express terms of a custody order is Interference with Child Custody under PC 25.03(a)(1), a state jail felony under 25.03(d).

### Q2 — Scenario
**LO:** 18.2 / 18.6 · **Source:** PC 25.03(b), (d)

A noncustodial mother, intending to interfere with the custodial father's lawful custody, repeatedly calls her 15-year-old daughter and persuades her to leave the custodial home and come live with her instead.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Enticing a Child
- [ ] No offense — the daughter went willingly
- [x] Interference with Child Custody
- [ ] Harboring a Runaway Child

**Classification:**
- [x] State Jail Felony
- [ ] Class A Misdemeanor
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony

**Explanation:** PC 25.03(b) specifically covers a noncustodial parent who, with intent to interfere with lawful custody, knowingly entices or persuades a child under 18 to leave the custodial parent — a state jail felony, same as 25.03(a).

### Q3 — Scenario
**LO:** 18.2 · **Source:** PC 25.04(a), (b)

A family friend with no custodial rights, intending to interfere with a mother's lawful custody, persuades her 10-year-old son to sneak out and come to the friend's house instead of school, with no intent to commit any further offense against the child.

**Offense:**
- [x] Enticing a Child
- [ ] Kidnapping
- [ ] Interference with Child Custody
- [ ] Aggravated Kidnapping

**Classification:**
- [ ] Class A Misdemeanor
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [x] Class B Misdemeanor
- [ ] State Jail Felony

**Explanation:** PC 25.04 (Enticing a Child) covers a non-parent who knowingly entices, persuades, or takes a child from the custody of a parent/guardian — a Class B misdemeanor, unless the actor intended to commit a felony against the child, which would elevate it to a third-degree felony. Interference with Child Custody (25.03) is reserved for someone who already had or is a noncustodial parent/violates a court order.

### Q4 — Scenario
**LO:** 18.8 · **Source:** PC 37.081(a), (b)

With intent to deceive, a woman knowingly files a report with police claiming her ex-boyfriend is a missing person, when she knows full well he simply moved out of state and cut off contact with her.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Filing a False Police Report (general)
- [x] False Report Regarding Missing Child or Missing Person
- [ ] Interference with Public Duties
- [ ] No offense — reports cannot be criminally false

**Classification:**
- [x] Class C Misdemeanor
- [ ] Class A Misdemeanor
- [ ] Class B Misdemeanor
- [ ] State Jail Felony

**Explanation:** PC 37.081(a)(1) makes it an offense to knowingly file a false missing-person report with intent to deceive; under (b) it's a Class C misdemeanor.

### Q5 — Scenario

**Classification:**

- [x] Class C Misdemeanor

### Q8 — Scenario
**LO:** 18.14 · **Source:** PC 20A.02(a)(7), (b)

A man recruits a 15-year-old runaway online, then causes her to engage in prostitution in exchange for money that he keeps.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Employment Harmful to Children
- [ ] Compelling Prostitution
- [x] Trafficking of Persons
- [ ] Online Solicitation of a Minor

**Classification:**
- [ ] State Jail Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [x] First Degree Felony

**Explanation:** Trafficking a child and, by any means, causing them to engage in prostitution (PC 43.02) is covered by PC 20A.02(a)(7)(E). Because the trafficked person is a child, the offense is a felony of the first degree under 20A.02(b) — this classification holds regardless of which of the several overlapping 2025 legislative amendments to 20A.02(b) ultimately controls, since a child-victim trafficking offense is elevated to first degree under all of them.

### Q9 — Scenario
**LO:** 18.14 · **Source:** PC 20A.03(a), (e)

Over a two-month period, a trafficker repeatedly forces the same victim into commercial sex acts on multiple separate occasions, using threats of violence each time to maintain compliance.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Continuous Sexual Abuse of a Child
- [x] Continuous Trafficking of Persons
- [ ] Trafficking of Persons
- [ ] Compelling Prostitution

**Classification:**
- [ ] Second Degree Felony
- [x] First Degree Felony
- [ ] State Jail Felony
- [ ] Third Degree Felony

**Explanation:** Engaging in conduct that constitutes trafficking two or more times against the same or different victims during a period of 30 or more days is Continuous Trafficking of Persons under PC 20A.03(a) — a first-degree felony punishable by life or 25-99 years under 20A.03(e).

### Q11 — Scenario
**LO:** 18.2 / 18.14 · **Source:** PC 20.04(a)(5), (d)

A man abducts his ex-girlfriend at knifepoint, telling her he wants to "terrorize" her into taking him back, but releases her unharmed at a well-lit gas station two hours later. At trial, he proves by a preponderance of the evidence that he voluntarily released her in a safe place.

**Offense:**
- [x] Aggravated Kidnapping
- [ ] Kidnapping
- [ ] Unlawful Restraint
- [ ] No offense — she was released unharmed

**Classification:**
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] State Jail Felony
- [ ] First Degree Felony
- [x] Second Degree Felony

**Explanation:** Abducting someone with intent to terrorize them is Aggravated Kidnapping under PC 20.04(a)(5) — normally a first-degree felony, but 20.04(d) lets the defendant reduce it to a second-degree felony at the punishment stage by proving, by a preponderance of the evidence, that he voluntarily released the victim in a safe place.

### Q12 — Scenario
**LO:** 18.2 · **Source:** PC 20.03(a), (c)

A man intentionally abducts a coworker after an argument, holding her in a locked storage room for an hour with no ransom demand, no weapon, and no intent to injure her, before she is found by other employees.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Aggravated Kidnapping
- [ ] Unlawful Restraint
- [ ] Interference with Child Custody
- [x] Kidnapping

**Classification:**
- [x] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] Class A Misdemeanor
- [ ] State Jail Felony
- [ ] First Degree Felony

**Explanation:** Intentionally or knowingly abducting another person, without any of the aggravating intents listed in PC 20.04(a) (ransom, shield/hostage, facilitating a felony, injury, terrorizing, or interfering with a government function) or a deadly weapon, is plain Kidnapping under PC 20.03(a) — a third-degree felony under 20.03(c).

### Q24 — Scenario
**LO:** 18.2 · **Source:** PC 20.03(b)

A grandmother, worried about her grandson's safety, picks him up from his mother's apartment without permission and takes him to her own home for the weekend, intending only to look after him — not to use or threaten deadly force, and not to permanently remove him from his mother's custody.

**Offense:**
- [ ] Kidnapping
- [x] No offense — affirmative defense applies (relative, no deadly force, sole intent to assume lawful control)
- [ ] Interference with Child Custody
- [ ] Aggravated Kidnapping

**Classification:**
- [ ] Third Degree Felony
- [ ] State Jail Felony
- [ ] First Degree Felony
- [x] N/A — no offense committed

**Explanation:** PC 20.03(b) provides an affirmative defense to Kidnapping where the abduction wasn't coupled with intent to use or threaten deadly force, the actor was a relative of the person, and the actor's sole intent was to assume lawful control of the victim — all present here. (This is an affirmative defense the grandmother would have to prove at trial, not an automatic bar to arrest or investigation.)

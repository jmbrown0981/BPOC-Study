How to read this: classifications and fine/jail figures come from the site's copy of the Alcoholic Beverage Code (Texas Codes → Alcoholic Beverage Code, built from resources/ABC) and the Penal Code. Several Alcoholic Beverage Code offenses state a fine/jail range instead of a class (for example, §101.63), and some state no penalty at all, so the §1.05 general penalty applies. Those are tagged Misdemeanor (stated range). Administrative rows are TABC actions against a license or permit, not criminal charges; both can come from the same event (BPOC 16.6). Statute links open the section in Texas Codes.

CATCH-ALL General Penalty — the bottom rung for the whole code ▾

When an Alcoholic Beverage Code section creates a duty or prohibition but doesn't say what breaking it costs, §1.05 supplies the penalty.

CatchallStatuteApplies toPenalty
General PenaltyABC § 1.05Any Alcoholic Beverage Code provision for which a specific penalty is not provided (for example, 104.01 and 61.01). A “specific penalty” is one that might be imposed as a result of a criminal prosecution (1.05(b)).Misdemeanor: fine $100–$1,000, county jail up to 1 year, or both
106.071 Minor-Offense Ladder — how 106.02, 106.025, 106.04, 106.05, and 106.07 escalate ▾

A “minor” is anyone under 21 (§106.01). The offenses below share one punishment section. It counts prior convictions; a prior juvenile adjudication or order of deferred disposition counts as one (§106.071(f)).

No prior convictionClass C misdemeanor8–12 hrs community service · 30-day DL suspension
One priorClass C misdemeanor20–40 hrs community service · 60-day DL suspension
Two or more priors, not a “child”Fine $250–$2,000, jail up to 180 days, or both180-day DL suspension · no deferred
“Child” has the Family Code 51.02 meaning (generally 10 through 16), so the enhanced range in §106.071(c) applies to minors 17 through 20. The driver's license suspension (or denial, if the minor has none) takes effect on the 11th day after conviction (§106.071(h)). Under §106.115, a first conviction also requires an alcohol awareness or substance misuse education program.

Public intoxication by a person under 21 is punished the same way as these offenses (PC 49.02(e)).

DUI by a minor (§106.041) has its own similar ladder: Class C; with two or more priors and not a child, fine $500–$2,000, jail up to 180 days, or both; community service 20–40 hrs (40–60 with a prior).

106 Offenses by Minors (Ch. 106) — punished under 106.071 ▾
OffenseStatuteLevelFine / JailFalls to (if no penalty stated)
Purchase of Alcohol by a Minor ABC § 106.02 Class C misdemeanor Punished under 106.071 (see the minor-offense ladder below). Not an offense when the minor buys under the immediate supervision of a commissioned peace officer enforcing the code. —
Attempt to Purchase Alcohol by a Minor ABC § 106.025 Class C misdemeanor Punished under 106.071. Specific intent to buy, plus an act amounting to more than mere preparation that fails. (The BPOC scenario cites “106.024”; the section is 106.025.) —
Consumption of Alcohol by a Minor ABC § 106.04 Class C misdemeanor Punished under 106.071. Affirmative defense: consumed in the visible presence of the minor's adult parent, guardian, or spouse. Does not apply to a minor who first requested medical help for an alcohol overdose, or who reports a sexual assault. —
Possession of Alcohol by a Minor ABC § 106.05 Class C misdemeanor Punished under 106.071. Exceptions: lawful employment by a licensee or permittee; visible presence of an adult parent, guardian, spouse, or court-ordered custodian; under a peace officer's supervision; lawfully provided under 106.16. —
Misrepresentation of Age by a Minor ABC § 106.07 Class C misdemeanor Punished under 106.071. Falsely stating to be 21 or older, or presenting any document showing 21 or older, to a person selling or serving alcohol. —
Driving or Operating a Watercraft Under the Influence of Alcohol by a Minor (any detectable amount) ABC § 106.041 Class C misdemeanor Class C for a 1st or 2nd offense, plus 20–40 hrs community service (40–60 with a prior). Minor who is not a child with 2+ priors: fine $500–$2,000, jail up to 180 days, or both; no deferred disposition or adjudication. Not a lesser included offense of DWI. The officer may cite instead of taking the minor into custody. —
106 Offenses by Sellers & Adults Involving Minors (Ch. 106) ▾
OffenseStatuteLevelFine / JailFalls to (if no penalty stated)
Sale to Minors ABC § 106.03 Class A misdemeanor Criminal negligence. Per PC 12.21: fine up to $4,000, up to 1 year jail. Defense: the minor showed an apparently valid, government-issued photo ID consistent with their appearance (lost if an electronic ID check showed it invalid). —
Purchase of Alcohol for a Minor; Furnishing Alcohol to a Minor ABC § 106.06 Class A misdemeanor Class A by default. State jail felony if the minor, as a result of drinking it, caused serious bodily injury or death to another. Exception: the minor's own adult parent, guardian, spouse, or court-ordered custodian who is visibly present. On community supervision for a binge-drinking gathering: 20–40 hrs community service, an alcohol awareness program, and a 180-day license suspension. —

Selling Malt Beverages or Possessing Them for Sale Without a License or Permit ABC § 61.01 Misdemeanor (stated range) 61.01 states no penalty, so the general penalty applies: fine $100–$1,000, county jail up to 1 year, or both. ADMIN Administrative Sanctions Against the License or Permit (TABC) ▾
OffenseStatuteLevelFine / JailFalls to (if no penalty stated)
Cancellation or Suspension of a Permit (code or rule violation, conviction, disorderly or lewd establishment, etc.) ABC § 11.61 Administrative After notice and hearing: suspension for up to 60 days, or cancellation. Separate from any criminal case against an individual; double jeopardy does not apply (BPOC 16.6). —
Sanctions Against a Retailer for Sale to a Minor (or permitting a minor to consume or possess on the premises) ABC § 106.13 Administrative 1st: suspension up to 90 days or cancellation. 2nd: up to 6 months or cancellation. 3rd within 36 months: up to 12 months or cancellation. The commission may relax sanctions if the violation could not reasonably have been prevented, the permittee was entrapped, or an employee acted without the permittee's knowledge. —
Actions of Employee Not Attributed to Employer (seller training “safe harbor”) ABC § 106.14 Administrative An employee's sale to a minor or an intoxicated person is not attributed to the employer if the employer requires commission-approved seller training, the employee attended it, and the employer did not encourage the violation. It does not excuse the employee's own criminal conduct. —

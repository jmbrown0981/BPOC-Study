Why this matters: a possible victim (or trafficker) using this language unprompted — scripted answers, deferring to “Daddy” or a “Manager,” referring to a “quota” or “the life” — is a recognized interview/observational cue under BPOC Ch.18.17 and Ch.21.9, not proof on its own. The Department of Homeland Security list this glossary is built from is explicitly not exhaustive — slang shifts by region and over time — and officers shouldn't introduce this terminology first; understand it when a victim uses it, don't lead with it. ROLES Roles & Hierarchy Within a “Stable” 6 terms ▾
Daddy
The term a male trafficker might require his victims to call him.
Manager
Another term used for a trafficker — often easier for a victim to say to others than “pimp” or “daddy.”
Bottom Bitch / Bottom Girl
The person in a stable of victims appointed by the trafficker to manage, recruit, and supervise the others — often a victim herself, not merely an accomplice.
Stable
A group of people under one trafficker's control at the same location.
Family / Folks
The environment a trafficker creates in an attempt to recreate the family structure some youth lack — part of how control is normalized.
Enforcers / Gorilla Pimp
Those who “teach victims a lesson” through beatings, or by showing them the consequences of not following the rules.
CONTROL Control, Recruitment & Branding 5 terms ▾
Grooming / Recruiting / Breaking-in / Training
The process a trafficker uses to manipulate a victim into prostituting for them — commonly includes romantic gestures and promises before the exploitation begins.
Finesse
Someone who is a sweet talker and will say or give almost anything to catch a person's attention or keep them interested.
Branded
When a trafficker tattoos a victim to show ownership — a red-flag indicator BPOC also lists under Ch.18's exploitation signs.
Breaking
Breaking a victim down through control and force until they are subservient and lose the will to fight back.
Turnout
When a trafficker puts someone out to work for the first time.
TRADE The Trade — Transactions & Locations 9 terms ▾
The Life / The Game
Commercial sexual exploitation, or the sex industry, generally.
Quota
The amount a victim must earn for their trafficker each night.
Hit a Stain
To contact someone to have sex for money.
Trappin’
Having sex with many people.
Track / Stroll
A street location used for commercial sex.
Incall
When a buyer comes to a residence or hotel room for sex.
Outcall
When the victim goes to the residence or hotel room of a buyer for sex.
Tricks / Johns / Dates
Buyers of commercial sex.
Twelve (12)
Police officer(s) — useful to recognize if overheard in a conversation or text message during a stop.
FARR Trafficker Roles — the Business Structure (Farr, 2005) 6 roles ▾
Recruiter
Finds and brings victims into the industry, usually by deception but sometimes by force; sells victims to brokers or directly to employers such as brothel or bar owners.
Employment / Travel Agent
Arranges a “legitimate” job and job description, or a “legitimate” trip and alleged purpose — often a front run by someone who genuinely operates an employment or travel agency, also used for the trafficking operation. May arrange the victim's visas, passports, and other papers.
Document Thief / Forger
Arranges and obtains “legitimate” travel documentation — may steal or illegally obtain real documents, or create false ones. This position may be filled by an immigration officer or a lawyer.
Transporter (escort, jockey, or coyote)
Accompanies victims on the trip by plane, train, bus, car, or on foot, sometimes through one or more transit cities/countries, and delivers the victim to a broker or directly to an employer.
Employer (procurer)
Purchases and resells the victim to the customer, provides a place of employment/living, and sets the conditions of work, living arrangements, and demands. Most commonly a bar, club, or brothel owner/manager; a smaller number are street pimps without a business establishment.
Enforcer (guard, roof)
Protects the place of business (and, to a lesser extent, the victim) from other criminal gangs, extortionists, and police; ensures victims comply and don't escape, and that customers pay for services.

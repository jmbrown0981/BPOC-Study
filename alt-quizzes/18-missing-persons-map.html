A missing person case is never out of jurisdiction — every law enforcement agency that receives a report must act on it (CCP 63.009(a), 63.00905(a)). This page maps how a case is classified (type + circumstance), how it moves through the reporting timeline (CCP Ch.63 deadlines), which database or statewide alert applies, and how exploitation and trafficking are separately defined once a person is found to be a victim rather than simply missing.

18.13 Types of Exploitation ▾

Exploitation means the illegal or improper use of a child, elderly individual, or disabled individual — or of their resources — for monetary or personal benefit, profit, or gain (PC 32.53). Instructors note the definition is legally anchored to those three groups but the underlying conduct extends beyond them.

18.14 Types of Human Trafficking ▾

Traffic means to transport, entice, recruit, harbor, provide, or otherwise obtain another person by any means (PC 20A.02). Trafficking is involuntary, involves forced exploitation, and can occur anywhere — including the victim's own residence.

TypeDefinitionCitation / Note

Prostitution vs. trafficking: prostitution can become trafficking, but trafficking must never be treated as prostitution — a sex trafficking victim is a rape victim.
